# Mapping China's Military Suppliers

*A field guide to the PLA's defense-industrial base: the primes, the load-bearing subsidiaries nobody writes about, the military-civil fusion upstarts, and what still comes from abroad.*

*Working draft — August 30, 2026. Companion interactive map: `pla-supplier-map.html` in this folder.*

---

## How to read this map

China's defense industry is not organized like America's. There is no Lockheed that wins competitions against a Boeing. Instead, the system is a set of **state-owned conglomerates with quasi-monopolies over their domains** — one for combat aircraft, one for engines, one for warships, and so on — sitting under two masters: the Central Military Commission's **Equipment Development Department** (the customer, roughly DoD acquisition) and **SASTIND**, the state defense-industry regulator under MIIT. Below each conglomerate sits a lattice of numbered **research institutes and academies** — the actual design houses, many older than the corporations that own them — plus factories and publicly listed subsidiaries that raise capital on Chinese exchanges.

Since roughly 2015, a second system has grown up beside the first: **military-civil fusion (MCF)**, later rebranded into the "new-type whole-of-nation system." Private and commercial firms — drone makers, AI labs, chip designers, commercial space startups, "little giant" component specialists — now feed the PLA directly through open bidding portals and venture-style funds. The most interesting action in Chinese defense-industrial policy is at the seam between these two systems.

Four tiers below. Tier 1 is the ten-ish central conglomerates. Tier 2 is the subsidiary/institute layer where the actual work happens — the most underrated layer for outside analysts. Tier 3 is MCF and commercial. Tier 4 is what China still buys, licenses, or smuggles from abroad.

**One structural update most coverage has missed:** in July 2025 Beijing dissolved **China South Industries Group (CSGC)**, the smaller of the two ordnance conglomerates. Its automotive empire (Changan) was [spun off as an independent central SOE](http://en.sasac.gov.cn/2025/07/31/c_19613.htm), and its defense assets were [folded into NORINCO](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/103361096-changan-automobile-announces-restructuring-progress-csgc-to-be-split-into-independent-central-state-owned-enterprise) — consolidating Chinese ground-systems production under a single prime for the first time since 1999.

## Tier 1 — The primes

### AVIC — Aviation Industry Corporation of China 中国航空工业集团
The monopoly airframer. Roughly 400,000 employees across ~100 subsidiaries; builds essentially every PLA fixed-wing combat aircraft and helicopter. Its Chengdu complex produces the **J-20** (300+ delivered by late 2025, the largest stealth fleet outside the US) and flew the three-engine **J-36** sixth-generation demonstrator in December 2024; its Shenyang complex builds the **J-16**, carrier-borne **J-15/J-35**, and the tailless **J-50**. Xi'an builds the **Y-20** transport/tanker family and **H-6** bombers, and is presumed home of the still-unrevealed **H-20** stealth bomber. AVIC also owns the Wing Loong armed-drone line and the airborne-systems supply chain (ejection seats, avionics, EO targeting). Long on the Entity List and 1260H; sells the J-35A, VT-4s' airborne cousins, and Wing Loongs abroad through CATIC.

### AECC — Aero Engine Corporation of China 中国航空发动机集团
Spun out of AVIC in 2016 by Xi Jinping personally, because engines were the binding constraint on Chinese airpower. It still is the pacing item, but the gap is closing fast: the **WS-10** now powers most Chinese fighters (ending dependence on Russia's AL-31), the **WS-20** high-bypass turbofan has replaced Russian D-30s on new Y-20s, and the **WS-15** — the F119-class engine the J-20 was designed around — is in [low-rate production with full-scale deployment expected 2026–27](https://www.twz.com/chinas-j-20-fighter-with-long-awaited-ws-15-engines-may-have-flown). The **WS-19** for the J-35 is in test at Guizhou. Watch the institute layer: Shenyang's 606 design institute and the Liming plant, Xi'an's production base, and the listed arm AECC Aviation Power.

### CASC — China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation 中国航天科技集团
The space prime and strategic-missile house — think NASA-contractor-base plus half of the ICBM enterprise in one company. Its numbered academies are the real units: **CALT** (1st Academy) builds Long March launchers and strategic missiles; **CAST** (5th Academy) builds satellites from Beidou to military recon constellations; the 6th Academy builds liquid rocket engines; Shanghai's **SAST** (8th Academy) builds launchers, satellites, and the HQ-16 SAM; and the 11th Academy — an aerodynamics institute — moonlights as the maker of the **CH "Rainbow"** export drone family. CASC hardware anchored the September 3, 2025 parade: DF-61 and DF-5C ICBMs, DF-26D, and a widening family of hypersonic glide vehicles.

### CASIC — China Aerospace Science and Industry Corporation 中国航天科工集团
CASC's sibling and the PLA's tactical-missile monopolist. The 2nd Academy does strategic air and missile defense (HQ-9 family, HQ-19, and the new HQ-29 shown at the 2025 parade); the 3rd Academy does cruise and anti-ship missiles (YJ-12, CJ-10/100, and much of the YJ-series hypersonic lineup unveiled in 2025); the 4th Academy does solid-fuel ballistic missiles (DF-21, DF-26) and, through subsidiary **Expace**, the Kuaizhou commercial launcher. If a Taiwan scenario runs on any single company's products, it is CASIC's.

### CETC — China Electronics Technology Group 中国电子科技集团
The military-electronics giant nobody outside the field can name: radars, electronic warfare, C4ISR, secure comms, and the defense-semiconductor base, spread across ~50 numbered research institutes and listed vehicles like Guorui (radar) and Hikvision (which CETC incubated). Absorbed China Putian (2021) and Hualu (2023). CETC is arguably the most important single node in the PLA's "informatized warfare" build-out — see the institute layer below, because "CETC" as such designs nothing; its institutes design everything.

### CSSC — China State Shipbuilding Corporation 中国船舶集团
The largest shipbuilder in human history, re-merged from its two halves in 2019 (with the listed entities consolidated in a ~$16B stock merger completed in early 2025). CSIS estimates Chinese shipbuilding capacity at [roughly 230× that of the United States](https://www.csis.org/analysis/china-outpacing-us-defense-industrial-base), with a single CSSC yard — Jiangnan — exceeding all US yards combined. The commercial order book (over half of global output) subsidizes the naval one: carriers (**Fujian**, commissioned November 5, 2025), Type 055 cruisers, Type 076 drone-carrier LHAs, and every PLAN submarine. The individual yards are profiled in Tier 2.

### NORINCO — China North Industries Group 中国兵器工业集团
The ground-forces prime: tanks (Type 99A and the new ~40-ton **Type 100** family unveiled September 2025), artillery (PCL-181), rocket artillery (PHL-191), precision munitions, ATGMs, small arms, and most of the PLA's ammunition base — plus a major arms-export business (VT-4 tanks, SH-15 howitzers) and, awkwardly for sanctions enforcers, one of China's biggest civilian oil-and-minerals traders. As of the July 2025 restructuring it has absorbed China South Industries' defense assets (light weapons, optics, vehicle-mounted systems), making it the sole ordnance prime.

### CNNC + CAEP — the nuclear complex 中核集团 / 中国工程物理研究院
Two organizations share the warhead enterprise. **CNNC** runs the fuel cycle — enrichment, plutonium production (including the new fast reactors at Xiapu that worry US planners), naval reactor fuel — behind a large civilian nuclear-power business. **CAEP** in Mianyang, a direct central-government institute outside any conglomerate, is China's Los Alamos: warhead design, pit production, simulation (its computational-mathematics institute in Beijing), and laser-fusion facilities. CAEP has been on the US Entity List since 1997 — the list's charter member — and still turns up in investigations acquiring US-origin chips through intermediaries. Relevant given DoD's estimate that China's stockpile passed 600 warheads and is headed toward ~1,000 by 2030.

### CEC — China Electronics Corporation 中国电子信息产业集团
The "other" electronics SOE — IT systems, cybersecurity, and the party-state's trusted-computing stack. Matters to this map mainly through **Phytium** (Entity-Listed CPU designer tied by US investigators to PLA supercomputing and hypersonic modeling) and China Greatwall's secure computers. A long-rumored CETC-CEC merger has not happened.

*Adjacent but not primes: **COMAC** (civil airliners; its C919 still flies on Western engines — a useful reminder of where dependencies bind hardest), and the new **China Changan Automobile Group**, the demilitarized remnant of CSGC.*

## Tier 2 — The load-bearing layer: subsidiaries and institutes

This is the layer Western coverage skips, and where an analyst's marginal hour is best spent. Two to three sentences each.

**Air**

- **Chengdu Aircraft Industry Group (CAC / AVIC 611-132)** — J-10, J-20, J-36, and the JF-17 with Pakistan. The 611 Institute is the design brain; its alumni network seeds half the drone startups in Sichuan.
- **Shenyang Aircraft Corporation (SAC / AVIC 601-112)** — Flanker derivatives (J-11/15/16), the J-35 in both carrier and land-based/export variants, and the J-50. Historically the "second" design house; the J-35 export win in Pakistan and the J-50 have revived it.
- **Xi'an Aircraft (XAC)** — Y-20 (now the platform for the KJ-3000 AEW&C and YY-20 tanker), H-6 bombers, and presumptively the H-20. Also builds wings for COMAC: dual-use in one factory.
- **Hongdu (Nanchang)** — L-15 trainers and the GJ-11 Sharp Sword stealth UCAV, the PLA's loyal-wingman centerpiece paraded in 2025.
- **Harbin & Changhe (AVIC helicopters)** — Z-20 (Black Hawk-class, Harbin) and Z-10 attack helicopters (Changhe); the Z-20 is quietly one of AVIC's best-executed programs.
- **AVIC 607 / Leihua (Wuxi)** — the airborne fire-control-radar institute, CETC's in-house competitor for fighter AESAs.

**Engines**

- **AECC Shenyang (606 Institute + Liming plant)** — design home of the WS-10 and WS-15; Liming is the volume fighter-engine plant. The single most consequential industrial site for Chinese airpower.
- **AECC Guizhou Liyang** — mid-thrust engines: WS-13 and the WS-19 for the J-35.
- **AECC Aviation Power Co. (Xi'an, listed)** — the serial-production and finance vehicle; its disclosures are one of the few windows into engine output rates.

**Space & missiles**

- **CALT (CASC 1st Academy)** — Long March 5/7/10 and strategic missiles; also parent of commercial-ish spinoff China Rocket.
- **CAST (CASC 5th Academy)** — satellite monopolist: Beidou PNT, Yaogan recon constellations, comms relays. The PLA's kill chain runs through hardware CAST built.
- **CAAA (CASC 11th Academy)** — the aerodynamics institute behind the CH/Rainbow drones and much of China's hypersonic glide-vehicle wind-tunnel work. A great example of an "institute" out-competing corporations.
- **CASIC 2nd / 3rd / 4th Academies** — air defense, cruise/anti-ship, and solid ballistic missiles respectively (see CASIC above); each is a de facto multi-billion-dollar prime hidden inside a conglomerate.

**Electronics (CETC's institutes)**

- **CETC 14th Institute (Nanjing)** — "the cradle of Chinese radar": shipborne Type 346 AESAs on the Type 055, fighter AESAs including the KLJ-7A, counter-stealth arrays, and now in-house AI chips via its Huachuang Micro arm. If you learn one institute number, learn this one.
- **CETC 38th Institute (Hefei)** — the other big radar house: large early-warning and counter-stealth arrays (JY-27A lineage), airborne surveillance radars, and a growing chip-design arm.
- **CETC 29th Institute (Chengdu)** — airborne electronic warfare; the J-16D's suite traces here.
- **CETC 13th (Shijiazhuang) & 55th (Nanjing) Institutes** — GaAs/GaN RF semiconductors: the transmit/receive modules inside every Chinese AESA. China's radar leap in the 2010s was really a compound-semiconductor leap executed by these two.
- **CETC 54th Institute (Shijiazhuang)** — military satcom, ground stations, and datalinks; the connective tissue of "systems destruction warfare."

**Naval (CSSC yards and institutes)**

- **Jiangnan Shipyard (Changxing Island, Shanghai)** — the Fujian carrier, Type 055s, Type 052Ds; more capacity than the entire US shipbuilding base, cross-subsidized by boxship orders.
- **Dalian Shipbuilding** — the Shandong carrier, Type 055/052D serial production; the northern anchor of surface-combatant output.
- **Hudong-Zhonghua (Shanghai)** — amphibs: Type 075 and the drone-carrying Type 076 Sichuan, plus Type 054B frigates — and the world's premier LNG-carrier yard, the cleanest illustration of CSIS's dual-use shipyard findings.
- **Bohai Shipbuilding (Huludao)** — every PLAN nuclear submarine, now expanding halls for Type 095/096 production. Possibly the most strategically important single factory in China.
- **Wuchang Shipbuilding (Wuhan)** — conventional submarines (Yuan-class and the Pakistan Hangor exports) and much of the unmanned/experimental fleet.
- **701 & 719 Institutes** — surface-combatant and nuclear-submarine design bureaus respectively; 719 is the sub-force's brain trust.
- **703 Institute (Harbin)** — marine gas turbines: the QC-280s that power the Type 055. China's naval expansion rode on this institute solving what Ukraine's Zorya-Mashproekt once supplied.

**Ground & guidance**

- **Inner Mongolia First Machinery (Baotou)** — NORINCO's tank plant: Type 99A, Type 100, VT-4 exports.
- **North Navigation Control Technology (listed)** — NORINCO's guidance house: laser/satnav guidance kits and gyros that turn dumb rockets into the PHL-191's precision rounds.

**Materials & process specialists (the truly unheralded)**

- **Western Superconducting Technologies (Xi'an)** — aerospace titanium alloys and superconducting wire; qualified into engines, airframes, and submarine programs. A STAR-market "little giant" that shows up in nearly every advanced platform's bill of materials.
- **Gaona Aero Material (Beijing)** — turbine-disk and blade superalloys under the CISRI steel-research empire; the WS-15's production ramp is partly a Gaona story.
- **Guangwei Composites (Weihai)** — for years the only military-qualified carbon-fiber maker; the J-20 and CH drones fly on its tows. Now joined by Zhongfu Shenying and others as China ended Toray dependence.
- **Fushun Special Steel** — bearing, gear, and high-temperature steels for engines and airframes; chronically troubled, persistently indispensable.
- **Bright Laser Technologies (Xi'an)** — metal additive manufacturing for airframe and engine parts; printing is quietly becoming a signature Chinese aero-manufacturing shortcut.

## Tier 3 — Military-civil fusion and the commercial layer

**The AI-compute stack.** The clearest MCF success story is the Huawei-centered chip ecosystem: **HiSilicon**-designed Ascend accelerators, fabbed on **SMIC's** N+2 7nm process at [~40% yields](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20250225PD224/huawei-ascend-ai-chip-yield-rate.html), with **CXMT** ramping domestic HBM as the binding constraint. Huawei is targeting [~600,000 Ascend 910Cs and up to 1.6M total Ascend dies in 2026](https://techblog.comsoc.org/2025/10/02/huawei-to-double-output-of-ascend-ai-chips-in-2026-openai-orders-hbm-chips-from-sk-hynix-samsung-for-stargate-uae-project/) — not parity with Nvidia, but enough to run a wartime targeting stack. Around it: **Cambricon** (Entity-Listed AI accelerators, now a domestic-substitution darling), **Loongson** and **Phytium** (CPUs; Phytium's PLA supercomputing ties got it listed in 2021), **Jingjia Micro** (military GPUs), and **Fudan Microelectronics** (secure FPGAs — Entity-Listed September 2025 with its affiliates for military modernization and circumvention).

**Drones.** **DJI** remains the world's de facto small-drone arsenal — ubiquitous on both sides in Ukraine, Entity-Listed, and still on 1260H after its court challenge failed — with **Autel** close behind. The defense-native tier includes **Sichuan Tengden** (TB-001 twin-boom strike drone, a regular around Taiwan; founded out of AVIC's Chengdu orbit), CASC's Rainbow and AVIC's Wing Loong export lines, and the new **Jiutian SS-UAV** jet "drone mothership" — a ~16-ton swarm carrier that [began flight testing in 2025–26](https://www.twz.com/air/chinas-high-flying-swarm-mothership-drone-has-flown). Below the airframes sits the component chokehold: firms like Nanchang's **T-Motor** dominate global FPV motors and ESCs, giving China leverage over everyone's drone supply chain, including Ukraine's and ours.

**Commercial space.** The sector crossed a threshold on August 18, 2026, when **LandSpace's** stainless-steel, methane-fueled Zhuque-3 [became the first Chinese commercial rocket to land an orbital-class booster](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/08/19/landspace-becomes-first-commercial-chinese-company-to-land-an-orbital-class-booster/) (it later toppled — Falcon 9 circa 2015 vibes). **Space Pioneer** (Tianlong-3), **Galactic Energy**, **CAS Space**, and **iSpace** fill out the launch tier, while **SpaceSail's Qianfan** megaconstellation [passed 200 satellites this summer](https://spacenews.com/qianfan-constellation-deployment-hits-200-satellites-with-long-march-8-and-6a-launches/) en route to a planned ~14,000 — PLA writings are explicit about wanting a Starlink equivalent. The cautionary tale is **Chang Guang Satellite** (Jilin-1, the world's largest sub-meter imaging constellation): [sanctioned in 2023 for imaging for Wagner](https://www.defenseone.com/business/2025/05/closer-look-chinese-space-company-accused-helping-houthis/405153/), accused by State in 2025 of feeding targeting imagery to the Houthis, and reported by Hudson to have supplied Iran — commercial remote sensing as deniable fires support. **Spacety** (SAR smallsats) drew SDN designation on the same Wagner file.

**AI, sensing, and the rest.** **iFlytek** (speech/AI), the surveillance quartet (SenseTime, Megvii, CloudWalk, Yitu), and **4Paradigm** are all Entity-Listed and all inside the PLA's procurement universe; open reporting has PLA units experimenting with **DeepSeek** models on Ascend hardware. Lidar makers **Hesai** and **RoboSense** and surveillance giants **Hikvision/Dahua** round out the sensing layer. The June 2026 1260H update — [now 188 entities](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/department-of-war-updates-section-1260h) — pulled in **Alibaba, Baidu, BYD, and WuXi AppTec**, signaling that Washington now reads China's cloud, autonomy, battery, and biotech champions as part of the military supply base. Maritime autonomy has its own cluster (**Yunzhou Tech's** USV swarms; the AJX-002 XLUUV shown at the 2025 parade came from the state side).

## Tier 4 — What still comes from abroad

The dependency map has inverted since 2015: Russia went from supplier to customer, and most platform-level imports are gone. What remains flows through narrower, higher-value chokepoints:

- **Advanced AI compute.** Three parallel channels: legal sales under the shifting US license regime ([H200s cleared case-by-case in May 2026](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/department-commerce-revises-license-review-policy-semiconductors-exported-china) for ~10 firms with 75k-unit caps, while Blackwell-class remains barred and Beijing itself intermittently blocks purchases); pre-ban H20 stockpiles; and industrial-scale smuggling — DOJ [broke up a $160M Nvidia-diversion ring in December 2025](https://www.thewirechina.com/2026/03/01/chasing-the-chip-smugglers-nvidia-ai-chips-china/), and Taiwan [indicted nine people in August 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/johnwerner/2026/08/25/taiwan-indicts-conspirators-in-illegal-nvidia-shipments/) for diverting restricted chips.
- **Semiconductor tools and memory.** SMIC's 7nm (and reported 5nm push) runs on an installed base of ASML DUV immersion tools and US/Japanese equipment bought before the 2022–24 controls, serviced in a gray zone; domestic toolmakers (NAURA, AMEC, SiCarrier) are climbing but haven't closed the litho gap. Pre-control Samsung/SK Hynix HBM stockpiles bridged Huawei until CXMT's ramp.
- **EDA software.** The May 2025 Synopsys/Cadence/Siemens cutoff — reversed within weeks as part of the rare-earths truce — demonstrated both the leverage and how quickly Washington will trade it.
- **Precision machine tools.** High-end five-axis machining centers from Japan, Germany, and Switzerland still enter through third-country resellers and front companies; domestic champion Kede CNC is qualifying into aerospace lines but hasn't displaced imports at the top end.
- **Legacy Western components.** C4ADS- and RUSI-style teardowns keep finding US/European MCUs, FPGAs, and RF parts in Chinese and Chinese-supplied munitions, moved through Hong Kong and Shenzhen distributor networks — the same channels that supply Russia.
- **Residual Russian items.** Mostly gone. AL-31 and D-30 engines are being designed out; what's left is niche (some helicopter engines, legacy fleet support).

The flip side: China now runs the export-control playbook in reverse — gallium, germanium, antimony, and the April/October 2025 rare-earth-magnet regime — using its position in *our* supply chains as negotiating leverage over the controls above.

## The unheralded ten

If the primes are priced in, these are the names worth an analyst's attention — each small enough to miss, load-bearing enough to matter:

1. **CETC 14th Institute** — every important Chinese radar traces here; watch its chip arm.
2. **CETC 55th & 13th Institutes** — the GaN/GaAs foundries behind the AESA revolution.
3. **AECC Shenyang (606/Liming)** — the WS-15 ramp is the single best indicator of Chinese airpower maturity.
4. **Bohai Shipbuilding, Huludao** — the entire sea-based deterrent comes out of one yard.
5. **703 Institute (Harbin)** — marine gas turbines; no QC-280, no Type 055 fleet.
6. **CAAA (CASC 11th Academy)** — Rainbow drones plus the hypersonics wind-tunnel base.
7. **Western Superconducting (Xi'an)** — the titanium in nearly everything that flies or dives.
8. **Guangwei Composites (Weihai)** — carbon fiber independence, achieved.
9. **North Navigation (NORINCO)** — the guidance kits behind massed precision fires.
10. **Chang Guang Satellite (Changchun)** — commercial imagery as an exportable kill chain, already battle-tested via Wagner, the Houthis, and Iran.

Honorable mentions: T-Motor (FPV components), Bright Laser (aero additive manufacturing), Fudan Microelectronics (secure FPGAs), Expace/Kuaizhou (CASIC's launch arm), Yunzhou (USVs).
