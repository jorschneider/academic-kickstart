# Indicators & Warnings: What Would Prove This Atlas Wrong

*Part of the [China Defense-Industrial Atlas](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/ecc69e75-bcc3-46cf-a303-3b95f8ac9b4c). Compiled from open sources through August 2026.*

An atlas that cannot be wrong is not analysis — it is decoration. The scorecard says where China stands. The trajectory arrows say which way it is moving. The [confidence grades](./methodology-and-confidence.md) say how much to trust each placement. This page supplies the missing fourth thing, and the one that makes the other three falsifiable: **the specific, observable events that would force a rating to change.**

These are tripwires, not predictions. Each one names a claim the atlas makes, the concrete thing you could watch to test it, where that evidence would surface, and what it would mean if it tripped. Written this way, the atlas commits itself in advance — which is the difference between an analytic product and a set of opinions that can absorb any news.

Three kinds of tripwire run through the list:

- **↑ Strengthen** — evidence that China's position is better or moving faster than rated. Most of the list.
- **↓ Constrain** — evidence that a Chinese advantage is eroding, or that a structural brake is biting.
- **⚡ Break** — evidence that would not adjust a rating but invalidate the model behind it.

---

## The asymmetry is itself a finding

Compile the tripwires honestly and a lopsidedness appears immediately: three in five point toward China being *stronger* than rated, not weaker. That is not a thumb on the scale. It reflects something real about the evidence environment.

Chinese capability tends to be revealed **late and in one direction**. Programs surface when they are already working — the J-36 flew before anyone outside knew its configuration; the Type 076 appeared in a yard before its drone-carrier role was understood; two operational XLUUV classes rolled down a parade route drawn from inventory rather than prototypes. Open-source analysis of an opaque system is therefore biased toward *understatement*, and the corrections, when they come, mostly correct upward.

The constraining indicators are real and worth watching — allied magnet capacity, a commercial-shipbuilding downturn, fiscal deceleration — but they are slower, more diffuse, and easier to over-read from a Western vantage point where they are heavily covered. An analyst using this atlas should hold that asymmetry in mind: **the surprises have historically run one way.**

## Near term — the next twelve months

These are the tripwires with dated, checkable resolution points.

**⚡ EUV at commercial throughput.** *Tests:* the atlas's deepest and most consequential claim — that SMIC runs 7nm on pre-sanction DUV with no domestic EUV (rated Dependent, confidence A). *Watch:* the Shenzhen laser-induced-discharge-plasma source reportedly certified in December 2025 moving from ~100–150W toward the ~250W needed for production throughput — and, the real test, wafers actually exposed at commercial rates. Reporting on that prototype ranged from credible specialist analysis to content-farm noise, so the threshold to watch is the power figure and a verified wafer, *not* the announcement. *Where:* The Diplomat's tracking of the program, TechInsights, SEMI, Huawei/SiCarrier patent filings. *If it trips:* the single largest model-break available. It would collapse the deepest dependency in the atlas and invalidate the theory of the case behind the entire export-control regime.

**↑ WS-15 as standard fit.** *Tests:* "the WS-15 is in low-rate production" (Aero-Engines, Catching up, B). *Watch:* J-20 serial airframes appearing with the WS-15 as the standard fit rather than the interim WS-10. *Where:* airframe serials and spotting, plus AECC Aviation Power's exchange disclosures on output and backlog — one of the few public windows into Chinese engine rates. *If it trips:* the historic weak point is closed at the fighter tier; Aero-Engines moves toward Competitive.

**↓ First non-Chinese heavy-rare-earth chain.** *Tests:* the durability of the magnet lever — the atlas's biggest single piece of leverage. *Watch:* MP Materials commissioning its Mountain Pass heavy-separation circuit (200 t/yr Dy/Tb nameplate) and its Independence line actually *shipping* magnets in H2 2026. Count tonnes shipped, not capacity announced. *Where:* MP quarterly results, DoD offtake disclosures, Lynas quarterlies. *If it trips:* the first real non-Chinese heavy-RE-plus-magnet chain exists. Note the scale honestly: MP's planned 10,000 t/yr of magnets is about a quarter of what JL MAG alone already runs. This is erosion, not escape.

**↑ Reusable booster reflight.** *Tests:* "behind in reusable heavy-lift" (Space, Competitive, B). *Watch:* LandSpace re-flying a *recovered* Zhuque-3 booster — the August 2026 landing succeeded and then toppled. *Where:* launch reporting, SpaceNews. *If it trips:* reuse crosses from demonstration to operation, and the megaconstellation cadence becomes credible on cost grounds.

**↑ Counter-UAS combat verification.** *Tests:* the atlas's only C-graded rating — lasers and high-power microwave "openly fielded," with reliability unproven. *Watch:* independent third-party confirmation of a Chinese laser or HPM system achieving shoot-downs in a real engagement, most plausibly through an export customer. *Where:* conflict reporting, export-customer statements, OSINT imagery. *If it trips:* the highest-value evidentiary unlock in the atlas — it would move counter-UAS from inferential to documented in a single stroke.

**↑ The annual warhead number.** *Tests:* "600+ warheads, heading toward ~1,000 by 2030" (Nuclear, Catching up, B, rising fast). *Watch:* the stockpile estimate in DoD's China Military Power Report, published each December, and whether the 2030 trajectory holds, accelerates, or is revised down. *Where:* DoD CMPR; FAS Nuclear Notebook. *If it trips:* a figure materially above trend moves Nuclear toward Competitive earlier than modeled.

**↑ Type 096 hull in the water.** *Tests:* Bohai/Huludao as the sole nuclear-submarine yard, and undersea as the PLAN's weak tier. *Watch:* commercial imagery showing a launched ~20,000-ton Type 096 SSBN hull. *Where:* Planet and Maxar imagery as analyzed by FAS, CSIS, and H.I. Sutton. *If it trips:* sea-based deterrent modernization is on or ahead of schedule; both Nuclear and Undersea move.

## Mid term — one to three years

**↑ A verified 5nm SMIC process.** *Watch:* a TechInsights teardown confirming a genuine 5nm-class SMIC node in a shipping product at commercial yield — physical evidence, not a corporate claim. *Where:* TechInsights, Huawei product launches. *If it trips:* multipatterning DUV has stretched further than the control regime assumed, and Advanced Semiconductors moves off Dependent even without EUV.

**↑ CXMT high-bandwidth memory at volume.** *Tests:* "CXMT is the binding constraint on the Ascend ramp" — currently the chokepoint inside the chokepoint. *Watch:* HBM3-class stacks shipping in volumes sufficient to feed more than a million Ascend dies. *Where:* TrendForce, SemiAnalysis, CXMT capex, Korean-supplier commentary. *If it trips:* the one constraint on China's AI-compute build-out is removed; Military AI Compute moves up.

**↑ CJ-1000A certification.** *Tests:* whether the hot-section and superalloy gap is genuinely closed. *Watch:* CAAC certification of the CJ-1000A commercial turbofan. Verification flights on the C919 began in March 2025; analysts project certification around 2027–28 with volume production near 2030, and the 14th Five-Year Plan names it a priority. *Where:* CAAC notices, AECC/ACAE disclosures, COMAC deliveries. *If it trips:* commercial certification is a harder and far more transparent bar than any military program — the clearest public proof available that the metallurgy problem is solved.

**↑ Harmonic-reducer displacement.** *Tests:* "top-tier reducers (Japan)" as one of the last named robot dependencies (Robots, Dominant, B). *Watch:* Leaderdrive and Green Harmonic displacing Harmonic Drive Systems in genuinely high-precision applications — visible in HDS's own share commentary and in teardowns of Chinese humanoid units. *Where:* Interact Analysis, HDS earnings, Unitree/AgiBot BOM teardowns. *If it trips:* the inversion deepens; one of the few remaining Western holds on the robot stack goes.

**↑ A quieting assessment.** *Tests:* "quieting and detection are the PLAN's acknowledged weak spot" — the single most consequential naval variable, because undersea is the principal US edge in the [Taiwan lens](./taiwan-contingency-lens.md). *Watch:* an ONI assessment or congressional testimony stating that Type 095 acoustic performance has materially closed the gap. *Where:* ONI, USNI reporting, congressional testimony. *If it trips:* the US operational advantage that the contingency analysis leans on hardest starts to erode.

**↓ Drone-component decoupling.** *Tests:* "80–90% of the world's FPV motors" (Components, Dominant — the leverage, not the dependency). *Watch:* Ukrainian and Western FPV production sourcing motors and ESCs outside China at scale. Watch the *component* layer, not airframe reshoring, which is the easier and less meaningful half. *Where:* procurement disclosures, teardowns, customs data. *If it trips:* the most durable lever in the atlas starts to loosen.

**↑ Energetics surge becomes visible.** *Tests:* magazine depth — the decisive variable the Taiwan lens flags and open source cannot currently measure. *Watch:* Chinese propellant, energetics or nitrocellulose exports at scale, or new construction at 204-Institute-linked plants visible in imagery. *Where:* customs data, sanctions actions, commercial imagery. *If it trips:* it would put the first real number on wartime endurance — see the [ground-systems essay](./ground-systems.md).

**↑ Megaconstellation cadence.** *Watch:* Guowang and Qianfan sustaining a combined deployment rate above roughly 40 satellites a month against the ~14,000 planned. *Where:* Jonathan McDowell's catalog, SpaceNews. *If it trips:* a PLA communications and ISR layer resilient enough to survive counterspace attrition.

**↓ Heavy-rare-earth independence.** *Tests:* the true crossover clock on the magnet lever. *Watch:* dysprosium and terbium separation outside China at industrially meaningful tonnage. China holds roughly 91% of heavy-RE supply, and analysts see no Western independence before 2028; Lynas produced eight tons of Dy/Tb in a recent quarter, which is the right order of magnitude to track. *Where:* Lynas quarterlies, MP Materials, USGS. *If it trips:* the hardest link in the chain breaks, and the [leverage ledger](./chokepoint-leverage-ledger.md) needs repricing.

**↑ A fourth carrier, nuclear-powered.** *Watch:* module assembly for the Type 004 and whether it carries nuclear propulsion with full integrated electric power. *Where:* satellite imagery of Dalian and Jiangnan, CSIS ChinaPower. *If it trips:* the 712 Institute's electric-propulsion work has matured, and blue-water reach follows.

**↓ Commercial shipbuilding downturn.** *Tests:* the mechanism behind the ~230× capacity finding — that a vast commercial order book cross-subsidizes naval construction. *Watch:* a sustained collapse in Chinese commercial ship orders, or effective deterrence from USTR port-fee measures shrinking the dual-use yard base. *Where:* Clarksons order-book data, CSIS ChinaPower, USTR Section 301 implementation. *If it trips:* the capacity advantage is revealed as partly a commercial-market artifact, and the naval surge gets materially more expensive.

## Long horizon — three to five years and structural

**⚡ Steady-state microbunching EUV.** *Watch:* the Xiong'an SSMB facility — construction began in early 2025, with theoretical output above 1kW — producing usable EUV light. *Where:* Tsinghua publications, facility imagery. *If it trips:* a fundamentally different, non-ASML path to EUV. Slower than the LDP route, but blocked by no export control anyone currently holds. This is the long-horizon model break.

**⚡ Combat performance data.** *Tests:* every rating in the atlas, none of which rests on combat evidence. *Watch:* Chinese-built systems used in a real shooting war by an export customer, generating third-party performance data. *Where:* conflict reporting, allied assessments. *If it trips:* it would resolve more B- and C-grade uncertainty than any other single event — this is the atlas's largest structural blind spot, named as such in the [methodology page](./methodology-and-confidence.md).

**⚡ Resumed nuclear testing.** *Watch:* seismic or imagery evidence of activity at Lop Nur consistent with a supercritical test. *Where:* CTBTO and academic seismic monitoring, commercial imagery, FAS analysis. *If it trips:* warhead-design ambitions beyond the modeled expansion, and the arms-control frame the atlas assumes breaks.

**↑ Domestic EDA at the leading edge.** *Tests:* "high-end EDA software" as a named dependency across Electronics and Compute. *Watch:* Empyrean, Primarius or Semitronix supporting a complete sub-7nm digital design flow used in a shipping chip. *Where:* EDA vendor disclosures, SMIC and Huawei design-flow reporting. *If it trips:* the May 2025 Synopsys/Cadence/Siemens cutoff demonstrated the leverage; domestic replacement at the leading edge retires it permanently.

**↑ Adaptive-cycle propulsion.** *Watch:* evidence of a variable- or adaptive-cycle engine in test, particularly tied to the J-36. *Where:* AECC disclosures, J-36 flight-test observation. *If it trips:* a different and more serious claim than WS-15 maturity — China competing at the propulsion frontier rather than catching up to it.

**↓ Fiscal deceleration.** *Tests:* the "wartime footing" premise underneath most of the scorecard. *Watch:* official defense-budget growth falling below nominal GDP growth for consecutive years amid local-government fiscal stress. *Where:* NPC budget releases each March, SIPRI. *If it trips:* procurement tempo is the engine behind nearly every trajectory arrow; sustained deceleration slows all of them at once.

**↓ Sanctions-regime inflection.** *Tests:* "military-civil fusion makes 'military supplier' an unbounded category." *Watch:* whether US listings keep widening — 188 entities on the 1260H list as of June 2026 — or Washington shifts to a narrower, enforcement-led approach. *Where:* DoD 1260H updates, BIS actions. *If it trips:* it would tell you whether the sanctions treadmill is a strategy or a taxonomy problem.

## The five that matter most

If you can only watch five things, watch these. They are ranked by how much of the atlas each one would move.

1. **EUV at commercial throughput** *(⚡ break)* — collapses the deepest dependency and the export-control theory of the case with it.
2. **Combat performance data** *(⚡ break)* — resolves the atlas's largest structural blind spot; nothing else unlocks as much B- and C-grade uncertainty.
3. **A PLAN quieting assessment** *(↑ strengthen)* — erodes the principal US operational edge in the only contingency that matters most.
4. **Heavy-rare-earth production outside China** *(↓ constrain)* — the real crossover clock on China's biggest lever, and the hardest link to replace.
5. **Energetics and magazine depth becoming visible** *(↑ strengthen)* — puts a number on the variable that decides a long war and that nobody can currently measure.

Note the composition: two of the five are model-breaks rather than tier adjustments, and three of the five concern things the atlas explicitly admits it cannot see. That is the honest shape of the problem. The most valuable indicators are not the ones that would nudge a rating — they are the ones that would tell us the map was drawn wrong.
