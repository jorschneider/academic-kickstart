China Defense-Industrial Atlas

The world's largest defense-industrial base — and the most uneven. China builds ships, drones and robots at a scale no rival can match and runs its arsenal on a wartime footing while America's stays on a peacetime one. It has turned its grip on drones, robots and rare-earth magnets into leverage the West now feels. Yet in the deepest process technologies — lithography, jet engines, EDA — it still bends the knee. This atlas maps who actually builds for the PLA, domain by domain.

03

Four throughlines

What the whole atlas adds up to.

Two footings

China's defense-industrial base operates on a wartime footing — acquiring key systems five to six times faster than the US and holding shipbuilding capacity roughly 230× larger (CSIS) — while the American base stays on a peacetime one. Scale, not sophistication, is the sharpest asymmetry.

Where it still bends the knee

The deepest process technologies — EUV lithography, electronic-design-automation software, jet-engine hot-section materials, top-tier harmonic reducers, high-bandwidth memory — are where China remains dependent. The gaps are narrowing, but they are the real chokepoints, and they are few.

The inversion

In drones, robots and rare-earth magnets the dependency runs the other way — the West depends on China. Beijing has begun to wield the export-control weapon it once only received, metering magnet licenses buyer-by-buyer. For once, China is upstream.

The sanctions treadmill

Military-civil fusion makes "military supplier" an unbounded category. The Pentagon's 1260H list keeps widening — Alibaba, BYD, Unitree — because when the commercial product and the military one ship from the same factory, the dual-use surface is the entire tech sector.

China wins where the game is mass, and lags where the game is the machine that makes the machine.

Methodology & caveats

Compiled entirely from open sources — DoD's Military and Security Developments Involving the PRC (Dec 2025) and the Section 1260H list (June 2026), BIS Entity List actions, CSIS ChinaPower, RUSI, RAND, FDD, Kharon, IISS Military Balance 2026, SemiAnalysis, and trade-press reporting cited in the companion essays. The scorecard tiers are analytical judgments synthesized across those sources, not quantitative measurements; reasonable analysts will place some domains a tier up or down. Corporate structures, shipment figures and sanctions status are a 2026 snapshot of a fast-moving picture. This is a living document, updated as the picture moves.
