# China's Electronics, Radar & Electronic Warfare

*The domain that decides whether every other system can see and shoot — and the one where a single conglomerate, CETC, sits closest to the world's frontier. The radar leap, the gallium-nitride foundation beneath it, and the reality gap in the marketing.*

## The invisible domain that ties the atlas together

Radar, electronic warfare, secure communications, and C4ISR are the connective tissue of "informatized warfare" — the sensors and datalinks that let a missile find a carrier, a fighter cue a wingman, or a swarm coordinate. It is also the domain where China's national champion, **CETC (China Electronics Technology Group)**, sits closest to the global frontier. The atlas rates it **Competitive** — world-class in parts, still dependent in a few — and the story is really the story of one conglomerate's ~50 numbered institutes.

The underappreciated fact is that China's radar leap of the last decade was, underneath, a **compound-semiconductor leap** — a shift to gallium-nitride transmit/receive modules — executed by CETC institutes most people have never heard of. And in a twist that ties back to the atlas's reverse-leverage theme, China also controls the **gallium supply** those modules depend on.

## The radar portfolio

- **Fighter AESAs — now combat-credentialed.** CETC's **14th Institute (Nanjing)** builds the fire-control AESAs for Chinese fighters, and the exported **KLJ-7A** got a real-world résumé in May 2025: mounted on Pakistan's J-10CE, it was [credited with a central role in downing Indian aircraft](https://defencesecurityasia.com/en/china-klj-7a-radar-j-10ce-rafale-india-pakistan-air-war/) in the India–Pakistan air clash — the first high-visibility combat test of a Chinese AESA against Western-supplied jets, and a marketing coup regardless of the contested details.
- **Shipborne AESAs.** The 14th Institute's **Type 346** family arms the Type 055 cruiser and the fleet's air-defense combatants — the naval radar backbone (see the [naval map](./naval-undersea-suppliers.md)).
- **Counter-stealth early warning — the signature bet.** CETC's **38th Institute (Hefei)** anchors the anti-stealth push: the **JY-27V** (unveiled May 2025) is a large, rapidly deployable low-frequency AESA that [claims to detect "extremely stealthy" targets and cue precision strikes](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/military/article/3311054/china-aims-new-jy-27v-radar-stealthy-targets-such-americas-fifth-gen-fighters), improving on the JY-27A with a bigger antenna, lower band, larger power aperture, and smarter signal processing; the **SLC-7** adds a multifunction, anti-jam, multi-target set that tracks everything from stealth aircraft to artillery shells. Betting on low-band + processing to erode US stealth is a coherent, distinctive strategy.

## The gallium-nitride foundation — and the gallium lever

- **The T/R modules.** A modern AESA is thousands of tiny transmit/receive modules, and **GaN** delivers ~5–10× the RF power density of the older gallium-arsenide devices. China's **13th and 55th Institutes (Shijiazhuang / Nanjing)** are the compound-semiconductor foundries behind those modules — the genuinely unheralded, load-bearing nodes that made the radar leap possible. The J-20's GaN AESA is downstream of them.
- **The reverse-leverage twist.** GaN starts with gallium, and China [dominates global gallium supply](https://features.csis.org/hiddenreach/china-critical-mineral-gallium/) — a chokepoint it began weaponizing with export controls in 2023. So China not only builds the radar modules; it meters the raw material everyone else's radar industry needs. Another domain where the dependency runs outward.

## Electronic warfare

CETC's **29th Institute (Chengdu)** is China's airborne-EW center of gravity — the jamming and electromagnetic-spectrum suite on the **J-16D** and much of the PLA's spectrum-warfare arsenal traces here. Combined with the **54th Institute's** military satcom and datalinks, CETC supplies the full "systems-destruction warfare" toolkit: see the adversary first, jam their sensors and links, then strike.

## The reality gap — the honest hedge

The marketing runs ahead of the proof. A [January 2026 report on Venezuela's Chinese-made JY-27A radars](https://asiatimes.com/2026/07/chinas-anti-stealth-shield-has-a-radar-reality-gap/) during a US operation cited maintenance problems, limited readiness, and weak integration — and analysts caution that counter-stealth *detection* is not the same as producing a *weapons-quality track* good enough to guide an interceptor. China's radar claims are aggressive; the gap between "can detect" and "can kill" is where the honest uncertainty lives, and it is why the domain rates Competitive rather than Dominant.

## The suppliers (CETC's institutes)

- **14th Institute (Nanjing)** — "the cradle of Chinese radar": shipborne Type 346, fighter KLJ-7A, counter-stealth arrays, and now in-house AI chips.
- **38th Institute (Hefei)** — early-warning and counter-stealth radar (JY-27V, SLC lineage).
- **13th & 55th Institutes** — the GaN/GaAs T/R-module foundries.
- **29th Institute (Chengdu)** — airborne electronic warfare (J-16D).
- **54th Institute (Shijiazhuang)** — military satcom, ground stations, datalinks.

CETC is 1260H-listed and OFAC-sanctioned, with several institutes on the Entity List.

## Where it's competitive, not dominant

- **High-end EDA.** The advanced RF and mixed-signal chip design still leans on Western design software — the semiconductor domain's chokepoint reaching into this one.
- **Systems integration & data fusion.** Turning many excellent sensors into one coherent, resilient, weapons-quality picture — and doing it under jamming — is the harder, less-visible edge, and where Western C4ISR retains an advantage.
- **Proven performance.** As the Venezuela case shows, exported readiness and integration are uneven; combat-proven-ness is thin outside the contested Pakistan example.

## Assessment

CETC has taken Chinese radar and EW from generations behind to genuinely competitive — world-leading in some areas (counter-stealth low-band, GaN module volume) and holding a raw-material chokepoint (gallium) over everyone else's radar industry. What separates competitive from dominant is the hard part that doesn't parade well: weapons-quality integration under fire, and the residual dependence on Western design tools. A strong domain with an asterisk.
