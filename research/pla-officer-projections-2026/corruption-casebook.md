# The Casebook: what has actually been adjudicated

*Companion to [`corruption-analysis.md`](corruption-analysis.md), which sets out the inference framework, and [`why-purges-recur.md`](why-purges-recur.md), which asks why the record looks like this. This file is the evidentiary base underneath both — the cases where charge language, method, amount or sentence is actually on the record, as against the far larger set where an officer simply stopped appearing.*

**Prepared: 31 August 2026 · Confidence tags as in the main report.**

---

## 0. Why a casebook, and how to use it

Almost everything written about PLA corruption is written from the *announcement* — a name, a formula, a date. Announcements are thin. They tell you that an officer was removed; they almost never tell you what he did, how the money moved, or what it cost.

A small number of cases are different. In roughly a dozen instances the state has published enough — a full multi-count verdict, an itemised seizure, a court's findings on method — to reconstruct the mechanism rather than infer it. Those cases carry nearly the entire analytical weight of the PLA-corruption literature, including this report's. They are worth setting out precisely, because three things follow:

1. **The mechanisms recur.** The same rent, in the same office, twenty years apart, taken by different men in the same way. Section 1 below is essentially one case told twice.
2. **The disclosure is asymmetric in a systematic way.** Civilian courts trying civilian defence-industry executives publish amounts and transactional theories in full. Military courts trying officers publish neither. That asymmetry is itself a finding (§4).
3. **The best-documented case in the corpus is not a soldier.** The most informative single document available on how money is actually extracted from China's defence sector is the March 2026 Dalian Intermediate People's Court verdict against the former chairman of AVIC — a civilian (§3).

**A caution on what these cases license.** Every case below is a case the state chose to prosecute and chose to publicise. They are not a sample; they are a selection. They establish that certain mechanisms *exist* and *are punishable*. They do not establish frequency, and nothing here supports an estimate of how much graft the PLA sustains in any year — see [`why-purges-recur.md`](why-purges-recur.md) §4 on why that number does not exist.

## 1. The barracks-and-housing rent, taken twice

The clearest demonstration that PLA corruption is *positional* rather than personal is that the same office produced two nearly identical death-with-reprieve cases six years apart.

### 1.1 Wang Shouye 王守业 — the template (2006)

**Post [OFFICIAL].** Director, General Logistics Department Infrastructure & Barracks Department (总后勤部基建营房部部长), concurrently Director of the All-Army Housing Reform Leading Group Office, 1995/96–2001. Then **Deputy Commander, PLA Navy** from July 2001; Vice Admiral 2002, ranked second of five deputy commanders. Due to retire June 2006.

**Period of offending [OFFICIAL].** 1997–2001, while holding the infrastructure and housing portfolio — responsible for the PLA's third-generation barracks renovation programme.

**Method [OFFICIAL].** 以权谋私 — used control of capital construction to embezzle and to take bribes from construction contractors. Ran an off-books slush fund (私设小金库) in an account held in his own office. Distributed nearly RMB 20 million of public funds to colleagues "as welfare" — i.e. bought institutional silence with the same money he was stealing.

**Seized [OFFICIAL].** RMB 52 million in cash plus US$2.5 million in cash across residences in Beijing and Nanjing; over RMB 50 million more in the office slush-fund account.

**Detection [OFFICIAL].** Multiple people had reported him, to no effect whatever. He was brought down only when a mistress, **Jiang Wen** (蒋雯), a military performing-arts figure with whom he had a son, reported him directly to CMC leaders after negotiations over support and marriage broke down. Jiang was herself investigated and her assets — largely bought with Wang's proceeds — impounded by the CMC Discipline Inspection Commission.

**The detail that matters most [OFFICIAL].** In **four of the five years** during which he was committing these offences, Wang was rated an "outstanding Party member" and "outstanding cadre" and won two third-class merit citations. The internal evaluation system did not merely fail to detect him. It certified him.

**Charges and sentence [OFFICIAL].** 10 April 2006, PLA Military Court: 贪污 and 挪用公款 (embezzlement and misappropriation of public funds) totalling **RMB 160 million** — at the time the highest amount and highest rank ever published for a PLA general. Death with two-year reprieve. On 14 December 2006 the **Supreme People's Court**, on second-instance review, reduced this to **life imprisonment**. Detained (双规) December 2005.

### 1.2 Gu Junshan 谷俊山 — the same office, the same crime (2015)

**Post [OFFICIAL].** Deputy Director, GLD Barracks & Housing Department, July 2001; Major General 2003; **Director, GLD Barracks & Housing Department and concurrently Director of the All-Army Housing Reform Office**, June 2007 – December 2009 — *the post Wang Shouye had held*; Deputy Director, General Logistics Department, December 2009 – February 2012; Lieutenant General July 2011.

**The rent [OFFICIAL].** During his tenure the PLA sharply raised housing standards for every officer grade, adjusted all-army housing rents, undertook large-scale barracks construction, and comprehensively rolled out competitive-bidding land transfer — from which **cumulative land-transfer proceeds exceeded RMB 30 billion (300多亿元)**.

That is the mechanism in one sentence: the PLA held enormous urban land acquired for nothing, and Gu ran the office that decided which parcels were released, to whom, at what price, and who got the resulting housing — while also running the office that wrote the rules.

**Methods [OFFICIAL / REPORTED].**

- **Land disposal.** [REPORTED — Caixin, January 2014] Roughly **6% of proceeds** on a Shanghai land sale worth ~RMB 2 billion (~US$327m); arrangements with real-estate companies taking up to a **60% profit share**.
- **Housing-reform allocation.** The distribution of upgraded officer housing is itself a good of enormous value.
- **Captive family suppliers.** [OFFICIAL/REPORTED] Gu's brother **Gu Xianjun** (谷献军) ran the family's "Rong"-named enterprises. A furniture factory and a canvas factory in central Puyang supplied output **exclusively to military logistics** — known locally as "the arsenal." The canvas plant ran a workshop of over 1,000 m² with several hundred workers and annual output of RMB 30–40 million. The office-equipment company was granted **300 mu** of land in a single allocation by Puyang High-Tech Zone leaders, **30 mu of which was then resold**. Both closed when Gu came under investigation.
- **Village land conversion.** Gu Xianjun, as village Party secretary of Dongbaicang 2001–2010, sold **over 3,000 mu** of collective land without villagers' consent, and encroached on land designated for affordable and resettlement housing.
- **Petition suppression as an enabling service.** Gu's wife **Zhang Suyan** (张素燕) was a Puyang public-security official handling petitions (信访), later head of the High-Tech district PSB sub-bureau and political commissar of the municipal PSB. She retained her Puyang post while living in Beijing, working at the Puyang Beijing Liaison Office **intercepting petitioners** — including villagers travelling to complain about her brother-in-law. *The graft was protected by a dedicated grievance-suppression capability.*
- **Buying protection upward.** [OFFICIAL/REPORTED] January 2012: **RMB 40 million to Xu Caihou**; [REPORTED] a gift card worth ~RMB 20 million to Xu's daughter.

**Seized [OFFICIAL].** At the family home in Dongbaicang village, Puyang, Henan, investigators found in the basement a large **gold ship** (寓意"一帆风顺"), a **gold washbasin** (寓意"金玉满盆"), a **solid gold statue of Mao Zedong**, and large quantities of military-exclusive Moutai. **Four trucks** were required to remove the suspected proceeds.

The **"General's Mansion" (将军府)** was built 2006–2011 on 13–14 mu of collective village land, **designed by engineers from the Palace Museum's design institute in imitation of Forbidden City architecture**. Gu Xianjun named the road in front of it 容府大道. A *second* mansion, near Wukesong on Taiping Road in Beijing's **Haidian District** — in the middle of the district where the PLA's leading organs sit — was revealed on 2 October 2017 in episode five of 《强军》, the documentary co-produced by the **CMC Political Work Department** and CCTV.

**How the case broke [OFFICIAL/REPORTED].** At the CMC enlarged meeting of 25–28 December 2011 — roughly 100 attendees, including all CMC members and the principals of every general department, service, arm, military region and provincial military district — GLD Political Commissar General **Liu Yuan** (刘源) held up a photograph of the mansion and named Gu Junshan in front of the room. Liu then stated that the following were *pervasive*:

> 贪污军产、倒卖军火、卖官鬻爵、侵吞公款、私用公款、乱吃回扣
>
> *embezzling military assets; trafficking in arms; selling offices and titles; misappropriating public funds; using public funds privately; taking kickbacks indiscriminately.*

He pointed openly at Xu Caihou, Guo Boxiong and Liang Guanglie.

[REPORTED — John Garnaut, "Rotting from Within," *Foreign Policy*, 16 April 2012] Hu Jintao had to fall back on the **civilian** Central Discipline Inspection Commission because the PLA's own disciplinary body had **ignored his direct orders** to investigate Gu. That is the founding datum of the principal–agent analysis in [`why-purges-recur.md`](why-purges-recur.md) §1: the agent did not merely fail to report — it refused a lawful instruction from the principal.

**Verdict [OFFICIAL].** Indicted 31 March 2014 on 贪污罪, 受贿罪, 挪用公款罪 and 滥用职权罪; **行贿罪 added during trial**. On 10 August 2015 the PLA Military Court sentenced:

| Count | Sentence |
|---|---|
| 贪污罪 embezzlement | death, suspended 2 years |
| 受贿罪 taking bribes | death, suspended 2 years |
| 挪用公款罪 misappropriation | 10 years |
| 行贿罪 offering bribes | 14 years + RMB 500,000 confiscation |
| 滥用职权罪 abuse of power | 6 years |
| **Combined** | **death with two-year reprieve; political rights removed for life; all personal property confiscated; illicit gains recovered; stripped of Lieutenant General rank** |

**No total amount was published.** Trial footage in 《强军》 showed a co-defendant tried alongside him, never publicly identified.

### 1.3 What the pairing establishes

Wang Shouye and Gu Junshan held the same portfolio and committed the same offence with the same assets, separated by six years and one intervening prosecution that was the largest in PLA history at the time. The 2006 conviction did not change the behaviour of the man who took the desk next.

This is the strongest single piece of evidence for the positional-rent reading of PLA corruption: **the office generates the crime.** It is also the reason this report treats portfolio, not personality, as the primary risk variable in [`corruption-analysis.md`](corruption-analysis.md) §2 — and why an exposure register organised by *seat* is defensible where one organised by *character* would not be.

## 2. The promotion market: Xu Caihou and Guo Boxiong

### 2.1 Xu Caihou 徐才厚

**Posts [OFFICIAL].** Secretary, **CMC Discipline Inspection Commission**, December 2000 – November 2002; Director, **General Political Department**, November 2002 – September 2004; **CMC Vice Chairman** (Party) September 2004 – November 2012; Politburo member 2007–2012. General 1999.

*Read that sequence again. He ran military discipline, then the department that controls promotion, then the commission that controls both.*

**Charge language [OFFICIAL].** Investigation opened 15 March 2014; expelled from the Party 30 June 2014 for 严重违纪 and 涉嫌受贿犯罪; expelled from military service and stripped of rank 30 July 2014; case transferred for prosecution 27 October 2014. The finding: assistance for others' **promotion in post**, bribes taken **directly and through family members**, 数额特别巨大 ("the amount being particularly enormous"), full confession.

**Amount: never published.**

**Seized [REPORTED].** Over one tonne of mixed currency; 100–200+ kg of jade; antiquities, artwork, precious hardwoods; **12 trucks**. Cash reportedly **boxed and labelled with individual officers' names** — which, if accurate, means the market kept books.

**Disposition [OFFICIAL].** Died of terminal bladder cancer with multi-organ failure, 15 March 2015, aged 71. Prosecution terminated under Article 15 of the Criminal Procedure Law. **He was never tried.**

**Xi's own characterisation [REPORTED — NYT, 11 May 2026, from a previously unreported version of Xi's 2014 Gutian speech]:** *"Xu Caihou always swore verbally that he was 'forever loyal to the Party and loved the Party,' but in fact in the depths of his soul he had long since become estranged from the Party and rotten."*

That sentence is doing more work in 2026 than it did in 2014. It is the template for how the Party now explains men Xi personally elevated — see [`why-purges-recur.md`](why-purges-recur.md) §3.

### 2.2 Guo Boxiong 郭伯雄

**Posts [OFFICIAL].** CMC Vice Chairman (Party) November 2002 – November 2012; Politburo member 2002–2012.

**Timeline [OFFICIAL].** Investigation opened 9 April 2015 (not announced at the time); Politburo reviewed the CMC DIC report and expelled him 30 July 2015; case transferred 5 April 2016.

**Charge language [OFFICIAL].** The military procuratorate established that Guo *利用职务便利，为他人职务晋升或调整提供帮助，直接和通过家人收受贿赂，数额特别巨大* — "used the convenience of his office to provide assistance for the promotion or reassignment of others in post, accepted bribes directly and through family members, the amount being particularly enormous." He confessed without reservation.

**The formula is identical to Xu Caihou's, word for word in its operative clause.** That matters for the charge-language decoder in [`corruption-analysis.md`](corruption-analysis.md) §3: this is a *stock* formula for office-selling, and when it appears it is telling you which market the officer was in.

### 2.3 Guo Zhenggang 郭正钢 — the case with no ending

Detained by military prosecutors **10 February 2015**. A serving major general and the son of a CMC vice chairman. **Eleven and a half years later there is no published disposition.**

This is the single best illustration of what [`corruption-analysis.md`](corruption-analysis.md) §3 calls Tier D — silence. It also sets the outer bound on the timing model in [`forecasting-model.md`](forecasting-model.md): the distribution of disappearance-to-announcement intervals has no known right-hand tail, because at least one case has been open for over a decade.

## 3. The most informative document in the corpus — and it is a civilian one

**Tan Ruisong 谭瑞松**, General Manager of AVIC March 2012 – May 2018; **Chairman and Party Group Secretary of AVIC**, 7 May 2018 – March 2023.

**Timeline [OFFICIAL].** Investigated by the CCDI/NSC 30 August 2024; expelled from the Party 24 February 2025, with the characterising phrase **靠军工吃军工** — "relying on the military-industrial sector to eat off the military-industrial sector"; tried at **Dalian Intermediate People's Court** 30 October 2025; verdict **25 March 2026**.

**The court's published findings [OFFICIAL]:**

> **贪污 (embezzlement)** — *July 2003 to 2010*: using the convenience of his posts as deputy general manager of China Aviation Industry Corporation II and then of China Aviation Industry Corporation, he unlawfully took public property totalling **RMB 89.93 million**.
>
> **受贿 (taking bribes)** — *1998 to 2024*: across posts from deputy general manager of **Harbin Dong'an Engine (Group)** to the chairmanship of AVIC, he **provided assistance to others in matters including 企业并购 (mergers and acquisitions) and 承揽项目 (winning and contracting for projects)**, unlawfully receiving property totalling **more than RMB 613 million**.
>
> **内幕交易、泄露内幕信息 (insider trading and disclosure of inside information)** — *March 2012 to March 2023*: repeatedly traded securities connected to inside information during sensitive periods and repeatedly leaked it, **expressly or implicitly directing others to trade**; circumstances particularly serious.

**Sentence [OFFICIAL]:** 贪污罪 — 15 years + RMB 5 million fine. 受贿罪 — **death, suspended two years**, political rights removed for life, all personal property confiscated. 内幕交易罪 — 6 years + fine. Combined: death with two-year reprieve. Embezzled property returned to the victim entities; bribe proceeds and accrued interest remitted to the state treasury.

**Four things this verdict establishes that no military case does:**

1. **The rent is in M&A and contract award, not in unit prices.** The court did not find Tan inflating airframe prices. It found him **selling decisions about who gets acquired and who gets the work**. In a consolidating state-owned aviation sector, the chairman's power to direct a merger or award a subcontract is worth far more than any margin on a delivered aircraft. This is why the defence-industry interface, not the shop floor, is where the money is — and it is the specific reason [`defense-industrial-base.md`](defense-industrial-base.md) treats conglomerate leadership churn as a leading indicator for uniformed procurement cases.
2. **The duration is twenty-six years.** Tan took bribes continuously from a single engine plant in Harbin to the chairmanship of the national aviation conglomerate. **The corruption scaled with the man.** He was not corrupted by high office; he arrived carrying the practice and expanded its scope with each promotion. Any campaign that removes incumbents while recruiting from the same career pipeline re-imports the practice with the replacement.
3. **The insider-trading count is a channel that did not exist a generation ago.** The defence-industrial consolidation Tan was directing was itself the tradeable event. Monetising state secrets through a listed market requires no cash, no basement, and no truck — and leaves no seizure photograph. Investigators looking for gold washbasins will not find this.
4. **靠军工吃军工 is a positional-rent theory in four characters.** The CCDI is not alleging that Tan stole a thing; it is alleging that he *consumed the sector by virtue of sitting in it*. That is the same theory this report applies to the barracks portfolio in §1.

**And the asymmetry.** Tan's judgment discloses RMB 613 million and the specific transactional theories. Li Shangfu's and Wei Fenghe's — handed down six weeks later, for the two most senior defence officials China has ever prosecuted — disclose **nothing**. Same state, same campaign, same year; one court publishes a forensic account and the other publishes a sentence. Whatever explains that gap (classification, unwillingness to quantify damage to a force preparing for 2027, or a decision to keep deterrence purely hierarchical), the practical consequence for analysts is that **the best available window into PLA-adjacent graft is through civilian defendants**, and it will stay that way.

**Not one uniformed verdict in this table publishes an amount.** The single exception, Wang Shouye's RMB 160 million, is twenty years old and was published precisely because it was a record. The trend in disclosure has run in one direction: *downward*.

**Two rows deserve separate comment.**

**Zhang Yang** is the case that should discipline any analysis of the current campaign. He directed the Political Work Department and personally chaired the all-army leading group charged with eliminating the residual influence of Guo Boxiong and Xu Caihou — while being, on the state's own subsequent account, part of it. The enforcer *was* the case.

**Li Shangfu** is the only defence minister charged with **行贿 (offering bribes)** as well as **受贿 (taking them)**, and the charge sheet records that he sought personnel benefits *for himself*. That combination is the empirical foundation of this report's central mechanism claim in [`corruption-analysis.md`](corruption-analysis.md) §2A: procurement graft and office-selling are not two markets but **one circuit** — money extracted from equipment programmes flows upward to buy the next appointment, which enlarges the extractable rent. Li is the documented instance of a man on both sides of that circuit simultaneously.

**The Zhang Jianhua charge deserves flagging on its own.** 斡旋受贿 — *influence-peddling bribery*, accepting payment to use one's influence over **other** officials rather than one's own authority — is the legal category that captures brokerage. It was charged against a man who had run **SASTIND's Military Project Audit Centre** and then its Finance & Audit Department. The audit function was itself for sale. That is a specific, adjudicated instance of the structural point in [`why-purges-recur.md`](why-purges-recur.md) §2.

**And a scale check from the bottom of the pyramid.** [REPORTED — Yang Yi, "Causes and Solutions: Corruption in Chinese Military Enterprises," *Journal of Humanities, Arts and Social Science* 5, no. 1 (2021): 69–80] Major General **Li Mingquan** reportedly helped a private firm obtain military orders for **RMB 1.4 million**. The same mechanism as Tan Ruisong's, four orders of magnitude smaller, at brigade level. Order-steering is not a general-officer speciality; it is available at every grade that signs a contract.

## 6. What is *not* in the casebook

Rigour here means naming the empty boxes as clearly as the full ones.

**吃空饷 (ghost payroll).** No adjudicated PLA case found. The category is plausible — Wang Shouye disguised RMB 20 million of public funds as personnel welfare, and the PLA maintains a large reserve, militia and People's Armed Forces Department structure with weak headcount verification — but plausibility is not evidence. **Currently an assertion without a case.**

**Military-civil fusion arbitrage.** [EVIDENCE GAP] No documented, adjudicated case framed as 军民融合 abuse — no MCF subsidy fraud, no capture of MCF investment vehicles, no dual-use designation fraud. The MCF policy literature documents the architecture (the Central Military-Civil Fusion Development Committee, established January 2017) and records no MCF-specific prosecutions. The closest analogue is the **August 2024 debarment of Xi'an Technological University, Xi'an Jiaotong University and Southwest Jiaotong University from Rocket Force procurement for 围标串标 (bid-rigging)** — corruption at the civilian-university/military-procurement interface, which is what MCF means operationally in R&D. MCF creates *a priori* arbitrage opportunities; as of August 2026, none has been publicly adjudicated on that theory. **Watch item, not a finding.**

**Arms trafficking.** Named by Liu Yuan in December 2011 (倒卖军火) as pervasive. No PLA case has been publicly prosecuted on that theory since.

**Amounts, for any uniformed officer since 2006.** See §4.

**Whether Li Yuchao was sentenced.** English-language sources record a suspended death sentence on 7 May 2026; Chinese-language sources for the same date name only Wei Fenghe and Li Shangfu. **Unresolved** — and a reminder that even the sentencing record has soft edges.

## 7. What the casebook is for

Three uses, in descending order of confidence.

1. **As the evidentiary floor.** Every mechanism claim in [`corruption-analysis.md`](corruption-analysis.md) traces to a case above. Where it does not, the claim is marked inferential. A reader who distrusts this report's analysis can check the analysis against the casebook without leaving the repository.
2. **As a calibration device for reading announcements.** The gap between what an announcement says and what the casebook shows the underlying conduct to have been is roughly constant. When the MND says an officer is under investigation for 严重违纪违法, the casebook is the answer to "what does that usually turn out to mean."
3. **As the source of the seat-level exposure register.** The barracks portfolio produced two death sentences from the same desk. The equipment portfolio has now destroyed three consecutive Equipment Development Department directors. The Political Work Department has been decapitated twice. Those are the three most productive rent-bearing offices in the PLA on the documented record, and they are the three that anchor the exposure register in the main report's §6.3 — not because of anything known about the men currently in them, but because of what is known about the desks.
