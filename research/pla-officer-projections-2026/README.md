## 0. How to read this document

This is an open-source analytical assessment of the kind think tanks (Jamestown, CASI, CSIS, RAND) and specialist journalists produce routinely. Three ground rules govern everything below:

1. **Facts are sourced.** Statements about who holds what post, who was promoted, and who was expelled rest on official announcements (Ministry of National Defense, Xinhua, NPC Standing Committee bulletins) or quality reporting (SCMP, Caixin, Reuters, Jamestown, CSIS, CMSI/Naval War College). Each load-bearing claim carries a confidence tag:
   - **[OFFICIAL]** — announced by PRC state organs
   - **[REPORTED]** — carried by named, quality outlets
   - **[WIKI]** — sourced to Chinese/English Wikipedia career chronologies (generally reliable for PLA bios but unofficial)
   - **[RUMOR]** — circulating in émigré/dissident media (Epoch Times, RFA columns, aboluowang); noted only where the underlying signal (e.g., an officer's disappearance from public view) is independently checkable
2. **Projections are probabilistic guesses, clearly labeled.** Nobody — including, on the evidence of the last three years, the officers themselves — can know who will run the PLA in 2036. What analysis *can* do is identify the eligible cohort (age × grade × network), the seats that must be filled, and the selection logic Xi Jinping has revealed through ~four years of purges and promotions, then rank candidates. Confidence bands are given throughout.
3. **The corruption section is speculative risk assessment, not accusation.** Section 6 estimates *exposure* — which serving officers sit on portfolios and patronage lines that the documented purge record shows to be dangerous. Unless an officer appears in the purge tracker with an official announcement, **nothing in this document should be read as a claim that any named individual has committed any offense.** In today's PLA, being purged is not proof of corruption, and survival is not proof of innocence — a point developed at length in §6.

## 1. Executive summary

**The situation.** As of 31 August 2026 the People's Liberation Army is in the most severe leadership crisis of the post-Mao era. The clearest way to see it is a count. Across the PLA's **eighteen principal operational billets** — five theater commanders, five theater political commissars, and the commander and political commissar of each of the four services — exactly **three are substantively held by full generals; four are vacant; and eleven are held by lieutenant generals or vice admirals acting above their grade** (§2.3). All ten theater principal billets turned over between March and December 2025 (§2.2), and the Ground Force, the largest service on earth, has neither a commander nor a political commissar. The Central Military Commission that seven men joined at the 2022 Party Congress is down to two: Xi Jinping and Vice Chairman Zhang Shengmin. Five of the six uniformed members have been destroyed, including — in January 2026 — Xi's own oldest military ally, first Vice Chairman Zhang Youxia, and the PLA's top operational officer, Joint Staff chief Liu Zhenli (both formally stripped on 28 August 2026, three days before this writing). Across the force, roughly half of the ~176 most senior billets have turned over. The Army has gone ~20 months with no commander; the Rocket Force has no full generals at all and is run by an acting caretaker; the **Western Theater Command, which faces India, has had a formally vacant commander's seat for about twelve months**, and the Taiwan-facing Eastern Theater ran nine months without one in 2025. Service headquarters and the entire strategic-forces, logistics and procurement complex are riddled with vacancies and acting officers. **The PLA is being run, at the strategic level, by a caretaker layer of lieutenant generals and vice admirals.**

**What that does to a projection.** Three things. First, the base rate is now measured rather than guessed, and it is brutal: the lifetime purge rate for uniformed CMC members rose from **9.1% under Jiang Zemin and Hu Jintao to 66.7% under Xi**, and **50% of the officers Xi promoted into the vacancies created by the last great purge were themselves purged** (72.7% for the tightest analogue cohort). No individual forecast in this system deserves high confidence; the honest unit of analysis is the *seat* and the *type of officer* who fills it, not the name.

Second — and this inverts the natural reading of any list like this one — **the historical record says exposure duration, not favour, determines who survives.** Among the last rebuild cohort, officers out of post by 2018 were purged at 8.3%; those still in senior post into the 2020s, at 81.3%. Today's risers have not survived anything; they have just entered the window. Read "most promising" as "most exposed" with equal weight.

Third, the generational handover this exercise was meant to project into the 2030s **has already started, early, under purge conditions** — the men now acting in the top jobs were born 1969–1971, a decade younger than those they replaced. And the promotion freeze is rank-specific: full-general ceremonies have nearly stopped, but the lieutenant-general layer beneath is still being filled, which is where the 2032 leadership is being assembled in plain sight.

**Who is most likely to be very senior in 2031 / 2036 / 2041.** The single strongest name-level call is **Vice Admiral Zhang Zheng** (b. 1969) — first captain of the carrier *Liaoning*, UK-educated, now de facto head of the Navy at 56 — who is positioned for a CMC seat by 2032 and a vice-chairmanship by the late 2030s. Around him, a distinct "risers" cohort recurs across all three horizons: **Zhu Chuansheng** (b. 1970, ex-Marine, now running the Joint Staff), **Dai Mingmeng** (b. 1971, carrier-aviation hero, now a theater-navy deputy commander), **Liu Zhe** (b. 1970, in the CMC war room), **Huang Xucong** (b. 1968, the best-credentialed young ground-force officer), and — for the 2041 horizon — **Chen Zhiguo**, captain of the new carrier *Fujian* and the only officer of the mid-1970s cohort China has publicly entrusted with a strategic national asset. Above them, four principles govern who Xi promotes: **the discipline/enforcer class is ascendant** (the last CMC survivor and both 2026 elevations are discipline-lineage); **Air Force generals are the interim operational elite** (two theaters, the new service chief); **internal-security credentials now beat warfighting résumés** (a PAP counter-terror officer runs a conventional theater); and **seats are left empty rather than filled fast** — so every permanent appointment between now and the 21st Congress (~autumn 2027) is a direct read on the next high command. Full ranked slates with confidence bands are in §5.

**The corruption question (§6, read the caveats).** The documented purge shows corruption is *structural*, concentrated in four rent systems: equipment/procurement (**all three** Equipment Development Department directors in the department's history destroyed, plus a defence-industry cohort now past fifteen conglomerate and institute principals), the political-work promotion market (the PWD decapitated twice), Rocket Force/strategic construction, and logistics. The forward-looking, explicitly speculative reading: the highest-probability *future* exposure among officers still standing sits with **Navy Equipment Department director Wang Hongli** and the naval shipbuilding chain (the next boom to be audited), a further **Rocket Force procurement cluster** (a fresh tip-solicitation opened days ago), the **logistics/JLSF** next layer, and formal charges against the dozen senior officers who have already *vanished* from public view without announcement (Hu Zhongming, Wu Yanan, Wang Haijiang, Huang Ming, Chang Dingqiu, and more) — on the 2023–26 pattern, disappearance precedes the announcement. Two findings from the second research pass cut against the natural reading of all this. First, **the purge is not costing the PLA money**: SIPRI records that Chinese military spending rose 7.4% in real terms in 2025 — the largest single-year increase in a decade — and that the campaign “did not appear to result in any new constraints.” This is a distributional fight over a growing budget, not a retrenchment. Second, **the campaign has no external monitor and never has had one**: the 2016–17 reform raised the discipline commission's grade but left enforcement in the hands of officers drawn from the population being enforced against — which is why Xu Caihou ran military discipline before he ran the promotion market, and why Wang Houbin, installed in 2023 to clean the Rocket Force from outside, was expelled from it in 2025 (§6.5). But the deepest finding is an epistemic one: **the purge is no longer a corruption campaign with political side effects — it is a loyalty-reconstruction campaign that uses corruption as its legal instrument.** In this system, being purged is weak evidence of guilt and survival is weak evidence of innocence; some of the fallen are guilty only of the wrong patron, and some of this document's "most promising" officers are carrying liabilities that will surface the moment they stop being useful. Nothing here is an accusation against any serving officer — §6 estimates *exposure*, not conduct.

**Confidence.** High on the current org chart and the documented purge record (both heavily sourced); moderate on the selection *logic* and the seat-level projections; deliberately low and probabilistic on individual names, especially past 2031. The further out the horizon, the more the answer becomes a claim about *institutions* (the carrier strike groups, the Eastern Theater joint staff, the missile bases, the discipline apparatus) rather than people — and past ~2036 it collides with the unresolved question of Xi's own succession, treated as three scenarios in §5.3.

## 2. The state of play, August 2026: a decapitated military

Any projection has to start from an honest accounting of how abnormal this moment is. There is no precedent for it in post-Mao China — and it changes the projection method itself (§3).

### 2.1 The Central Military Commission is down to Xi plus one

The 20th Party Congress (October 2022) seated a seven-man CMC: Xi Jinping; vice chairmen **Zhang Youxia** and **He Weidong**; members **Li Shangfu** (defense minister), **Liu Zhenli** (chief of the Joint Staff Department), **Miao Hua** (director of the Political Work Department), and **Zhang Shengmin** (secretary of the Discipline Inspection Commission).

Five of the six uniformed members of the 20th CMC have been destroyed. The two men purged this January were not peripheral: Zhang Youxia was Xi's oldest military ally — a princeling family friend whose father fought alongside Xi's father — retained past retirement age in 2022 precisely because he was trusted; Liu Zhenli was the PLA's top operational officer and the most plausible next senior vice chairman. A PLA Daily commentary accused the pair of having "betrayed the trust of the party and undermined the party's absolute control of the military" — language of political betrayal, not merely graft [REPORTED: SCMP, Caixin]. Days before his investigation was announced, Zhang Youxia was still publicly announcing promotion orders at the 22 December 2025 ceremony.

Beyond the CMC proper, the same weeks consumed **Zhong Shaojun** (钟绍军) — the former director of the CMC General Office who had been Xi's closest personal military aide since Zhejiang days — and **Ju Qiansheng** (巨乾生), former Strategic Support Force commander, both stripped of NPC seats on 28 August 2026 [OFFICIAL].

**The CMC today is effectively Xi Jinping and Vice Chairman Zhang Shengmin**, with Defense Minister **Dong Jun** outside the commission (he has never received the CMC seat or State Councilor rank his predecessors held), an acting Political Work Department director, a vacant Joint Staff chief's chair, and a newly minted discipline secretary (**Zhang Shuguang**, promoted 3 July 2026). CSIS's February 2026 assessment counted **100+ senior officers removed since 2022, touching roughly half of the PLA's ~176 most senior billets, including 38 theater-command-grade commanders and political commissars and ~56 deputy-theater-grade officers**; between a third and four-fifths of the PLA's 2022 Central Committee delegation is gone [REPORTED: CSIS].

### 2.2 Two org charts: the official one and the observed one

The **observed ledger** — who actually appears at meetings, receptions, parades, and in the leadership tables that Chinese-language trackers maintain from protocol coverage — is far worse. On the observed ledger, a **synchronized sweep between the 3 September 2025 parade and the October 2025 Fourth Plenum emptied most of the theater-command top floor without a single announcement**: tenure records and protocol absences indicate Southern TC commander **Wu Yanan**, Western TC commander **Wang Haijiang**, Northern TC commander **Huang Ming**, and theater political commissars **Zheng Xuan** (NTC), **Xu Deqing** (CTC), and ETC's **He Ping** all stopped appearing around September–October 2025; Army political commissar **Chen Hui** — promoted to full general only in December 2024 — may have followed them around December 2025, though his case is genuinely unresolved (see the caution below); roughly **fourteen full generals were reported absent without explanation from the March 2026 Two Sessions** [WIKI tenure records; REPORTED: overseas monitoring; individually UNVERIFIED as investigations — no official action announced against any of them].

**A caution on reading absence.** The observed ledger is inference, and it can be wrong in a specific way worth naming. Chinese-language leadership trackers are maintained by volunteers reading protocol coverage; a roster page and a biographical page for the same officer can disagree, and a single low-quality outlet's "missing generals" list can propagate into apparent consensus. Chen Hui is the live example: one service-roster page ends his tenure in December 2025 and one outlet counts him among the absent, while his own biographical entry still lists him as the incumbent Army political commissar with no adverse note. Absence from public view is also produced by illness, ordinary retirement, and reassignment — none of which the PLA announces either. This report therefore treats disappearance as a *probabilistic leading indicator*, calibrated in [`forecasting-model.md`](forecasting-model.md) §1, rather than as evidence of wrongdoing; where the underlying signal is thin, it says so.

Of the ten replacements, **only two are substantive full generals** (Yang Zhibin, Han Shengyan). **One command seat and one commissar seat are simply empty.** The remaining **six are acting lieutenant generals**, five of them holding the theater job concurrently with their existing deputy billet — the definition of a caretaker arrangement.

Then read the services. Three of the five theaters have their political work run by an officer from **outside the theater's dominant arm** — two Rocket Force political officers (Zhang Jichun at Eastern, Zhang Shaoying at Western) and a naval one (Li Dongyou at Northern). Cross-posting political commissars into unfamiliar theaters is not a staffing accident; it is what a system does when it wants **watchers who owe nothing to the people they are watching**. It is the same logic that put a PAP counter-terrorism officer in charge of the Northern Theater, and it is the single most concrete evidence for this report's claim that loyalty verification has displaced operational continuity as the organising principle of PLA personnel policy.

**The theater commands have been running with holes in them for a year or more.** Three documented gaps, each at a strategically live command: the **Eastern Theater** (Taiwan) ran roughly nine months without a commander in 2025 after Lin Xiangyang's removal; the **Western Theater** (India) is on its **twelfth month with the commander's seat formally vacant**; and the **Northern Theater** (Korea, Russia) went empty from September 2025 until an acting commander was installed in 2026. For a military whose entire 2016 reform was built around empowering joint theater commanders, this is not a personnel inconvenience — it is a hole in the warfighting chain of command, and it is the strongest single piece of evidence that Xi is prioritising political reliability over operational readiness.

Add the two sitting theater commanders who fell earlier in 2025 — **Lin Xiangyang** (Eastern TC, vanished ~March, expelled October; the Taiwan-facing theater ran ~nine months headless [REPORTED: CSIS]) and **Wang Qiang** (Central TC, the first PLAAF officer ever to command a theater; stopped appearing mid-2025, silently replaced in December [REPORTED: RFA; protocol absences]) — and the totals become: **since September 2024, every one of the five theater commands has lost its commander, its political commissar, or both, mostly without announcement.**

### 2.4 A caretaker military: lieutenant generals running a superpower's armed forces

The deeper pattern in the table above: the PLA's strategic level is now substantially a **caretaker government of lieutenant generals and vice admirals** — Cai Zhijun (Army), Zhang Zheng and Hu Yuhai (Navy), Zhu Chuansheng (Joint Staff), Lei Kai and Zhou Jingjiong (Rocket Force), Wang Xianfeng (Southern TC), Peng Jingtang (Northern TC), Zhang Shaoying (Western TC political work), Liang Ping (Central TC political work), Zhang Jichun (Eastern TC political work), Chen Demin (PWD), Shi Honggan (PLAAF political work), Cao Junzhang (PAP), Bi Yi (ISF), Han Xiaodong (CSF political work) — men acting above their rank in billets that doctrine assigns to full generals. And note the birth years inside that caretaker set: Zhang Zheng (b. 1969) and Zhu Chuansheng (b. Apr 1970), with Dai Mingmeng (b. 1971) a vice admiral one rung below. **The generational handover this document was commissioned to project for the 2030s has already begun, in acting capacities, under purge conditions.** Chinese-language commentary has a phrase for it: **上将断层，中将治军** — "a full-general gap: lieutenant generals are running the military" [REPORTED, headline-level].

The arithmetic of the full-general corps makes the point brutally:

- Promotions to 上将: **17 (2019) → 5 (2020) → 9 (2021) → 8 (2022) → 7 (2023) → 4 (2024) → 2 (2025) → 2 (2026 to date)** [OFFICIAL ceremonies; WIKI tally]
- Full generals visibly functioning at the March 2026 Two Sessions: **roughly four** (Zhang Shengmin, Dong Jun, Yang Zhibin, Han Shengyan) — joined in July by Zhang Shuguang and Wang Gang, for **about six** today [REPORTED: session monitoring; consistent with ASPI's count]
- PLA delegates to the NPC: **281 (2023) → 237 (June 2026)** — a net loss of 44 seats, overwhelmingly to disciplinary removals [REPORTED: roster tallies]
- The 3 September 2025 V-J Day parade — the regime's showcase — was commanded by a **lieutenant general** (Han Shengyan) for the first time, because no trusted full general was available in the parade-owning billet [OFFICIAL coverage; REPORTED analysis]

**But the freeze is rank-specific, and that is the more interesting finding.** While 上将 promotions have nearly stopped, the **lieutenant-general / vice-admiral layer is still being filled** — Dai Mingmeng was promoted vice admiral in December 2025, Liu Zhe by August 2026, Peng Jingtang and Fu Wenhua in 2025, Zhang Zheng and Zhu Chuansheng into service- and joint-staff billets across 2025–26. The bottleneck sits precisely where promotion becomes a **public political act**: a full-general ceremony is televised, personally conducted by Xi, and reads as an anointment, whereas two-star appointments are made quietly and can be reversed without embarrassment. So the PLA is not failing to identify successors — it is **declining to endorse them publicly**. For this report's purposes that is encouraging rather than otherwise: the 2032 leadership is being assembled now, in a layer that is observable if you look one rung below where everyone else is looking.

Xi has not stopped promoting because there are no vacancies — vacancies have never been more plentiful. He has stopped because **he does not yet trust enough people to fill them**, and because every ceremony is now a highly legible signal of who is in the inner circle. The officers elevated across the last three ceremonies are therefore the closest thing to a published answer key for this document's question:

### 2.5 What the rebuild pattern reveals

Five selection principles are visible in who has risen since late 2024 — they drive every projection in §5:

1. **The enforcer class is ascendant — but not immune.** The last CMC survivor (Zhang Shengmin) is a career political-officer-turned-discipline-chief from the missile force. The one man he has been allowed to elevate into a CMC-department directorship is a pure discipline-system product: Zhang Shuguang's entire career ran through case-review and inspection bureaus (GPD discipline department → CMC DIC bureaus → Northern TC Air Force discipline secretary → Army discipline secretary → CMC DIC secretary). The pattern repeats one rung down: the Army political commissar named in December 2024, **Chen Hui**, was himself a former Air Force *discipline* secretary rather than a classic propaganda-track commissar. The Rocket Force's own discipline secretary was purged (Wang Zhibin, July 2025) and the discipline commission's deputy secretary Tang Yong lost his CPPCC seat (March 2025) — the watchmen are watched too — but as a *class*, discipline officers are the only network being systematically promoted rather than destroyed.
2. **Air Force generals are the interim operational elite.** Both new theater commanders (Yang Zhibin, Han Shengyan) and the new service chief (Wang Gang) are aviators, as were the fallen EDD director (Xu Xueqiang), JLSF commander (Wang Kangping) and the vanished Chang Dingqiu and Wang Qiang. This is less "the PLAAF won" than "the ground-force patronage networks lost": the He Weidong (Fujian/31st Group Army) and Zhang Youxia (armaments/Shaanxi) lineages are radioactive, and theater-air-force staff officers are the largest clean pool at the right grade. The purge does not spare aviators as a class — it spares *unaffiliated* officers, many of whom happen to wear blue.
3. **Internal-security credentials now outrank joint-warfare résumés.** The clearest single appointment of 2026: **Peng Jingtang** — a career People's Armed Police counter-terrorism officer (Xinjiang PAP chief of staff, PAP deputy COS, then Hong Kong Garrison commander) — acting commander of the Northern Theater, a conventional-war command facing the Korean Peninsula and Russia. His profile (Xinjiang stability + Hong Kong political reliability) is the profile Xi is buying.
4. **Cross-network orphans are preferred.** The risers share an absence: no 31st Group Army service, no EDD/armaments chain, no debt to Miao Hua's cadre pipeline. Officers from *watching* institutions (discipline, staff bureaus, test bases, training departments) are stepping over officers from *commanding* lineages.
5. **Seats are being left empty rather than filled fast — so each eventual filling is the signal.** An acting Rocket Force commander, an acting PWD director, an acting PAP commander, a two-year-vacant Army commandership: Xi is tolerating extraordinary command-and-control risk rather than promote anyone he might have to destroy later. The corollary: **every permanent appointment between now and the 21st Congress (~autumn 2027) is a direct read on the next CMC.**

## 3. Framework: how PLA elites are made (and unmade)

### 3.1 The mechanics that still hold

- **Grade before rank.** The PLA's real hierarchy is its 15-grade ladder (corps deputy → corps leader → theater deputy → theater leader → CMC member → CMC vice chairman), not the three general-officer ranks. Grades attach to *organizations* as well as people, and the PLA's own rule is explicit: where an officer of higher rank is subordinate to one of lower rank, **the higher grade is the superior**. Never infer authority from stars. Service commanders, theater commanders and the theater political commissars sit at theater-leader grade — the pool from which CMC members are drawn. ([`methodology.md`](methodology.md) §1)
- **Age gates, stated properly.** The statute contains **two** ceilings, routinely conflated: a maximum age to *hold a billet* of a given grade (corps 55, **theater-deputy 63, theater-leader 65**) and a higher maximum age to *remain on active duty at all* (corps leader 60), which is the actual up-or-out machinery. Below theater level an officer pushed out of a billet can stay in uniform another 3–5 years; **at theater level the two converge and there is no extension provision**, which is why 63 and 65 behave as cliffs. Two essential qualifications: these provisions have been **formally suspended since 1 January 2017**, with the operative rules unpublished — so they are observed practice, not binding law; and **reaching the CMC is itself an age waiver**, since CMC members have served to 68 and possibly 70 (Qiao Qingchen and Ma Xiaotian both to 68; Miao Hua retained at 67). Zhang Youxia's retention at 72 in 2022 was an exception even by that standard. ([`methodology.md`](methodology.md) §3)
- **The congress clock.** CMCs are remade at party congresses: the 21st (~autumn 2027), 22nd (~2032), 23rd (~2037). The three horizons in this document — 2031, 2036, 2041 — each sit inside the term of one of those CMCs, so the question "who is very senior in 2031?" translates to "who wins seats at the 21st Congress (or in the interim appointments now underway), and survives?"
- **Pipelines — and a correction to the folklore.** The measured record of the 31 officers promoted to three stars between December 2019 and March 2024 shows that what is near-universal is the **grade sequence** (97% held a Corps Leader billet, 84% a Theater Command Deputy Leader billet), *not* any particular job. Strikingly, **only 26% ever commanded a group army** and only 48% ever served in one — so "group army command is the gateway to theater command," a claim this report's earlier draft leaned on, is at best half true. Only **6%** passed through the Joint Staff Department, whose deputy-chief billets were quietly downgraded a grade between 2018 and 2019, destroying the old lateral route to service command. Mean age at third star: **59.3**. And the two-theater-deputy-tour norm has decayed to a coin flip. **Model the grade ladder, not the billet folklore.** ([`methodology.md`](methodology.md) §2)
- **The clock has compressed.** The historic interval from second star to third was roughly six years (three in a theater-deputy billet, three more). It is now **2.0–2.5 years**. Any model still assuming the old pace will systematically under-predict young risers — precisely the error that would cause an analyst to dismiss Zhang Zheng or Zhu Chuansheng today.
- **Feeder posts with outsized hit rates**: Xinjiang and Tibet Military District commanders; theater chiefs of staff; the carrier programme and nuclear-submarine flotillas; theater air-force and navy component commands; and, for the political track, service and theater discipline-inspection secretaries.

### 3.2 The Xi-era distortions (post-2023 rules of thumb)

1. **Loyalty screens dominate.** Since mid-2023, every senior appointment has functioned first as a counter-intelligence judgment. The observable proxies for "trusted": discipline-system lineage; service overlap with Xi's build-out institutions (the parade commands, the JOCC); *absence* of ties to the purged networks; and — increasingly rare — personal history with Xi (which, after Zhong Shaojun and Zhang Youxia, is no longer protective either).
2. **Cross-service and cross-track transplants are the norm for sensitive seats.** A navy admiral ran the Rocket Force (badly, it turned out); an air-force discipline secretary is political commissar of the Army; air-force generals run two theaters; the defense minister is a sailor-diplomat. Projections should therefore *not* assume service lanes: the question for any vacancy is "who is clean, at grade, and adjacent," not "whose turn is it."
3. **Purges create promotion velocity.** Every wave vaporizes a cohort and drags the next one up early: Han Shengyan jumped from theater-air-force commander to theater commander and full general within months; Zhang Zheng went from staff work to Navy chief of staff as the incumbent fell. For the 2036/2041 horizons this cuts both ways — today's colonels will reach two-star billets years faster than doctrine anticipated, and will be destroyed just as summarily if their patrons stumble.
4. **The office no longer protects the man, and the man no longer protects the office.** Between 2012 and 2022 an officer who reached the CMC could expect to retire with honor. That equilibrium is dead. Analytically, this means *seat-based* projection (who holds which chair in 2031) must be paired with *attrition modeling* — and it means the corruption question in §6 is not a separate topic but the same topic: the purge is now the principal mechanism of elite circulation in the PLA.

### 3.3 Age math for the three horizons

The eligibility windows (using CMC-entry ≤ ~65 at appointment, theater-leader ≤ ~63 at first appointment, with Xi-era elasticity of ±2 years):

|---|---|---|---|---|

### 3.4 The duration effect: why early promotion is a hazard, not a shield

The most counter-intuitive finding in this study comes from the historical record, and it should govern how §5's rankings are read.

Within the cohort Xi promoted after the Guo Boxiong / Xu Caihou purge, the eventual outcome was determined almost entirely by **how long the officer stayed exposed**:

- Officers who **left post by the end of 2018** — mostly late-career figures finishing out their service: **1 of 12 purged (8.3%)**
- Officers who **remained in senior post into the 2020s**: **13 of 16 purged (81.3%)**

Being promoted in the aftermath of a purge was not itself dangerous. **Still being in the promoted generation when the next wave arrived was.** [`base-rates.md`](base-rates.md) §3.1.

The current cycle supplies an even sharper version of the same finding. CASI's July 2026 study reports that **of the 35 two- and three-star officers Xi personally promoted since 2020, 32 were probably investigated and 29 likely removed.** On that record, elevation by Xi is not protection — it is closer to a leading indicator.

Three consequences follow, and they cut against the natural reading of this report:

1. **Prominence in 2026 is a hazard rate, not a shield.** Zhang Zheng, Zhu Chuansheng, Wang Gang, Zhang Shuguang, Yang Zhibin and Han Shengyan have not survived anything yet — they have just *entered* the exposure window. The 50% figure above was only measurable because eight years elapsed; this cohort is roughly one year in. Every ranking in §5 is severely right-censored, and the reader should treat "most promising" as "most exposed" with equal seriousness.
2. **The officers running the PLA in 2041 may be those who are invisible in 2027.** If exposure duration drives attrition, the survivors of a long purge cycle are disproportionately those who spent it at a level too junior to be worth destroying. This is an argument for taking §5.3's cohort-level treatment of the 2041 horizon more seriously than any named prediction — and a caution against assuming today's anointed compound their advantage.
3. **Cross-service transplantation confers no immunity.** The cleanest test in the record: in July 2023 the Rocket Force's leadership was replaced wholesale by outsiders — Wang Houbin from the Navy, Xu Xisheng from the Air Force — *specifically because* they came from outside the compromised missile corps. Both were gone within 27 months. Being an unaffiliated outsider is what gets an officer promoted in this system; it is not what keeps them there.

### 3.5 Tradecraft: how to read a military that has stopped announcing things

Since roughly 2021 the PLA has stopped publishing lieutenant-general and major-general promotion lists, and since 2023 it has stopped announcing most senior removals at the time they happen. Personnel analysis has therefore migrated from *reading announcements* to *reading protocol*. The methods below are what this report actually relies on; naming them makes its evidence auditable.

1. **Seating and protocol order.** The PLA is rigid about precedence, and its television coverage is composed accordingly. This is now the single most productive instrument. The clearest worked example in the current record is CMSI's identification of Zhang Zheng as the Navy's de facto chief: at the **February 2026 Spring Festival greeting broadcast** he occupied the seat where the Navy commander normally sits, and in a **12 January 2026 Xinwen Lianbo** segment he appeared seated between the Army and Air Force chiefs of staff — placing him, by protocol arithmetic, as a service chief of staff at vice-admiral grade [REPORTED: CMSI Note 23, Shaw/Erickson/Sharman, 29 Jul 2026]. No announcement was ever made; the seating chart was the announcement.
2. **Ceremonial role assignment.** Who commands a parade, reads a promotion order, or presides at a ceremony encodes standing. Two examples carry real weight here: **Han Shengyan commanded the 3 September 2025 V-J Day parade as a lieutenant general** while the theater commander who normally holds that role (Wang Qiang) was still nominally in post — an unmistakable signal months before Wang's quiet replacement; and **Zhang Youxia read the promotion orders on 22 December 2025**, one month before his own investigation was announced, which is why this report treats "still performing protocol duties" as weak evidence of safety.
3. **Absence tracking.** Systematic comparison of who attends set-piece events — the Fourth Plenum, the Army Day reception, the Two Sessions delegation, a carrier commissioning — against who should. Absence is the leading indicator that [`forecasting-model.md`](forecasting-model.md) §1 calibrates. It is also the method most prone to error, per the caution in §2.2.
4. **Roster archaeology.** Chinese-language leadership trackers record tenure end-dates inferred from protocol coverage, often months before any announcement. Their value is high and their reliability is uneven — the Rocket Force page, for instance, still lists as serving deputy commanders two officers (Li Chuanguang, Liu Guangbin) who were removed or reported detained years ago. Treat a tenure end-date as a lead, not a fact.
5. **Institutional cross-reference.** Some PLA officers surface reliably in *civilian* records: provincial military district commanders and political commissars sit on provincial party standing committees; officers hold NPC and CPPCC seats whose termination is gazetted; defence-industry executives appear in SOE and listed-subsidiary filings. These civilian paper trails are frequently the first public confirmation of a military fall.
6. **Negative space.** What is *not* said carries information: a promotion ceremony that elevates two officers when a dozen billets are vacant; an expulsion announcement that omits monetary language; a successor gazetted with no word about the predecessor. The charge-language decoder in [`corruption-analysis.md`](corruption-analysis.md) §3 formalizes this.

**The systematic bias this creates.** All six methods detect *visible senior officers at ceremonial events*. They are close to blind below theater grade, and they systematically over-detect drama (a purge produces signals; an ordinary retirement produces the same silence as a detention). Both biases run in the same direction — toward a picture that is top-heavy and purge-weighted. This report inherits that bias, and §8 says so plainly.

## 5. Projections: 2031, 2036, 2041

**How to read the confidence bands.** *High* = better-than-even; *Moderate* = 25–50%; *Low* = 10–25%; *Speculative* = <10% but named because the seat logic points there. These are joint probabilities of (a) the projection logic being right AND (b) the officer surviving politically.

The survival term is now grounded in measured history rather than impression. From [`base-rates.md`](base-rates.md): **50% of the officers Xi promoted into the vacancies created by the last great purge — the Guo Boxiong / Xu Caihou wave of 2012–18 — were themselves subsequently purged** (14 of 28), rising to **72.7%** for the tightest analogue, the August 2017 cohort that stepped directly into the purged men's chairs. The lifetime purge rate for uniformed CMC members rose from **9.1% under Jiang and Hu to 66.7% under Xi.** Accordingly, **no individual five-year forecast in today's PLA deserves better than ~55% survival-adjusted confidence, and no ten-year forecast better than ~35%.** The seat-based projections — what *kind* of officer holds which chair — are far more robust than any name attached to them, and the reader should weight them accordingly.

### 5.3 Fifteen years out — 2041 (the 23rd Congress, and the succession overhang)

Individual-level projection to 2041 from open sources is **nearly impossible**, and it is worth being precise about why — the reason is measurable, not merely a shrug.

### The cliff at 1973

A systematic sweep of the Chinese-language open record by birth year ([`succession-pipeline.md`](succession-pipeline.md) §1) produces this distribution of PLA-affiliated people known to hold a **corps-grade or higher** billet:

> 1966: 8 · 1967: 5 · 1968: 6 · 1969: 3 · 1970: 3 · 1971: 1 · **1972: 1** · 1973: 1 · **1974–1978: 0** · 1979: 1 · 1980: 0

**Below 1973 the open record contains essentially nobody in a command billet above brigade.** And the distribution is probably *real* rather than an artefact: the record does contain 1972–73-born senior colonels in military sub-district and garrison deputy posts, which is roughly where that cohort should be. **Working inference: the officers who will hold theater commands in the late 2030s are today brigade commanders and division deputies at senior-colonel rank — a grade at which the PLA has never published systematically, purge or no purge.**

### The one name on the leading edge

**Gu Zhong** (顾中, b. 1972) — **commander of the 72nd Group Army** since December 2022, promoted major general in June 2019 at about 47, previously deputy chief of staff of the Eastern Theater Command. **He is the only officer born 1972 or later confirmed in a corps-leader command billet anywhere in the PLA**, and the 72nd — garrisoned at Huzhou in Zhejiang — is the Eastern Theater's principal amphibious-capable formation, the corps that would carry a landing. If one name belongs in a 2041 projection, it is his.

Behind him the visible field thins to officers the PLA chose to publicise rather than officers it chose to promote: **Man Guangzhi** (b. 1974, commander of the Zhurihe opposing-force brigade), **Chen Xiaoqian** (b. 1975, vice president of the Academy of Military Sciences and a Chinese Academy of Sciences academician — the technical-education route being the fastest available to this generation), **Wu Qian** (b. 1973, the youngest confirmed major general, but on a political-work and foreign-affairs track), **Qi Fabao** (b. 1979, the Galwan Valley hero now commanding the Urumqi Garrison), **Wei Huixiao** (b. 1977, first woman to command a PLAN principal surface combatant), and **Chen Zhiguo**, captain of the *Fujian*. That is a list of the famous, not of the powerful — and the distinction is the finding.

### And a negative case that disciplines all of the above

**Huo Jiangang** (霍建刚, b. October 1970) commanded the 79th Group Army, ran the Joint Operations College, and was made a **lieutenant general and deputy commander of the Central Theater Command in June 2022** — on track to be the PLA's youngest theater commander. **He was demoted in 2023.** It is the only documented demotion in the dataset, and it establishes that the purge reaches the 1970-born cohort and does not always take the form of disappearance. Succession modelling that assumes attrition happens only at the top is wrong.

### The governing variable is political, not biographical

Xi Jinping turns 88 in 2041, and the succession mechanics matter more than any résumé. Two precedents establish that **the CMC chairmanship is the last office transferred**: Deng Xiaoping held it until 1989, past his other posts; Jiang Zemin held it until 2004, two years past the general secretaryship. Joseph Torigian's comparative work supplies the reason — authoritarian succession is settled by the military and security apparatus, which is precisely why its command is surrendered last.

The arithmetic that follows lands squarely inside this report's window:

- a general-secretary handover at the **22nd Congress (~2032)** implies CMC transfer around **2034–2036**;
- a handover at the **23rd Congress (~2037)** implies CMC transfer around **2039–2041**.

**And there is a concrete leading indicator.** Under the Deng-era template an heir is seated as **CMC vice-chairman one Congress before** taking over — yet **no civilian has sat on the CMC since 2012**. So: *if no civilian is named to the CMC at the 21st Party Congress in autumn 2027, a 2032 general-secretary handover becomes structurally difficult and 2037 becomes the earlier realistic date. A civilian CMC appointment in 2027 is the strongest available signal that a 2032 transition is being prepared.* This is now prediction #23 in [`forecasting-model.md`](forecasting-model.md).

### Three scenarios

- **Scenario A — Continuity gerontocracy (Xi still chairman or paramount).** The enforcer template compounds; operators are technically superb but politically sterile; the Rocket Force and procurement complex remain under permanent receivership. The safest bets are the youngest of today's anointed — Chen Zhiguo, Gu Zhong, Dai Mingmeng as the elder — plus whoever emerged from the Rocket Force rebuild and the first ISF-native commander. **Weight: ~35%.**
- **Scenario B — Managed succession (an heir named 2027–2032, consolidating by 2035–37).** The 2041 leadership is selected for loyalty *to the succession settlement*: expect over-representation of officers who ran the People's Armed Police, the joint operations centre, the Beijing/Central theater and the discipline organs during the transition — the Peng Jingtang, Liu Zhe and Zhang Shaoying profiles, one generation down. Officers too personally identified with Xi's inner court become liabilities to the new order; **the Zhong Shaojun precedent is the warning**. **Weight: ~40%.**
- **Scenario C — Rupture (succession crisis, elite conflict, or a Taiwan war in the window).** Crisis performance re-sorts everything, and it is historically the one mechanism that produces genuinely authoritative PLA leaders. No names are projectable; the *institutions* that would produce them are the Eastern Theater joint staff, the carrier strike groups, the missile bases and the targeting complex. **Weight: ~25%.**

**A risk the literature under-argues, and which cuts across all three:** a successor inherits a PLA conditioned to loyalty **to Xi personally**, with no institutional habit of loyalty to the office. Wuthnow's expectation that post-Xi risk falls — because a successor lacks Xi's authority — assumes an orderly handover. A contested one inverts it entirely. See [`strategic-implications.md`](strategic-implications.md) §5.

### Cohort-level claims that hold across scenarios

Higher confidence than any name:

1. The 2041 CMC will contain **no officer with pre-reform (pre-2016) general-officer experience**, and probably nobody who was even a colonel before 2010.
2. **Naval and aerospace/information officers will hold the plurality.** The ground force's feeder cohort was disproportionately destroyed in 2023–26 and its headquarters has been leaderless for years; its institutional power may not recover to pre-purge levels.
3. At least one 2041 CMC member is today a **brigade commander, missile-base deputy or satellite-launch-centre director whose name has never appeared in Western analysis** — the senior-colonel layer the §1 sweep shows to be empty of published names.
4. The discipline apparatus will have produced either the chairman's closest uniformed ally (Scenarios A and B) or the most resented institution in the building (Scenario C).
5. **The generation now being commissioned into the rebuild reaches theater command in exactly this window** — and both Wuthnow and Brookings expect it to be more technically confident and less institutionally able to say no.

## 6. The corruption question

> **Read §0 again before reading this section.** What follows moves from documented fact (6.1), to structural analysis (6.2), to **clearly-labeled speculation** (6.3–6.4). For officers not named in official announcements, the ratings below are estimates of *structural exposure* and *political risk* — **not claims that any named individual has done anything wrong.** In the PLA of 2026, being purged is weak evidence of corruption and survival is weak evidence of innocence; that asymmetry is analyzed, not assumed away.

### 6.1 What the record shows

The complete documented ledger is in [`purge-tracker.md`](purge-tracker.md). The shape of it:

- **Where the money was, the purge went first.** The 2023–24 waves concentrated almost entirely on two systems: the **Rocket Force** (every commander it has ever had; deputies; the equipment chief; base commanders; its own discipline secretary) and the **armaments/procurement chain** (*every* EDD director in the department's history — Zhang Youxia, Li Shangfu, Xu Xueqiang — plus deputies Zhang Yulin and Rao Wenmin, and the chairmen of CASC and Norinco and a CASIC deputy GM). These are precisely the systems that absorbed the largest concentrated capital expenditure in PLA history: the silo-field build-out and the equipment-modernization surge. In May 2026, Wei Fenghe and Li Shangfu received **suspended death sentences** [REPORTED: RFA] — the heaviest sanctions against Chinese military leaders since the Cultural Revolution era, with official charging language citing bribery in "extraordinarily large" amounts, and (for Li) both taking and paying bribes — the paying half being the promotion-market tell.
- **Then the promotion market.** The 2024–25 wave decapitated the **Political Work Department twice** (Miao Hua; his executive deputy He Hongjun) and swept the service political commissars promoted on Miao's watch (Qin Shutong, Yuan Huazhi, Guo Puxiao, Li Wei, plus retired PCs Yu Zhongfu and Qin Shengxiang). The PWD ran cadre selection: if senior rank was being sold — as it demonstrably was under Guo Boxiong and Xu Caihou a decade earlier — the PWD was the marketplace. Zhong Shaojun's fall reportedly centers on general-officer selection [UNVERIFIED motive; OFFICIAL removal], which would put the market's other counter inside Xi's own office.
- **Then logistics and construction.** JLSF commander and PC; the Logistic Support Department director (Zhang Lin) and a deputy (He Song); Ding Laihang's case reportedly tied to an airport-construction project; and — days ago — the 24 Aug 2026 public solicitation for tips on **Rocket Force procurement across weapons, logistics, food, and medical categories** [REPORTED], which reads as the opening of another round.
- **One tell deserves separate notice.** When the Equipment Development Department opened its own tip-line in July 2023, it solicited reports on violations **back to October 2017** — the month Li Shangfu took the department over, and therefore a boundary that implicated him while excluding his predecessor, **Zhang Youxia**, who had run the armaments system from 2012. The scoping was a political act. **It did not hold:** Zhang Youxia was under investigation by January 2026 and stripped by August. For a report about who survives, that is the most instructive fact in the record — a boundary drawn around the leader's closest military ally lasted about thirty months.
- **Then everyone.** The 2025–26 waves (Fujian/31st GA network in October; Zhang Youxia's staff/armaments network in June; the two CMC principals themselves in January) carry charge language that has drifted from money to politics: "betrayed the trust of the party," "severely trampled on the CMC Chairman responsibility system" [OFFICIAL/REPORTED]. Whatever Zhang Youxia and Liu Zhenli did, the announced offense is *disloyalty*, with corruption as the procedural wrapper.

Scale, for calibration: 100+ senior officers removed since 2022; ~52% of the PLA's ~176 top billets touched; PLA NPC delegates down 44 seats in three years; five of six uniformed CMC members destroyed [REPORTED: CSIS; roster tallies].

### 6.2 Why it is structural

Four features make graft endemic rather than incidental, and they tell you where exposure concentrates:

1. **Monopsony procurement under secrecy.** One buyer (the EDD and service equipment departments), classified budgets, no external audit, prices negotiated with a handful of state primes. Three features make weapons the ideal target: abundant funding under monopoly; **strategic systems that are almost never used, so defects are never discovered** (the DF-41 has been tested roughly 7–10 times since 2012 — a deterrent works by *perceived* readiness, which is exactly what makes it safe to sell defectively); and a quality gate consisting of a single **军代表** embedded inside the manufacturer, whom China's own *Legal Daily* warned in 2012 was being paid off. The 2016 reform *centralised* procurement in the EDD and thereby centralised the rents — hence all three of its directors destroyed.
2. **The promotion market.** Officer appointment in the PLA is *political work*, administered by the political-officer chain: the Political Work Department's Cadre Bureau manages promotions, and until 2016 the military's disciplinary organ sat **inside that same department**. Xu Caihou ran the discipline commission (2000–02) and then the General Political Department (2002–04) in sequence. Where promotion is purchasable, **every officer who rose through the paying years carries paper** — the base-rate problem that makes "who is corrupt" partly a category error.
3. **And they are the same market.** This is the finding that reorganises the whole picture. Li Shangfu was charged with taking bribes **and giving them**, plus seeking "personnel benefits **for himself**." The only market in which the PLA's monopsonist buyer is himself a payer is his own career: he bought his advancement with money extracted from procurement. The same both-directions pattern was charged against Fang Fenghui, Zhang Yang and Gu Junshan. **Rents enter at the contract and exit at the appointment** — which is why purging either end alone has never worked, and why the campaign moved upstream from the Rocket Force and the EDD in 2023–24 to the political-work system in 2024–25. ([`corruption-analysis.md`](corruption-analysis.md) §2A.1)
4. **Construction and land.** Silo fields, base hardening, the JLSF's hospitals-depots-property empire, garrison real estate in tier-1 cities.
5. **Consumables.** Food, fuel, and medical procurement — low-glamour, high-volume, weakly supervised, and named explicitly in the August 2026 Rocket Force solicitation.

### 6.4 So: who might actually be corrupt? (the direct answer, with epistemics attached)

- **In the aggregate:** the officers most likely to have engaged in material corruption, on the structure of the system, are those who held **procurement, construction, logistics, or cadre-selection authority between roughly 2012 and 2023** — which is to say, a substantial share of two entire cohorts, including many already purged and some of the honored dead. The purge record is best read not as a list of the guilty but as the intersection of (probably guilty) ∩ (politically expendable) ∩ (network-affiliated with a fallen patron).
- **Named, forward-looking, speculative:** the highest-probability *future* corruption announcements among currently-standing officers are (1) a further **Rocket Force procurement cluster** below Lei Kai — the August 2026 tip-solicitation all but promises it, and a nine-year retrospective audit in September 2025 had already stripped some 74 bid-evaluation experts and 116 suppliers; (2) the **logistics chain's next layer** (JLSF and Logistic Support Department deputies, regional joint logistics centres); (3) one or more of the **Tier 1 vanished** being formally charged at an upcoming NPCSC session, most likely late October or December 2026; and (4) **further aviation-sector falls**, which the evidence now suggests is the live epicentre rather than the navy — see the correction immediately below.

- **A prediction this report is revising against its own evidence.** An earlier draft named **Wang Hongli** and the naval equipment chain as the likeliest next target, on the structural logic that the shipbuilding boom is the unaudited capital surge. That logic still holds, but the current indicators point the other way, and [`defense-industrial-base.md`](defense-industrial-base.md) §5 sets out why: the tip-solicitation notices have gone **Equipment Development Department-wide (Jul 2023) → Air Force (Dec 2025) → Rocket Force (Aug 2026), with no Navy notice**; CSSC's chairman is stable and visible; and shipbuilding was the *outperforming* subsector in H1 2026 while both AVIC combat-aircraft primes contracted sharply. **The epicentre right now is aviation.** The naval prediction is retained but downgraded, with an explicit falsification test: *a notice soliciting tips on Navy procurement violations would confirm it; its continued absence through 2027 against stable CSSC leadership and rising shipbuilding revenue should be treated as disconfirming.* Wang Hongli's structural exposure is unchanged; the timing claim was over-confident.
- **And the inversion, stated plainly:** some fraction of the men listed in the purge tracker are guilty of nothing but the wrong patron, and some fraction of the men this document ranks as "most promising" are carrying liabilities that will surface the moment they stop being useful. The PLA's own institutions cannot currently distinguish these cases; outside analysis certainly cannot. What analysis *can* say is that **the purge is no longer a corruption campaign with political side effects — it is a loyalty-reconstruction campaign that uses corruption as its legal instrument**, and it will keep "finding" corruption wherever trust fails, because in this system it is always findable.


### 6.5 Why fourteen years of enforcement has not worked

The obvious objection to everything above is that China has been running the largest anti-corruption campaign in its modern history since 2012, and the case count is *rising*. Either the campaign is failing, or — as the Party now argues explicitly — it is succeeding and the rising count measures how deep the investigators have got. The full argument is in [`why-purges-recur.md`](why-purges-recur.md); five findings from it bear directly on the projections.

**1. The enforcement function and the corruption function are drawn from the same pool.** This is the recursion the record keeps producing. **Xu Caihou ran the CMC Discipline Inspection Commission (2000–02) before he ran the promotion market. Zhang Yang chaired the all-army group for eliminating Guo–Xu influence while being, on the state's own account, part of it. Wang Houbin was installed in July 2023 specifically to clean the Rocket Force from outside, and was expelled from it twenty-seven months later. Zhang Youxia ran the armaments system before the man he is now accused of promoting.** The 2016–17 reform raised the CMC DIC out from under the Political Work Department and gave it CMC-member grade — a real change — but it did not create an external monitor. Phillip Saunders' formulation is the cleanest: Xi "created new monitoring mechanisms," yet "the reformed system still relied on PLA officers to monitor their peers." James Char's is the sharpest: **"the ability to vet and control appointments is no substitute for overseeing how those appointees conduct themselves."**

**2. The auditors were right, five years early, and it changed nothing.** This is the most underweighted fact in the open literature. Researchers *at the CMC Audit Office* published an open-literature diagnosis in the *Journal of Naval University of Engineering* in **2018** correctly identifying 围标/串标 (bid collusion and rigging) and merchant–officer collusion as the two principal failure modes of equipment acquisition — five years before the 2023 purge proved them right. The problem was never diagnosis. **The CMC Audit Office is headed by a major general and is expected to audit CMC vice chairmen, theater commanders and department directors three to four grades above it.** Xi elevated the discipline organ in 2017; he did not elevate the audit organ. And the audit function was itself for sale: SASTIND's former Military Project Audit Centre director, Zhang Jianhua, self-surrendered in May 2025 charged with 受贿 *and* 斡旋受贿 — influence-peddling bribery, the legal category for brokerage.

**3. The purge is not fiscal restraint, and the spending data settle it.** SIPRI's April 2026 report records that Chinese military expenditure **rose 7.4% in real terms in 2025 — the largest single-year increase in a decade — and that "the anti-corruption campaign did not appear to result in any new constraints on Chinese military spending in 2025."** The largest purge of PLA leadership since the Lin Biao affair coincided with the largest real spending increase in ten years. **This is a distributional fight over a growing pot, not a retrenchment**, and any projection that models the campaign as a brake on procurement is contradicted by the series.

**4. The rents follow the budget, which is why the epicentre moves.** The only official composition breakdown China has ever published — Table 2 of the 2019 defence white paper — shows the equipment share rising from 33.2% (2010) to **41.1% (2017)**, after which no update has ever been issued. The case record tracks it exactly: the 1998–2012 cases (Wang Shouye, Gu Junshan) are **logistics-and-land** cases, from the era when construction and real estate were the large discretionary flows; the 2023–26 cases are **equipment** cases. That is not a change in investigative fashion. It supplies the forward rule used in §6.4: *the next cluster forms wherever the marginal spending growth is now* — currently aviation and strategic systems, not shipbuilding.

**5. Installing a clean outsider is not a fix, and there is now a controlled experiment.** Elliot Ji argued that parachuting in loyal outsiders without sectoral expertise "risks repeating the cycle that produced the corruption initially." Wang Houbin is that prediction, tested: a Navy officer moved into the Rocket Force commander's seat precisely because no insider was trusted, purged in twenty-seven months. **Every acting officer named in §2.2 of this report is in the structural position Wang Houbin occupied in August 2023.** That is the single most relevant data point for how much confidence the caretaker cohort deserves — and it argues for less, not more.

**What this does to the forecast.** It removes the main reason one might expect the purge rate to revert. There is no reform since 2012 that introduces an external monitor, no published audit, no published amount, and no verdict that could deter by publicity. On Char's formulation, purges continue "until the CCP is willing to contemplate a legal and judicial system no longer sanctioned by itself." **The base rates in [`base-rates.md`](base-rates.md) should therefore be applied forward at close to their measured values rather than discounted as a one-off crisis** — which is precisely why the confidence bands in §5 are as wide as they are.

## 7. What would change this assessment (indicators to watch)

Because the projection rests on a still-unfolding purge, it is unusually falsifiable. Watch for:

- **The next NPCSC session (~Oct/Dec 2026, roughly bimonthly).** Each session since 2023 has stripped PLA delegates. Formal charges against any of the §6.3 Tier-1 vanished (especially Hu Zhongming, Chang Dingqiu) would confirm the "disappearance precedes announcement" model. Watch also whether *new* names appear from the operational-riser cohort — that would break the "operators are safe" assumption this document leans on.
- **The Fifth Plenum (~autumn 2026) and any CMC additions.** If Dong Jun and/or Zhang Shuguang are seated on the CMC, the rebuild is underway on schedule; if the commission stays at two, Xi is choosing to rule through a skeleton CMC into the 21st Congress — a more unstable equilibrium.
- **Permanent fills of the acting jobs.** A gazetted Rocket Force commander (Lei Kai or an outsider), a named Army commander, a full PWD director, a promoted-to-admiral Zhang Zheng — each resolves a specific projection in §5.
- **Wang Hongli's status.** The Navy Equipment Department director is this framework's cleanest out-of-sample corruption test: if the shipbuilding boom gets the Rocket Force treatment, he is exposed; his survival would suggest the naval equipment chain is being treated differently.
- **Whether any born-1970s officer is promoted to full general.** As of August 2026 the youngest full generals are born 1963–65. The first born-1970 三星 (Zhang Zheng, Zhu Chuansheng, Dai Mingmeng are the candidates) marks the formal start of the generational transition.
- **Whether a civilian is seated on the CMC at the 21st Congress.** The single best succession indicator available: under the Deng-era template an heir becomes CMC vice-chairman one Congress before taking over, and no civilian has sat on the commission since 2012. A civilian appointment in autumn 2027 signals a 2032 handover; its absence pushes the realistic date to 2037 (§5.3).
- **Protocol order in PLA listings.** When a deputy moves to the top of the protocol order among his peers, he is being groomed and those below him will most likely retire. It is the cheapest high-value indicator in open sources, and it is what identified Zhang Zheng ([`methodology.md`](methodology.md) §4).
- **The March 2027 defence budget, and whether SIPRI's finding holds.** The 2025 increase was 7.4% real — the largest in a decade — with SIPRI finding no evidence that the purge constrained spending. If the 2026–27 figures repeat that, the campaign is confirmed as distributional rather than fiscal, and procurement graft exposure stays high in a growing-budget environment. A visible deceleration would be the first evidence the purge has begun to cost money rather than redistribute it ([`why-purges-recur.md`](why-purges-recur.md) §4.3).
- **Whether the CMC Audit Office is elevated in grade.** It is headed by a major general and tasked with auditing officers three to four grades above it. Xi raised the CMC Discipline Inspection Commission to CMC-member grade in 2017 and left the audit organ where it was. **An elevation — or the first publication of any aggregate audit result — would be the single clearest signal that the leadership has diagnosed the recurrence problem correctly** rather than treating it as a loyalty problem. Its continued absence is evidence for the reading in §6.5.
- **Whether the nine expelled in October 2025 receive published verdicts.** If detailed financial judgments appear, those cases were genuinely about money; continued silence past mid-2027 is evidence they were not ([`corruption-analysis.md`](corruption-analysis.md) §3).
- **Gu Zhong's next posting.** The only officer born after 1971 in a corps-leader command billet. Elevation to deputy-theater grade would confirm the 1972 cohort has begun to move.
- **Signs of a succession signal at the 21st Congress (2027).** An heir-apparent's identity would reweight the entire 2036/2041 analysis toward §5.3 Scenario B.
- **A Taiwan or other crisis in the window.** Wartime performance is the one historical mechanism that produces genuinely authoritative PLA leaders and would override every résumé-based projection here (Scenario C).

## 8. Methodology, confidence, and sources

**Method.** This assessment was built from public sources only, in three layers. First, a set of directly-verified anchor facts — the current CMC composition, the 2024–26 promotion ceremonies, all five theater-command rosters and the four service rosters, and the purge chronology — each checked against a primary source. Second, a fanned-out open-source sweep across the ground force, navy, air and aerospace forces, rocket and strategic forces, and the political/CMC/PAP apparatus. Third, a deeper pass that produced the companion files: the professional analytical framework and its track record ([`methodology.md`](methodology.md)), empirical purge base rates back to 1992 ([`base-rates.md`](base-rates.md)), a birth-year sweep of the junior cohort ([`succession-pipeline.md`](succession-pipeline.md)), the readiness and succession literature ([`strategic-implications.md`](strategic-implications.md)), the civilian defence-industrial twin of the purge ([`defense-industrial-base.md`](defense-industrial-base.md)), the adjudicated case record ([`corruption-casebook.md`](corruption-casebook.md)), and the structural and quantitative literature on why enforcement keeps failing ([`why-purges-recur.md`](why-purges-recur.md)).

Every load-bearing fact carries a confidence tag ([OFFICIAL] / [REPORTED] / [WIKI] / [INFERRED] / [RUMOR]); conflicts between sources are flagged in place rather than resolved silently. For officer status the report follows CSIS's three-tier standard — *officially purged*, *potentially purged*, *missing* — because conflating those tiers is the most common error in reporting on this subject.

**Corrections made during the work, recorded rather than quietly applied.** Several claims in earlier drafts did not survive checking: Dai Mingmeng is a theater-navy deputy commander, not a fleet commander; Zhu Chuansheng is an Army lieutenant general, not a vice admiral; Chen Zhiguo's birth year is not in the primary record; Chen Hui's disappearance rests on a roster page and one outlet while his own biography lists him as incumbent; the Equipment Development Department has lost *all three* of its directors, not two; "group army command is the gateway to theater command" is contradicted by the measured record; and the report's network language has been qualified against the quantitative evidence that patronage networks have *declined* under Xi. Each is noted where it appears.

**Principal sources.** *Official:* Ministry of National Defense, Xinhua, PLA Daily/81.cn, NPC Standing Committee gazettes, CPPCC Standing Committee decisions, CCDI and military-court releases, gov.cn; the PRC Law on Officers in Active Service and the NPCSC Decision of December 2016; and the US Department of Defense's annual *Military and Security Developments Involving the PRC* (2024 and 2025 editions). *Quantitative and documentary primaries for §6.5 and the corruption companions:* the 2019 defence white paper *China's National Defense in the New Era* (its Table 2 is the only official breakdown of defence-budget composition ever published, and it stops at 2017); and the *PLA Daily* / *People's Daily* editorials of 18 October 2025 and 25 January 2026, which carry the Party's own explanation of why the case count is rising. *Specialist analysis:* the China Aerospace Studies Institute at Air University — Kenneth Allen's work on grades, ranks and promotion analysis is the backbone of [`methodology.md`](methodology.md), and Margaret Kuntz's July 2026 study of PLA corruption supplied the turnover base rates; **Known limitations.**
1. **The record is moving under our feet.** Facts current on 31 August 2026 will decay; the whole point of §7 is that this is a snapshot of an active purge.
2. **Deliberate opacity below theater grade.** The PLA stopped publishing lieutenant-general/major-general promotion lists around 2021. The group-army, missile-base, and carrier-captain cohort that will supply the 2041 leadership is largely invisible to open sources — a genuine collection gap, not an omission, and the reason the 2041 projection is scenario-based rather than name-based.
3. **"Acting" arrangements are under-announced.** Much of §2's observed org chart rests on Chinese-language leadership trackers reading protocol coverage, not official appointment notices; several posts carry conflicting roster states, flagged as [WIKI-CONFLICT].
4. **Survivorship and disappearance are ambiguous.** An officer's absence from public view is strong pattern-evidence in this environment but not proof of investigation; several officers treated here as "vanished" could be ill, sidelined, or reassigned.
5. **Corruption exposure is estimated, not established.** §6 rates structural and political risk for officers *not* named in official announcements; those ratings are analytical judgments about position and network, explicitly not findings of misconduct.
6. **Chinese-language name and date reconciliation.** A handful of birth years, characters, and single-sourced appointments (Lei Kai, Zhou Jingjiong, Chen Chi, Zheng Yanpo, Wang Xianfeng, and others) could not be independently corroborated and are tagged accordingly.

**A note on why this is legitimate analysis.** Everything here concerns the publicly-reported activities of senior public officials of a major military power, of exactly the kind that government, academic, and think-tank China-watchers produce continuously. It relies only on open sources, distinguishes fact from inference from rumor throughout, and makes no claim of misconduct against any serving individual beyond what state organs have themselves announced.
