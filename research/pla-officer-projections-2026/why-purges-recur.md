# Why the purges keep coming — and what the numbers do and do not support

*Companion to [`corruption-casebook.md`](corruption-casebook.md) (the adjudicated record) and [`corruption-analysis.md`](corruption-analysis.md) (the inference framework). This file addresses the question those two raise but do not answer: fourteen years into the most sustained anti-corruption campaign in PLA history, why is the case count accelerating rather than falling — and can the scale of the problem be measured at all?*

**Prepared: 31 August 2026 · Confidence tags as in the main report.**

---

## 0. The two facts that frame the question

**Fact one: the Rocket Force was purged twice.** In July 2023 Commander Li Yuchao, Political Commissar Xu Zhongbo and several deputies were removed. On 31 July 2023 two outsiders — **Wang Houbin** from the Navy and **Xu Xisheng** from the Air Force — were installed precisely because no one inside the service was trusted. On **17 October 2025, Wang Houbin was expelled from the Party and the army.** The remedy became the disease in twenty-seven months.

**Fact two: the Equipment Development Department has never had untainted leadership.** **Zhang Youxia** (2012/16–2017), **Li Shangfu** (2017–2022) and **Xu Xueqiang** (2022–) — all three directors of the department that buys everything the PLA fields are now disgraced or under investigation; the second was sentenced to death with reprieve, the first was stripped of rank on 28 August 2026. The department's political commissar seat has been vacant since April 2019.

Add two more from the casebook. **Zhang Yang**, the Political Work Department director who chaired the all-army group for eliminating Guo–Xu influence, was himself a Guo–Xu case. **Xu Caihou** ran the CMC Discipline Inspection Commission from 2000 to 2002, before he ran the promotion market.

**The pattern stated plainly: in the PLA, the enforcement function and the corruption function are held by the same people — sequentially, and sometimes simultaneously.**

Everything below is an attempt to explain that sentence.

---

## 1. The principal–agent argument (the dominant explanation)

**[REPORTED] Phillip C. Saunders** (National Defense University), writing in the Lowy Institute's *The Interpreter*, states it most cleanly. Xi "relied on trusted allies for day-to-day supervision of PLA affairs," and discovered that **"the officers he trusted to monitor their peers had become complicit in their corruption."** After the 2016 reforms Xi "created new monitoring mechanisms to deter corruption" — yet **"the reformed system still relied on PLA officers to monitor their peers."** The failure is not marginal. Self-policing failed at its foundation.

**[REPORTED] James Char** supplies the institutional history behind that. Before the 19th Party Congress the CMC Discipline Inspection Commission sat **under the General Political Department** — "it was controlled by the very same personnel whom it was meant to supervise." Xi raised the CMC DIC to CMC-member grade after 2017 and put a Rocket Force officer, **Zhang Shengmin**, in charge. Char's verdict on the limit of that fix is the most quotable sentence in the literature:

> **"The ability to vet and control appointments is no substitute for overseeing how those appointees conduct themselves."**

And the founding datum, from the casebook: **[REPORTED — John Garnaut, *Foreign Policy*, April 2012]** Hu Jintao had to route around the PLA entirely and use the **civilian** CDIC, because the PLA's own disciplinary body had **ignored his direct orders** to investigate Gu Junshan. The agent did not merely fail to report. It refused a lawful instruction from the principal.

**The residual structural fact, which is the one that matters for projection.** The civilian CDIC still cannot penetrate the PLA. Char: "As a mostly insular institution, the PLA cannot be penetrated by the civilian CDIC. Instead, the onus for oversight falls on the armed wing's own mechanism." **The 2016–17 reform changed the *rank* of the internal watchdog. It did not create an external one.** Whatever else is true, no reform since 2012 has introduced a monitor drawn from outside the monitored population.

## 2. Audit capacity: real, competent, and structurally capped

This is the part of the story that is usually missed, and it is more interesting than "the PLA has no auditors."

**[OFFICIAL] The CMC Audit Office (中央军委审计署).**

- Established 1985 as the CMC Audit Bureau; 1992–2016 the **PLA Audit Office** (head titled 审计长); reorganised in 2016 as the CMC Audit Office.
- **The critical reform: in 2014 the CMC Chairman signed an order placing the audit organisation directly under CMC leadership, removing it from General Logistics Department administration.** Until 2014, the auditors of the PLA's money reported to the department that spent it — the department of Wang Shouye and Gu Junshan.
- Functions: **经济责任审计** (economic-responsibility audits) of leaders at regiment level and above, including **离任审计** (departure audits); budget review, credited with cutting ~RMB 4.5 billion of expenditure 2001–2004; and procurement and construction audits. Audit centres are maintained across all five theater commands.
- Heads: Li Qinghe (2009–2015), Guo Chunfu (2015–2018), Tian Yixiang (2018–2020), Sun Bin (2020– ). **Rank: Major General.**

**Two caps, both visible in the record.**

**Cap one — rank.** The CMC Audit Office is headed by a **major general**. It is expected to audit CMC vice chairmen, CMC members, theater commanders and department directors: three to four grades above it. Compare the CMC DIC, which Xi *did* elevate to CMC-member grade in 2017. **The audit organ was not similarly elevated.** In a system where grade determines who may compel whom, that is not a detail — it is the whole answer to why departure audits do not stop a vice chairman.

**Cap two — the findings do not travel.** The best evidence that the audit function is technically *competent* is that its own researchers, publishing openly in the *Journal of Naval University of Engineering* in 2018, correctly diagnosed **围标 (bid collusion) and 串标 (bid rigging)** and merchant–officer collusion as the two principal failure modes of equipment acquisition, and documented a communications-equipment purchase RMB 2 million over cost. **They identified the mechanism five years before the 2023 purge.**

That is the finding this file exists to make. **The problem was never diagnosis.** The PLA's auditors knew what was happening, wrote it down, and published it in a journal anyone could read. What did not exist was an enforcement pathway upward against officers senior to the auditors.

**Related — the audit function was itself compromised.** [OFFICIAL] **Zhang Jianhua**, former director of **SASTIND's Military Project Audit Centre** and then of its Finance and Audit Department, turned himself in on 28 May 2025 and is charged with **受贿 and 斡旋受贿** (bribery and influence-peddling bribery). The man who audited military projects was selling his influence over the officials who decided them.

**[EVIDENCE GAP]** The CMC Audit Office publishes **no audit reports, no annual results bulletin, and no aggregate recovery figures.** China's civilian National Audit Office publishes an annual work report to the NPC Standing Committee naming problem amounts. **There is no military equivalent in the public record.** This is a hard ceiling on every quantitative claim in §4 below.

## 3. Why a classified budget defeats oversight

Assembling the observed facts into a mechanism, in order of how binding each constraint is:

1. **One published number.** [OFFICIAL] Each March China releases **a single aggregate figure** for national defence expenditure. There is no programme-level appropriation, no line item, no project disclosure. **The NPC cannot conduct an appropriations review of data it does not have.**
2. **A composition breakdown published once, retrospectively, and never updated.** The 2019 defence white paper's Table 2 (§4.2) is the only official breakdown ever published. It stops at 2017. Even that is three aggregate categories.
3. **Weapons that are never used are never audited by use.** For strategic systems there is no operational feedback loop that would reveal that money did not become capability. [REPORTED] DoD's assessment that the missile-silo investigation "likely resulted in the PLARF repairing the silos" implies the defect existed for some period **while the force was counted as operational**. A fielded system that is never fired cannot fail an audit it never takes.
4. **The quality gate is a single embedded official.** The **军代表** (military representative) stationed inside the manufacturer is the point where specification meets acceptance. One person, one signature, and — as the casebook shows — a market price.
5. **No external auditor, no free press, no independent judiciary, no adversarial legislature.** Char: "Externally, there are neither effective countervailing checks and balances nor a free media in China to scrutinize the CCP and its praetorian guard."
6. **The criminal process is sealed.** No PLA verdict publishes an amount ([`corruption-casebook.md`](corruption-casebook.md) §4). **Deterrence by publicity — the mechanism the civilian campaign relies on — is unavailable**, because nobody outside the room can learn what the offence was.

**The net effect: the only signal that money did not become capability is a purge.** That is why purges are the observable, and why they arrive in waves rather than continuously. A system with continuous monitoring produces a steady trickle of small cases. A system whose only detector is a periodic political investigation produces exactly what the PLA produces: long silences punctuated by mass removals.

**This has a direct methodological consequence for the projections.** A purge wave is not a measurement of how much corruption occurred in the preceding period. It is a measurement of **how recently someone looked**. Analysts who read case volume as a corruption index are reading the detector, not the signal — a point developed in [`corruption-analysis.md`](corruption-analysis.md) §1.

## 4. What the numbers actually support

### 4.1 The announced budget

**[OFFICIAL]** Announced each March as a single aggregate:

| Year | Announced | ≈ USD | Nominal increase |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | RMB 1.55tn | ~US$224bn | +7.2% |
| 2024 | RMB 1.67tn | ~US$231bn | +7.2% |
| 2025 | RMB 1.78tn | ~US$246bn | +7.2% |
| **2026** | **~RMB 1.90tn** | **~US$277bn** | **+7.0%** |

*(Sources differ between RMB 1.90 and 1.91 trillion for 2026; "approximately RMB 1.9 trillion" is the safe form. Reuters/CNA, 5 March 2026.)*

### 4.2 The composition — and the "41% equipment share" that everyone cites

**[OFFICIAL]** The 2019 white paper *China's National Defense in the New Era* defines three categories: **人员生活费** (personnel), **训练维持费** (training and sustainment), and **装备费** (equipment — research, testing, procurement, maintenance, transport and storage).

Its Table 2, the composition of annual defence expenditure 2010–2017:

| Year | Personnel | Training & sustainment | **Equipment** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 34.9% | 31.9% | 33.2% |
| 2012 | 29.2% | 34.8% | 36.0% |
| 2014 | 28.6% | 32.3% | 39.1% |
| 2016 | 31.3% | 27.4% | **41.3%** |
| **2017** | **30.8%** | **28.1%** | **41.1%** |

Total defence expenditure rose from **RMB 669.192 billion (2012) to RMB 1,043.237 billion (2017)**.

**Three cautions, because this table is routinely misused.**

1. **It is the only official composition breakdown ever published, and it stops at 2017.** No subsequent white paper has updated it.
2. **The ubiquitous "~41% equipment share" is therefore a 2016–17 figure applied forward by assumption.** Applied to the 2026 budget it implies an equipment line of roughly **RMB 780 billion (~US$114 billion)**. That extrapolation should always be labelled as such: the equipment share was still *rising* through 2017, while personnel costs have since been affected by the 300,000-troop reduction and the expansion of the civilian-cadre system. The true 2026 share could plausibly be higher or lower.
3. **The trend is the analytically load-bearing part, not the level.** [REPORTED] Char: "'equipment expense' has apparently taken the lion's share of China's annual defense expenditure since Xi's time in charge."

**And here is why that trend matters more than the number.** **The rents migrated to where the money went.** The 1998–2012 cases — Wang Shouye, Gu Junshan — are logistics-and-land cases, from the era when construction and real estate were the large discretionary flows. The 2023–26 cases are equipment cases. That is not coincidence, and it is not a change in investigative fashion. **It tracks the budget.** Which supplies a forward-looking rule: *the next concentration of cases will be wherever the marginal spending growth is now*, and on the evidence in [`defense-industrial-base.md`](defense-industrial-base.md), that is aviation and the strategic-systems complex.

### 4.3 SIPRI — and the single most important quantitative fact in this assessment

**[OFFICIAL — SIPRI, *Trends in World Military Expenditure, 2025*, April 2026]**

| Rank | Country | 2025 ($bn) | Δ 2024–25 | Δ 2016–25 | % GDP | % world |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | United States | 954 | −7.5% | +11% | 3.1% | 33% |
| **2** | **China** | **[336]** | **+7.4%** | **+62%** | **[1.7%]** | **[12%]** |
| 3 | Russia | [190] | +5.9% | +96% | [7.5%] | [6.6%] |
| 4 | Germany | 114 | +24% | +118% | 2.3% | 3.9% |
| 5 | India | 92.1 | +8.9% | +39% | 2.3% | 3.2% |

World total 2025: **US$2,887 billion** (+2.9% real).

SIPRI's China narrative, verbatim:

> "China's military expenditure has increased each year for **31 consecutive years — the longest streak of any country in the SIPRI Military Expenditure Database**. It rose by 7.4 per cent in 2025 (**the largest year-on-year increase in the past decade**) and by 62 per cent between 2016 and 2025. The increase in 2025 came amid a renewed campaign against corruption in military procurement within the People's Liberation Army (PLA), which led to the dismissal of several senior military and arms industry officials. **However, the anti-corruption campaign did not appear to result in any new constraints on Chinese military spending in 2025.**"

**That last sentence should reframe how the campaign is read.** The largest purge of PLA leadership since the Lin Biao affair coincided with the **largest single-year real increase in Chinese military spending in a decade**.

The campaign is not a budgetary retrenchment. It is not a response to money running out, and it is not producing money running out. **It is a fight over the distribution of a still-growing pot.** Any analysis that treats the purge as fiscal discipline, or expects it to slow procurement, is contradicted by the spending series.

### 4.4 The gap between announced and actual

**[OFFICIAL — DoD, 2024 Report to Congress]**

> "A survey of multiple models of the PRC's defense budget estimates that Beijing spends **40% to 90% more than it announces** in its public defense budget, which equates to approximately **$330 billion–$450 billion in total defense spending for 2024**."

DoD also records: announced 2023 spending of $220bn ≈ **1.2% of GDP**; the official budget nearly doubled over eleven years, growing **~6% annually in real terms 2013–2023**; and the PRC "can support continued growth in defense spending for at least the next 5 to 10 years." **[REPORTED]** A 2024 PPP-adjusted study put China's 2024 total at **US$471 billion**.

Note what this does to §4.2: if 40–90% of spending is outside the announced figure, then the equipment share of the *announced* budget is a share of an unknown fraction of the whole. The extrapolated "RMB 780 billion equipment line" is a floor on a number whose ceiling is not established.

### 4.5 Can the scale of the graft be estimated? No — and here is exactly why

**[UNVERIFIED — flagged because it is widely quoted and should not be]** Char records in a footnote that "as much as half of the country's military coffers [were] believed to have been siphoned off by corrupt generals at the start of Xi's tenure," sourced to a Voice of America Chinese-language panel discussion of 4 July 2014. **This figure has no documentary basis. It should not be used as an estimate.** Cite it, if at all, only as an indicator of what China-watchers believed in 2014.

**What can honestly be said about scale:**

| Basis | Figure | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Land-transfer proceeds through Gu Junshan's office | **> RMB 30bn** cumulative | [OFFICIAL] — *the flow, not the theft* |
| Wang Shouye, adjudicated | **RMB 160m** | [OFFICIAL] verdict |
| Tan Ruisong, adjudicated | **RMB 613m** bribes + **RMB 89.93m** embezzled | [OFFICIAL] verdict |
| One audited over-payment, communications equipment | RMB 2m over cost | [REPORTED] |
| One mid-level order-steering bribe (Li Mingquan) | RMB 1.4m | [REPORTED] |
| Gu Junshan → Xu Caihou protection payment | RMB 40m | [OFFICIAL/REPORTED] |
| A contested promotion | RMB 10m bid, outbid at RMB 20m | [REPORTED] |
| Cash seized from Xu Caihou | > 1 tonne, 12 trucks | [REPORTED] |
| Cash seized from Wang Shouye | RMB 52m + US$2.5m + RMB 50m slush fund | [OFFICIAL] |
| **Aggregate PLA graft, any year** | **—** | **No credible public estimate exists** |

Any aggregate figure an analyst encounters is an extrapolation from these few points, across a budget whose composition has not been officially disclosed since 2017, whose true total is estimated at 40–90% above the published figure, and whose criminal cases do not publish amounts.

**The honest statement is that the scale of PLA graft is unmeasured and, on present disclosure practice, unmeasurable from open sources.** What *is* measurable is direction: SIPRI's observation that the purge did not constrain spending, and the migration of cases from logistics and land to equipment in step with the equipment share.

### 4.6 The scale of the purge itself

**[REPORTED — CSIS, 2026 assessment]**

- **Over 100 senior PLA officers purged since 2022.**
- Approximately **52% of PLA leadership positions** (of ~176 billets) affected; **38%** of theater-command deputy-leader positions.
- **At least 13 confirmed removals at theater-commander grade**; potentially as many as 38 officers "disappeared"; **all five theater commands** affected.
- Of PLA officers seated on the 20th Central Committee in 2022, **at least 16 removed — possibly as many as 37, or 84%**.
- The CMC has contracted from **six uniformed members in 2022 to one**.
- A **nine-month gap** with no Eastern Theater Commander (March–December 2025).

**[REPORTED — Jamestown, *China Brief*]** During Xi's third term, "at least 36 generals and lieutenant generals and 65 officers" removed, missing, or in disgrace. Jamestown's read: Xi's "constant demands for loyalty, coupled with the persistent purges that indicate those demands remain unheeded, suggest chinks in the supreme leader's armament."

**[REPORTED — Bonny Lin, CSIS] Readiness signal:** exercise response times degraded from **3–4 days in 2024 to 12–19 days in 2025**; the April 2025 "Strait Thunder" exercise showed "minimal connection between discrete training activities, thus falling short of complexity." **[REPORTED — Suyash Desai] Counter-signal:** exercise tempo on the India–China border "remained constant" through the purge. Both are in [`strategic-implications.md`](strategic-implications.md).

**[REPORTED] The historical benchmark that puts this in proportion:** before Xi, **the only serving PLA full general to fall in CCP history was Huang Yongsheng (1971), in the Lin Biao affair.** As of January 2026, more than twenty senior figures had fallen in the current wave, with command vacancies persistently unfilled.

## 5. The competing explanations

Five arguments, none mutually exclusive, ranked by how much of the record each accounts for.

**[REPORTED] Thomas Christensen — corruption is universal, therefore chargeable.** *"The entire PLA has long been corrupt, and anyone towards the top has used bribery, received bribes, and/or witnessed and tolerated bribery."* If true, enforcement is necessarily *discretionary* — which makes it political, whatever the charge sheet says. This is the single most important claim for how this report reads the purge record, and it is the foundation of the "purge is weak evidence of guilt" argument in [`corruption-analysis.md`](corruption-analysis.md) §1.

**[REPORTED] Rob Pierce (*War on the Rocks*, "The Inevitability of Chinese Military Purges") — the structural model.** Three interlocking forces:

- **Endemic corruption as an enforcement lever.** Because advancement runs on "bribery, graft, nepotism, and patronage networks" rather than merit, **every senior official is chargeable**. Corruption is therefore an *omnipresent enforcement lever* — always available, never exhausted.
- **The dictator's dilemma.** Xi cannot concede governance failure without undermining his own legitimacy, so purges redirect blame onto scapegoats. Because they never touch root causes, failures recur and **"blame migrates upward,"** eventually reaching the most trusted lieutenants. That is precisely the Zhang Youxia trajectory, and Pierce wrote it before Zhang fell.
- **Elite paranoia as rational.** Senior officers acquire "independent influence and loyal networks by virtue of their seniority and longevity," and in an opaque system an autocrat "can never be confident of the benign ambitions of his underlings."

Pierce also identifies a **second-order operational cost**: purges make senior officers risk-averse, producing "scripted exercises, inflated readiness reporting, and a lack of tolerance for unscripted initiative." **Corruption degrades capability; the cure for corruption degrades capability differently.**

**[REPORTED] Elliot Ji — corruption as system lubricant.** Given "backward compensation systems for mid-to-lower ranked officers," corruption functions as **"a necessary form of currency or lubricant to keep the system going."** His warning: "abruptly choking off the flow of dirty money without addressing the underlying issue may only further deflate morale and loyalty," and installing loyal outsiders without expertise "risks repeating the cycle that produced the corruption initially." *That is the Wang Houbin case, predicted.* Ji also names the cultural precondition — **和平病 ("peace disease")**: the belief that "there will never be a war; even if there is one, it won't be me to fight it," which removes the natural enforcement mechanism of expecting the weapons to be used.

**[REPORTED] Performance and loyalty readings.** **M. Taylor Fravel:** the depth of the purge suggests "dissatisfaction with the PLA leadership's **performance** is a major factor in Xi's calculus" beyond corruption as such. **John Culver:** Xi has studied CCP crises involving civil-military relations, especially 1989; the purges aim at "absolute subordination" of the PLA to the Party and to Xi personally. **Brian Hart:** Xi prioritises "loyalty to him and his vision over other attributes like professional experience." **Jonathan Czin & Allie Matthias:** Xi now faces "a significantly smaller pool of officers from which he can fill those positions" — among plausible CMC candidates, "none of these officers are members of the Central Committee, or even alternate members." *(That last observation is the bench-depth problem this report quantifies in [`succession-pipeline.md`](succession-pipeline.md).)*

**[REPORTED] James Char — the contrarian reading, which deserves weight.** The purges are evidence of Xi's *strength*, not weakness: "scores of senior officers have been caught in the anti-graft drive and **replaced at will** by the CMC chairman," and the continued absence of an heir-apparent shows he retains support. Char also disputes two popular claims — that the technocrat purges reflect distrust of Western-exposed officers ("difficult to prove"), and that Rocket Force leaders were removed for opposing a Taiwan operation ("no evidence has emerged"). His structural conclusion is nonetheless pessimistic: **"Until the CCP is willing to contemplate a legal and judicial system no longer sanctioned by itself, such personnel purges are likely to continue."**

**[REPORTED] Joel Wuthnow (NDU) — the generational reading, which bears directly on the 2036 projection.** The disruption is "catalytic": the new cohort will be "better educated, more technically literate," "a more confident generation" — but **may be less willing to resist Xi's optimism about war**, creating risk in the 2030s. Wuthnow and Saunders' broader finding ("More Red but Still Expert," *Journal of Contemporary China*, 2024) is that senior officers' professions of loyalty "are little more than acts of self-preservation" — which is exactly why loyalty screening cannot substitute for oversight, and why §1's principal–agent problem is not solvable by better vetting.

**[REPORTED] Kou Chien-wen (National Chengchi University) — two observations worth isolating.** On the December 2023 removals: the purged NPC deputies were overwhelmingly **technical officers concentrated in the Rocket Force and the equipment-procurement system**, evidence those two systems had "blown up." And in the NYT, May 2026, on Zhang Youxia: **"Even his most trusted, most relied-upon confidants have fallen one after another. Who else can he trust?"** Kou also notes Xi's use of **二心** ("a second heart" — divided loyalty), a term from classical Chinese statecraft warning rulers against treacherous generals.

## 6. The Party's own answer — and why it should be engaged rather than dismissed

The Party has now put its explanation on the record, and it is a better argument than it is usually given credit for.

**[OFFICIAL]** *PLA Daily* / *People's Daily* editorial on the Zhang Youxia and Liu Zhenli cases, **25 January 2026**:

> 冰冻三尺非一日之寒，除三尺之冰也非一日之功。**当前腐败问题集中查处不是越反越腐，而是越挖越深。**
>
> *"Three feet of ice does not form from a single day's cold, and removing three feet of ice is not the work of a single day. The current concentrated investigation of corruption problems is not a case of 'the more you fight it, the more corrupt things get' — it is a case of 'the deeper you dig, the more you find.'"*

That sentence is a direct rebuttal of the "campaign is failing" reading, and it is doing real analytical work: **it reframes an accelerating case count as a measure of investigative penetration rather than of institutional decay.** [OFFICIAL] Caixin republished the accompanying 1 February 2026 commentary, 《持续深化政治整训 纵深推进正风反腐》, under exactly that line.

**[OFFICIAL]** The 18 October 2025 editorial supplies the etiology: the current cases are 郭伯雄、徐才厚流毒的**发酵变异** — the "fermentation and mutation" of Guo and Xu's residual poison — and the campaign is "a continuation of the rectification since the 18th Party Congress." It also forecloses the immunity theory: **没有免罪"丹书铁券"，没有"铁帽子王"** ("there are no iron-clad pardons, no 'iron-cap princes'").

**[OFFICIAL]** Xi's own framing, at the **CMC Political Work Conference, 17–19 June 2024**: political construction of the army suffers from **深层次矛盾和问题** ("deep-seated contradictions and problems"), and the task is to **铲除腐败滋生的土壤和条件** ("eradicate the soil and conditions in which corruption breeds").

**[OFFICIAL] The remedial programme** is **政治整训** (political rectification), across five fronts — 整顿思想、**整顿用人**、整顿组织、整顿作风、整顿纪律 — with the standing formula 一体推进不敢腐、不能腐、不想腐 and 把权力关进制度笼子. Note the second front: **整顿用人**, rectifying *the use of personnel*. The Party is naming the promotion market.

**[OFFICIAL] Countermeasures actually promulgated** — these are real, and analysts who treat the campaign as pure theatre have to account for them:

- **February 2021** — CMC EDD trial management measures on procurement quality assurance: vetting firms' industrial capacity and prior work quality, with penalties from one-, two- or three-year exclusion up to **lifetime bans**, extended to associated commercial entities, legal representatives and project agents. Review experts judged unprincipled are **publicly named** and barred.
- **May 2024** — State Council General Office guidelines on the bidding and tendering market.
- **July 2024** — Third Plenum decision calling for deeper reform of Party oversight of the armed forces and better integration between the forces and defence companies.
- **2025** — 《采购从业人员职业道德规范》 (Code of Professional Ethics for Procurement Practitioners), aiming at a *向战为战、廉洁高效、保障有力* procurement system and "purifying the procurement ecology" (净化采购生态); procurement digitalisation and institutional standardisation under the 15th Five-Year Plan.
- **Ongoing** — CMC Political Work Department and CMC DIC jointly issued 《关于加强新时代军队廉洁文化建设的措施》.
- **Ongoing** — merit-based promotion reform at NCO level. *PLA Daily* (August 2026) reports a Rocket Force unit explicitly breaking 论资排辈 (seniority ranking) in autumn NCO selection, making combat effectiveness the "first yardstick," refusing to advantage officers seconded to headquarters ("no shortcut of gilding your résumé, still less any hidden rule of bonus points for a familiar face"), and publishing every scoring stage. **This is the promotion-market problem being addressed at the bottom of the pyramid.**

### Assessment of the Party's answer

The "digging deeper" claim is not absurd. The elevation of the CMC DIC in 2017, the 2014 removal of audit from GLD control, and the 2021 procurement debarment regime are **real capability increases**, and Char is right that a decade of enforcement has changed behaviour at the margin.

But the claim is **unfalsifiable** in the absence of published amounts, published audits, or published verdicts. "The deeper you dig, the more you find" and "the more you fight, the worse it gets" generate identical observables when the only thing the public sees is the case count.

And it does not explain the specific recursion documented here:

> **Xu Caihou ran discipline before he ran the promotion market. Zhang Yang led the campaign against Xu's legacy while being part of it. Wang Houbin was installed to clean the Rocket Force and was purged from it. Zhang Youxia ran armaments before the man he is now accused of promoting.**

The pathology is not that the dirt is deep. **It is that the instrument of enforcement and the object of enforcement are drawn from the same pool — and the reform of 2016–17 changed the pool's supervisor without changing the pool.**

That is the finding this file exists to state, and it is why the base rates in [`base-rates.md`](base-rates.md) should be applied forward rather than discounted as a one-off crisis.

## 7. What this implies for the projections

Six consequences, carried into the main report:

1. **Assume continuation, not resolution.** No structural change since 2012 has introduced an external monitor. On Char's formulation, purges continue until the judicial system stops being self-sanctioned — which is not a 5-, 10-, or 15-year prospect. **The base rate should be applied forward at something close to its measured value, not discounted.**
2. **The next case cluster follows the marginal spending, not the last scandal.** Rents migrate to where money goes (§4.2). This is the reasoning behind the revised naval prediction in the main report §6.4 and [`forecasting-model.md`](forecasting-model.md) #9.
3. **Installing an outsider is not a fix, and the record now proves it.** Wang Houbin is the controlled experiment: a clean outsider, parachuted into the dirtiest service, purged in twenty-seven months. Ji predicted the mechanism; the case confirmed it. **Any projection that treats a newly installed caretaker as low-risk is ignoring the one directly relevant data point.**
4. **The enforcer track is simultaneously the safest and the most dangerous.** It is ascendant — the last CMC survivor and both 2026 elevations are discipline-lineage. It is also the track that produced Xu Caihou and Zhang Yang. **Zhang Shengmin's elevation from CMC DIC secretary to Vice Chairman reproduces the exact configuration — discipline authority plus command authority in one person — that produced the original problem.** That is not a prediction about Zhang Shengmin; it is an observation about the configuration.
5. **Purges do not slow spending.** SIPRI 2025 settles this for at least one year. Do not model the campaign as fiscal restraint or expect procurement to decelerate.
6. **Capability degrades from both directions.** Pierce's second-order cost — risk aversion, scripted exercises, inflated readiness reporting — is corroborated by Lin's exercise-response-time data. **The cure and the disease damage the same thing**, which is why [`strategic-implications.md`](strategic-implications.md) treats readiness as declining on both a corruption path and an anti-corruption path.

## 8. Open questions

Where the record actually stops. Each of these is falsifiable by a future disclosure, and each would move the analysis if answered.

1. **What did Li Shangfu pay, and to whom?** He is the only defence minister charged with 行贿 as well as 受贿, and the charge sheet says he sought personnel benefits *for himself*. The WSJ's reporting that Zhang Youxia was accused of "helping Li Shangfu obtain the defence-minister post" would, if true, close the circuit. [REPORTED, uncorroborated.]
2. **Why has no PLA verdict ever published an amount, when civilian defence-industry verdicts publish them in full?** Tan Ruisong's judgment discloses RMB 613 million and the transactional theories; Li Shangfu's and Wei Fenghe's disclose nothing. National-security classification, unwillingness to quantify damage to a force preparing for 2027, or a deliberate choice to keep deterrence purely hierarchical? **The asymmetry is systematic and worth an article of its own.**
3. **Is there a published price schedule for billets anywhere?** Reporting asserts "an implicit set of prices," but no primary document has surfaced. A CCDI notice, court document or leaked circular specifying per-billet prices would transform the office-selling literature. Until then, per-rank figures are rumour.
4. **What happened to Guo Zhenggang?** Eleven and a half years without a published disposition, for a serving major general who is the son of a CMC vice chairman.
5. **Was Li Yuchao sentenced?** English-language sources say suspended death, 7 May 2026; Chinese-language sources for that date name only Wei Fenghe and Li Shangfu. Unresolved.
6. **Does 吃空饷 exist in the PLA at scale?** No adjudicated case found. Plausible given Wang Shouye's RMB 20 million disguised as welfare and the size of the reserve/militia/PAFD structure — but currently an assertion without a case.
7. **Are there military-civil fusion arbitrage cases?** None found. The three-university bid-rigging debarment is the closest analogue. Watch item, not a finding.
8. **What is the CMC Audit Office actually finding?** It publishes nothing. Its researchers' 2018 diagnosis of bid-rigging was correct and five years early. Whether internal findings reach decision-makers, and whether the office will be given rank commensurate with its targets, is unknown — **and an elevation of the Audit Office to CMC-member grade would be the single clearest signal that the leadership has diagnosed the problem correctly.**
9. **Is Zhang Shengmin's promotion the fix or the next problem?** See §7.4.
10. **Does the purge bind or unbind spending?** SIPRI's 2025 finding argues distributional, not fiscal. If that holds in the 2026 data, it should reframe how the campaign's purpose is read.
11. **What does 深化政治整训 look like when it reaches the officer-promotion system?** August 2026 *PLA Daily* shows merit-based reform reaching **NCO** selection in the Rocket Force. Whether the same criteria are applied at corps-leader grade — where the money was — is the test that matters, and it is not observable.
12. **Is Zhang Youxia's fall a corruption case or a political case?** The MND used the standard formula; *PLA Daily* used far heavier political language (践踏破坏军委主席负责制); the WSJ reported espionage allegations; the NYT reported that U.S. officials deny them; the MND indirectly denied them. **All four can be true simultaneously if the corruption charge is the vehicle and the political dispute is the cause** — which is Christensen's point about corruption being universally chargeable, applied to the most senior case in the series.
