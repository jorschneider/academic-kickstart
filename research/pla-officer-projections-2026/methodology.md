# Methodology: how PLA succession analysis actually works

Companion to [`README.md`](README.md) §3. The main report's framework section states the rules; this file sources them, corrects several that are widely mis-stated in the secondary literature, and — unusually for this genre — reports the published **track record** of the forecasting method it uses.

The foundational work here is Kenneth W. Allen's, published through the China Aerospace Studies Institute (CASI) at Air University, together with the NDU Press volume *Chairman Xi Remakes the PLA* (Saunders, Ding, Scobell, Yang & Wuthnow, eds.) and Joel Wuthnow's work in *China Leadership Monitor*.

---

## 1. Grade, not rank, is the load-bearing variable

The PLA runs **15 grades (职务等级) and 10 ranks**. Grades are assigned to *organizations* as well as to people, and they govern authority: an organization may command an organization of lower grade and coordinate with one of equal grade. Rank is largely an overlay.

The PLA's own conflict rule is explicit: *when an officer of higher rank is subordinate to an officer of lower rank, the one with the higher **grade** is the superior.*

**Never infer authority from stars.** Two three-star officers can be a full grade apart.

The senior grades, with their ranks after the recent "rank-centric" adjustment:

| Grade | Rank |
|---|---|
| CMC Chairman | (none assigned) |
| CMC Vice Chairman | General/Admiral (上将) |
| CMC Member | General/Admiral |
| **Theater Command Leader (正战区级)** | General/Admiral |
| **Theater Command Deputy Leader (副战区级)** | Lieutenant General/Vice Admiral (中将), secondary Major General |
| Corps Leader (正军职) | Major General, secondary Lieutenant General |
| Corps Deputy Leader | Major General, secondary Senior Colonel |
| Division Leader | Senior Colonel |

Two structural notes that matter for this report:

- **The PLA has no four-star rank.** 上将 wears three stars. (A 一级上将 existed on paper 1988–94 and was never conferred.)
- **Theater Command Leader-grade billets are: the commander and political commissar of each service, of each of the five theater commands, and of the People's Armed Police, plus the director and PC of the CMC Logistic Support and Equipment Development Departments.** These are the eighteen-odd billets whose vacancy this report counts in README §2.3.

### 1.1 Two corrections that reshape the analysis

**The Joint Staff Department deputy-chief billets were downgraded** from Theater Command Leader to Theater Command Deputy Leader between April 2018 and March 2019. The old lateral move from JSD deputy to service command no longer exists. This is why the Joint Staff appears so rarely in modern career paths (§2).

**There are no CMC Member-grade organizations — only CMC Member-grade individuals.** Allen publicly retracted twenty-four years of his own published assessments on this point in August 2024: the Joint Staff, Political Work and Discipline Inspection Departments are Theater Command Leader-grade *organizations* whose directors hold a personal **policy promotion (政策升级)** — the PLA analogue of "frocking" — to CMC Member grade.

Two consequences. First, the National Defense University and Academy of Military Sciences were downgraded to TC Deputy Leader grade in March 2018, yet their presidents keep three stars and TC Leader grade *personally*, by the same device — so their three stars are not evidence of institutional weight. Second, and directly relevant to this report: **the Ministry of National Defense is not assigned a grade at all**, being a State Council body without a warfighting function. **Dong Jun's non-elevation to the CMC is therefore structurally normal and says nothing about his standing** — a correction to a common reading, including one this report's earlier drafts leaned toward.

## 2. The modal path to the top — measured, not assumed

Allen's CASI dataset profiles **all 31 officers promoted simultaneously to three stars and Theater Command Leader grade between December 2019 and March 2024**, with dates of birth and full billet-and-grade histories. Statistics derived from it:

**Age at three-star promotion (n = 31):** mean **59.3**, median **59**, range **54–62**. Seventy-four percent were 59 or older. The youngest was Chang Dingqiu at 54 — the outlier this report has treated as the ceiling case for how fast an officer can move.

**Grade and billet path:**

| Milestone | Share of the cohort |
|---|---|
| Held a **Corps Leader**-grade billet | **97%** |
| Held a **Theater Command Deputy Leader**-grade billet | **84%** |
| Ever served in a group army | 48% |
| **Ever commanded a group army** | **26%** |
| Passed through the Joint Staff Department | **6%** |

**This contradicts the conventional wisdom, including the version this report initially used.** "Group army command is the gateway to theater command" is at best half true: **fewer than one in three** of the recent three-star cohort ever commanded one. What is near-universal is the **grade sequence** — Corps Leader, then Theater Command Deputy Leader — not any particular billet. *Model the grade ladder, not the billet folklore.* And the Joint Staff figure of 6% confirms the effect of the 2018–19 downgrade.

**Norm decay, documented.** The traditional rule was that officers served in **two** Theater Command Deputy Leader billets before promotion. Allen and Corbett flagged the breach in January 2022 (five of seven officers had only one); across the full 2019–2024 cohort the split is now **13 with one, 13 with two** — a coin flip. Do not require two tours when screening candidates.

**The interval has collapsed.** Historically a one-star served 5–10 years before a second star, then roughly three years in a theater-deputy billet and three more before a third. **The average time to a three-star promotion coinciding with a grade promotion is now 2.0–2.5 years.** Any model still assuming a six-year two-step will systematically under-predict young risers — which is the error this report was at risk of making about Zhang Zheng and Zhu Chuansheng.

## 3. Age limits: two ceilings, currently suspended, with a waiver at the top

### 3.1 The statutory position

The PRC Law on Officers in Active Service (1988, amended 1994 and 2000) contains **two distinct age ceilings**, which nearly every secondary account conflates:

- **Article 14 — maximum age to hold a billet of a given grade:** platoon 30, company 35, battalion 40, regiment 45, division 50, corps 55, **theater-deputy 63, theater-leader 65**. Division and Corps Leader grades may be extended up to 5 years, Corps Deputy Leader up to 3 — **there is no extension provision at theater level**.
- **Article 46 — maximum age to remain on active duty at all:** regiment 50, division 55, corps deputy 58, corps leader 60; for all other grades, the same as the billet ceiling.

**This is the actual "up or out" machinery.** Below theater level an officer forced out of a billet may remain in uniform another 3–5 years, typically parked in a deputy or research post. At theater level the two ceilings converge and there is no extension, which is why 63 and 65 behave like genuine cliffs.

**The single most efficient screening test:** a 62-year-old two-star is not a candidate for anything; a 58-year-old two-star is. This eliminates more of a candidate pool faster than any patronage analysis.

### 3.2 Two crucial qualifications

**These provisions have been formally suspended since 1 January 2017.** An NPC Standing Committee Decision of 25 December 2016 temporarily suspended the officer law's provisions on grades, ranks, appointments, and retirement, authorising the CMC to write implementing measures. Those measures are unpublished. So the correct formulation is: *the pre-reform statutory ceiling, currently suspended but still apparently observed in practice, is 65.* Anyone asserting "by law, X must retire at 65" is over-claiming.

*(A related correction: there is no 2021 "Law on Military Officers." The officer-law revision has been on NPCSC plans since at least 2016 and has still not been reviewed. The 2021 legislation was the Military Service Law; the 2022 rank decision covered **enlisted soldiers only**.)*

**Reaching the CMC is itself an age waiver.** The 65 ceiling binds theater-leader grade. CMC members have served to **68, possibly 70** — Qiao Qingchen and Ma Xiaotian both served as service commanders and CMC members until 68; Miao Hua was retained at 67, Zhang Shengmin at 66. The informal "seven up, eight down" convention (67 stay, 68 go) governs the top body.

**This materially changes the age arithmetic in [`forecasting-model.md`](forecasting-model.md) §2:** an officer who is too old for a *theater* command may still be eligible for the *CMC*, and the officers already on the CMC can serve three to five years longer than the table's theater ceiling implies. Zhang Shengmin at 68 in 2026 is not an anomaly; he is inside the CMC-grade norm.

## 4. The predictors professionals actually use

Beyond grade, age and career path:

- **Protocol order (排序).** Names in PLA listings are ordered by importance and promotion probability, not seniority. *When a deputy moves to the top of the protocol order among his peers, he is being groomed; those listed below him will most likely retire.* The highest-leverage, lowest-cost indicator available — and the method that identified Zhang Zheng (README §3.5).
- **Foreign travel.** Most senior officers travel abroad once, if at all, and "their participation often presages promotion." Worked cases: Liu Shunyao accompanied the defence minister to the US in November 1996 and became PLAAF commander the following month; Qiao Qingchen accompanied Zhang Wannian to the US in September 1998 and became PLAAF political commissar in December. This makes military-diplomacy records a succession-analysis tool.
- **A training billet** somewhere in the career — a criterion a former PLAAF commander named in his own memoir.
- **Political credentialing** — NPC delegate or Party Congress membership. Note the current anomaly: the officers now being elevated largely **lack Central Committee membership**, which decouples military billet from Party rank in a way that has not been true for decades.
- **Number of same-grade billets held** — historically two theater-deputy tours; now decayed (§2).

## 5. The track record: a documented miss and a documented hit

This is what allows calibrated confidence rather than hand-waving. Both cases are Allen's, using the same method, and he reports the failure against himself.

**The miss (2017).** Analysis before the 19th Party Congress identified only one PLAAF officer meeting the criteria for service commander, and a published article predicted he would get the job. He did not; he became a theater commander instead.

**Why it failed matters more than that it failed.** The prediction was invalidated not by misreading the candidates but by **the rules changing underneath the model**: at the 19th Congress the CMC was cut from eleven members to seven, the service chiefs were removed from it, and the two billets that had served as the ante-chamber to service command — Joint Staff deputy chief and NDU commandant — were downgraded to theater-deputy grade.

> **The dominant failure mode in PLA succession forecasting is institutional change, not candidate misjudgement.** Any forecast should carry an explicit institutional-change caveat and be re-based whenever a Party Congress alters the CMC's size or a billet's grade moves.

**The hit (2021).** Applying the revised criteria to a screened field of eleven eligible officers, the same study named four "most likely candidates" for the next PLAAF commander. **Chang Dingqiu was among them, and got the job** — receiving his third star in September 2021 at 54. Han Shengyan, also named, went on to command the Central Theater. Wang Qiang and Xu Xueqiang, on the wider eligible list, both reached theater-leader-grade billets.

**But the timing was wrong.** The study projected the succession for 2022, reasoning the incumbent would serve to 65; he was replaced in December 2021 at 64.

> **Second lesson: the pool is far more predictable than the date.** Forecast *who is eligible* with reasonable confidence; forecast *when* with wide error bars, and expect early departures rather than late ones.

Both lessons are why this report ranks *types of officer* and *seats* with more confidence than names and dates, and why [`forecasting-model.md`](forecasting-model.md) attaches resolution criteria to every prediction.

## 6. A caution on factional analysis

This report uses network language — the "Fujian/31st Group Army line," the "Zhang Youxia armaments lineage" — and the reader should know the quantitative case against leaning on it.

Wuthnow marshals three pieces of counter-evidence:

1. **Daniel Mattingly's analysis of roughly 12,000 promotions** shows a **stark decline in CMC vice-chairman patronage networks during the Xi era** — the very networks a factional reading assumes are decisive.
2. Of 43 officers purged since July 2023, **only two besides Miao Hua were from Fujian, and only four served in the 31st Group Army.** The majority "lack career links to either Miao or He."
3. **He Ping**, Eastern Theater political commissar from 2019 to 2022 and therefore a clear He Weidong associate, was **not** purged.

Wuthnow's own preferred explanation is **readiness-driven housecleaning**, and he offers a structural finding that supports it: before the June 2024 Yan'an military conference, **13 of 15** purged officers were tied to the Rocket Force or the Equipment Development Department; afterwards, **21 of 25** came from other systems and **16 were operational commanders**. The campaign changed character mid-course, from a procurement clean-up into something that cut warfighters.

**Where this report uses network framing, treat it as descriptive of clustering in the announcements, not as a demonstrated causal mechanism.** The October 2025 nine really are heavily Nanjing/31st-Group-Army; the wider population is not.

## 7. Evidence tiers

This report adopts CSIS's three-tier classification, which is the cleanest public standard in the field, and recommends it to anyone extending the work:

- **Officially purged** — expelled from the Party, dismissed, stripped of an NPC or CPPCC seat, or sentenced.
- **Potentially purged** — absent from important meetings the officer would be expected to attend.
- **Missing** — conspicuously absent without further evidence.

CSIS's own scope limit is worth restating: its database "only includes generals and lieutenant generals **due to limitations in open-source information below those ranks**." That is the same wall this report hits in [`succession-pipeline.md`](succession-pipeline.md), reached independently.

**Conflating these tiers is the most common error in trade reporting on the purge**, and it is the error this report's §6.3 exposure register is structured to avoid.

## 8. The base rates professionals are working with

Two independent 2026 counts, with different methodologies, converging on a similar picture:

**CASI (Kuntz, July 2026)** — working from a denominator of **24 "critical" three-star roles** (two CMC vice chairmen, defence minister, Joint Staff chief, Political Work director, Discipline Inspection secretary, four service commanders and political commissars, and the commanders and PCs of the five theater commands):
- **41–91% turnover in those 24 critical roles in a single year** (10 officially actioned, at least 12 more rumoured).
- **44–94%** of all PLA three-stars removed or investigated in the same period.
- Over 100 two- and three-star officers removed, expelled or disappeared since 2022; only 38 officially announced.
- **Of the 35 two- and three-star officers Xi promoted since 2020, 32 were probably investigated and 29 likely removed.**

**CSIS ChinaPower (Feb/Apr 2026)**: 101 total (36 confirmed, 65 potential); **87%** of the 47 generals serving in 2022 or promoted since affected; **52%** of 176 senior billets; **61%** of purged active-duty leaders from the operational track; only **21%** of 52 key positions filled, **44%** interim or acting.

**The CASI figure — 29 of 35 officers Xi personally promoted since 2020 likely removed — is the sharpest available statement of this report's duration-effect finding** ([`base-rates.md`](base-rates.md) §3.1). Being promoted by Xi is not protection. On this record it is closer to a leading indicator.
