# The Civilian Twin: leadership churn in China's defence conglomerates

Companion to [`README.md`](README.md) §6 and [`corruption-analysis.md`](corruption-analysis.md). The purge has a civilian half that is under-analysed: the men on the *other side* of every procurement contract. Mapping it closes the loop, because it shows the campaign has now decapitated the buyer, the sellers, and the regulator simultaneously.

**A sourcing caveat that governs this file.** Every PRC-government primary source — the military procurement portals, SASAC, the CCDI website — was unreachable during research. Dates, names and outlets below are traceable to state-media reporting as carried in secondary aggregation, but **no procurement notice, CCDI announcement or appointment notice was read in the original.** Treat verbatim official language accordingly.

---

## 1. The headline finding

**Between December 2023 and August 2026, every major Chinese defence conglomerate changed its chairman at least once, and several changed twice or three times.**

And the removal mechanism is unusually quiet. Only a minority of these men were publicly placed under investigation. The commoner pattern is a **silent exit** — a routine-looking notice appointing a successor, followed months or years later by revocation of a CPPCC or NPC seat, which is often the first and only public confirmation that the man is in trouble.

**The seat revocation is the tell.** For anyone tracking this domain, CPPCC Chairman's Council and NPC Standing Committee delegate-termination announcements are the highest-value open-source indicator available: official, dated, and preceding formal disciplinary announcements by six months to two years. It is the same instrument that exposed the military purge (see [`purge-tracker.md`](purge-tracker.md)), applied to the industrial side.

### 2.1 The 4 February 2026 batch, which deserves more attention than it has had

An extraordinary out-of-session NPC Standing Committee meeting terminated the delegate status of three men on the same day: **Zhou Xinmin** (former AVIC chairman), **Luo Qi** (CNNC chief engineer, Chinese Academy of Engineering academician), and **Liu Cangli** (former president of the nuclear-weapons design establishment, Chinese Academy of Sciences academician).

**This is not procurement housekeeping. It is the design-authority layer of three separate weapons complexes, removed in a single sitting.**

### 2.2 The academician removals

Separately, and more tentatively, senior weapons designers began disappearing from the rosters of the Chinese Academies of Sciences and Engineering in March 2026 — reportedly including **Yang Wei**, the J-20's chief designer, **Wu Manqing** (airborne early warning radar), and **Liu Guozhi**, former director of the CMC Science and Technology Commission. Because academy membership is for life and de-listing is essentially unprecedented, quiet biography removal is a strong signal. **But none of these has an official announcement, and the reporting ranges from respectable to poor.** [REPORTED / UNVERIFIED]

If they hold up, the campaign has reached the people who *design* Chinese weapons, not merely those who buy and sell them — a materially different and more serious proposition.

## 3. The buyer, the regulator and the sellers, all at once

Three facts belong together:

1. **The buyer.** The CMC Equipment Development Department has had **no publicly identified director and no political commissar since October 2025**, and all three of its post-2016 directors — Zhang Youxia, Li Shangfu, Xu Xueqiang — have been disgraced or removed. Two of its deputies lost NPC seats in 2023.
2. **The regulator.** SASTIND, the civilian body that oversees and certifies the defence industry, has lost **two deputy directors**: Zhang Jianhua (who surrendered voluntarily, May 2025) and **Bian Zhigang, taken while in post in June 2026**.
3. **The sellers.** §2 above.

**There is no comparable simultaneous institutional decapitation anywhere else in the Chinese state.** And it raises a question this report cannot answer: if the Equipment Development Department has genuinely been leaderless for ten months, *who is signing China's weapons contracts?*

### 3.1 The link between the political and procurement purges

On **26 June 2026** the NPC Standing Committee terminated fifteen delegates' mandates in one batch. Two names in it matter together: **Ma Xingrui** — Politburo member, former CASC chairman, former Xinjiang party secretary — and **Xu Xueqiang**, the man who had run the Equipment Development Department from 2022 to 2025. Ma was formally expelled on 14 July 2026, the notice reportedly citing "large-scale family corruption."

The aerospace conglomerates functioned for two decades as a **cadre nursery** for the Chinese state: CASC alone produced a Politburo member, three provincial party secretaries, a MIIT vice-minister and a SASTIND director. That "aerospace faction" has now been substantially dismantled — Ma Xingrui, Xu Dazhe (ex-CASC chairman, ex-SASTIND director, ex-Hunan party secretary, investigated January 2026) and Zhang Hongwen (ex-CASIC deputy GM, later Hefei party secretary) are all down.

### 3.2 The counter-example: Cheng Fubo

One trajectory runs the other way, and it is the most revealing single career in this file:

> Chengdu Aircraft chairman (2013) → COMAC deputy GM (2018) → **Shaanxi vice-governor (2020)** → **Shaanxi organisation department head (2022)** → **Guangdong organisation department head (2022–24)** → **Norinco chairman (Apr 2024)** → **AVIC chairman (Jul 2025)** → **SASAC party secretary and director (Jun–Jul 2026)**.

Three of China's most sensitive defence-industrial and SOE-supervisory jobs in twenty-seven months — with **two provincial organisation-department postings** in between. A man who spent four years running personnel files for two provinces was inserted into two defence conglomerates in succession and then given the regulator of all central state enterprises.

**If one individual embodies the thesis that the defence-industrial churn is being run as a cadre-management operation rather than an industrial one, it is Cheng Fubo.**

A related pattern supports it: since 2024 the top jobs have increasingly gone to **outsiders** — CASIC's chairman came from CETC, its deputy party secretary from State Grid, CASC's chairman from municipal government. Set against Tan Ruisong (a twenty-year AVIC lifer) and Wu Yansheng (a CASC lifer), this looks deliberate: Beijing is breaking the practice of promoting from within closed technical hierarchies, which is the structure that produced the "feeding off the defence industry" pathology. It is also a capability risk, since it places non-specialists atop programmes of extraordinary technical complexity.

## 4. The procurement enforcement machinery

### 4.1 The tip-solicitation sequence — service by service

| Date | Issuer | Target | Look-back |
|---|---|---|---|
| **26 Jul 2023** | CMC Equipment Development Department | Equipment procurement and tendering violations | **Since October 2017** — the month Li Shangfu took over, thereby excluding his predecessor Zhang Youxia (see [`corruption-analysis.md`](corruption-analysis.md) §2A.6) |
| **18 Dec 2025** | Chinese military | **Air Force** procurement violations | Reported as open-ended |
| **24–25 Aug 2026** | Military Procurement Network | **Rocket Force** procurement violations | Not specified |

A military publicly soliciting corruption tips *from the general population* about a named service's procurement is close to unprecedented. The escalation is **EDD-wide → Air Force → Rocket Force**, and **there is no Navy notice.** That absence is the cleanest available test of this report's naval-audit hypothesis (§5).

### 4.2 The September 2025 Rocket Force retrospective audit

A **nine-year look-back** of Rocket Force procurement announced in September 2025 stripped qualifications from approximately **74 bid-evaluation experts (评标专家)** and **116 suppliers** — roughly 190 entities — with **lifetime bans** for procurement middlemen and penalties reaching central state-owned enterprises. [REPORTED, converging multi-source; not verified against a primary document]

**The evaluation-expert half is the more important finding.** Disqualifying 74 members of the bid-scoring panel is an admission that the supposedly independent technical layer that makes competitive tendering *mean* anything had been compromised. A single-source award is at least honestly single-source; **a rigged expert panel produces a competitive-looking award that is not competitive.** This is the mechanism by which the post-2016 reform that opened PLA procurement to competition was hollowed out from the inside.

### 4.3 The suspension-list regime

The military procurement network publishes a **suspension list (军队采购暂停名单)** naming barred firms. Its observable features: the dominant trigger is **围标串标** (bid-rigging and collusive bidding), applied at industrial scale; bans are **tiered by service** (some scoped specifically to the Rocket Force or Air Force); three years is standard but **lifetime bans exist and have been applied**; a graduated-supervision regime and a real (if unforgiving) appeals process both operate; and repeat offenders are re-listed.

The composition of the list is itself informative: pharmaceutical distributors, furniture makers, apparel brands, property management, telecoms engineering, logistics. **This is the sustainment and general-services tail of PLA procurement, not the weapons tail.** The high-classification tier — the actual platform programmes — is not published at all. **Everything an open-source analyst can see about PLA procurement enforcement is happening at the edge of the system, not at its core.** Do not over-read the visible cases as representative of where the money is.

## 5. Testing this report's naval-audit prediction

README §6.4 forecasts that naval equipment and shipbuilding are the likeliest next target. **The current evidence does not support it, and honesty requires saying so.**

**Against the prediction:**
- **There is no Navy tip-solicitation notice**, while the Air Force and Rocket Force both have one.
- CSSC's chairman, Xu Peng, is twenty months into the job and highly visible — not what a group under decapitation looks like.
- **Shipbuilding is the financial outperformer** of the Chinese defence complex: sell-side coverage of H1 2026 results reads "shipbuilding growth notably strong" while aviation contracted sharply.

**For the prediction:**
- Wen Gang's December 2024 exit carries every marker of a non-voluntary one — a two-month public disappearance before replacement, a Central Committee alternate quietly standing down — with no announcement in the twenty months since.
- The June 2026 investigation of **Yang Li**, former chairman of CSSC's Hong Kong shipping-leasing arm, opens the *financing* side, which is exactly where off-book value transfer in ship procurement would sit.
- The CSIC precedent (Hu Wenming, Sun Bo) shows the shipbuilding complex has been targeted before.

**Revised formulation.** Naval procurement remains the most plausible next target on structural grounds — it is where the capital has moved and it has never been audited at Rocket Force intensity — but the *current* indicators point elsewhere, and the prediction should carry an explicit falsification test:

> **The appearance of a notice soliciting tips on Navy procurement violations on the military procurement network would confirm it. Its continued absence through 2027, against a stable CSSC leadership and rising shipbuilding revenue, should be treated as disconfirming.**

Meanwhile the *actual* epicentre is aviation. See §6.

## 6. The financial traces

### 6.1 The macro number

SIPRI's Top 100 arms producers report (2024 data): **China's eight Top-100 companies saw combined arms revenue fall about 10% to US$88.3 billion**, against a global Top 100 that **grew 5.9%** to US$679 billion. **Norinco fell furthest, about 31%, to roughly US$14 billion.** AVIC and CASC also declined.

SIPRI's Nan Tian attributes it directly: corruption allegations "led to major arms contracts being postponed or cancelled in 2024," creating uncertainty over modernisation timelines and specifically over Rocket Force missile-deployment goals.

**The inflection is sharp.** In the previous data year China's Top-100 firms had grown 0.7%. The move from +0.7% to −10% is the purge showing up in revenue.

### 6.2 The micro numbers, and one genuinely interesting anomaly

H1 2026 results reported in late August 2026:

- **AVIC Shenyang Aircraft** (the J-15/J-16/**J-35** prime): net profit **down 58.4%** year on year — **with inventories sharply higher.**
- **AVIC Chengdu Aircraft** (the J-10/J-20 prime): revenue and net profit both down more than 20%.
- Shipbuilding, by contrast: growth "notably strong."

**The Shenyang inventory build is the most interesting single datum in this file.** Profit down 58% *with inventories rising* is not the signature of a factory that has stopped building. It is the signature of a factory that is building and **not getting its product accepted, delivered or paid for.**

Given that the acceptance function sits with the **军代表** military-representative system embedded in the plants, and the contracting function with an Equipment Development Department that has had no identified director since October 2025, the most parsimonious reading is a **customer-side bottleneck, not a supplier-side production failure.**

That has a real corollary for anyone counting Chinese airframes: **aircraft may exist that have not been accepted, and therefore have not been counted.**

### 6.3 What the financial data settles

The personnel map and the financial map tell the same story, and they both say **aviation, not the navy, is the current epicentre.** AVIC has lost two chairmen and a general manager and reportedly its J-20 chief designer, and both its combat-aircraft primes are contracting sharply. CSSC has had one quiet chairman swap and one leasing-subsidiary case, and is growing.

## 7. Programme impact: what the evidence does and does not support

**Supported:** contract-level disruption (SIPRI/Nan Tian, explicitly naming Rocket Force deployment goals); revenue-level disruption (§6); and real degradation of the *decision-making apparatus* for acquisition — a leaderless Equipment Development Department, a regulator down two deputy directors, a discredited bid-evaluation corps, and a design-authority layer under pressure.

**Not supported:** any claim that a specific named platform has demonstrably slipped. The J-35 appears to be *accelerating* (roughly 30 airframes reportedly built in its first year of service, carrier operations under way). The J-36 and J-50 continue in flight test. The Type 004 carrier is progressing, possibly accelerating, consistent with shipbuilding's revenue strength. Western commentary speculating about H-20 delays has no Chinese-source or documentary basis and should not be cited as fact.

**The defensible framing:** *the purge has not visibly stopped any flagship programme, but it has broken the machinery that turns money into contracts. The measurable damage is in the transaction layer — cancelled and postponed contracts, a 10% revenue contraction, a leaderless acquisition department, a discredited bid-evaluation corps, and an inventory build at the fighter primes suggesting output is outrunning acceptance. Programmes continue; the system that buys them does not currently work properly.*

## 8. Open questions

1. **What happened to Wu Yansheng?** CPPCC seat revoked December 2023, then **thirty-two months of official silence** — while Tan Ruisong, removed earlier, went through the full public cycle of investigation, expulsion, trial and sentence. The asymmetry is itself a finding.
2. The same question for **Wang Changqing, Wang Xiaojun and Zhou Xinmin** — all lost seats, none has a public disposition.
3. **Who is signing PLA procurement decisions** with the Equipment Development Department publicly leaderless since October 2025?
4. **Is there a Navy tip-solicitation notice?** The single highest-value falsification test in this file (§5).
5. **What did the December 2025 PLA equipment-domain regulation actually say?** It is the reform response to the whole affair and its text was not retrievable.
6. **Did the Liu Weidong case involve falsified weapons?** One low-quality outlet framed his thirteen-year sentence that way. If a court found a conglomerate deputy GM implicated in falsified weapons, that moves the story from rent extraction to delivered capability that does not work. **Verify against the judgment before use.**
7. **How does the 军代表 system work, and who staffs it?** PLA officers permanently resident inside supplier factories, signing acceptance — the structural heart of the military-industrial interface, and a total open-source blank.
8. **Does the Shenyang inventory build reflect an acceptance bottleneck?** Test against full-year results and any delivery reporting.
