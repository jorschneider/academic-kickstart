# China's Space, Missiles & Hypersonics

*The aerospace-strategic domain — where China is a peer or ahead in some of the most consequential categories (hypersonics, counterspace, theater missiles) and racing to close the one gap that remains (reusable, commercial-scale spaceflight). The primes, the programs, and the 2026 megaconstellation surge.*

*Working draft — August 30, 2026. Part of the [China Defense-Industrial Atlas](./defense-industrial-atlas.md); covers the atlas's "Missiles & Hypersonics" and "Space & Counterspace" scorecard rows. Nuclear delivery is treated in the [Nuclear Forces essay](./nuclear-forces.md).*

---

## The domain and its owners

This is the domain organized around two PLA services and two conglomerates. The **PLA Rocket Force** operates the missiles; the **PLA Aerospace Force** — created in April 2024 from the dissolved Strategic Support Force — runs space and counterspace. Behind them sit **CASC (China Aerospace Science and Technology)** and **CASIC (China Aerospace Science and Industry)** — sibling giants (~164,000 and ~150,000 employees respectively as of 2020, workforces up ~50% since 2000) whose numbered academies build essentially every Chinese rocket, satellite, and strategic or tactical missile.

The atlas rates the two halves **Competitive** — and in several of the highest-stakes categories (hypersonics, counterspace, theater anti-ship missiles), China is arguably at parity or ahead. The one place it is genuinely still catching up is reusable, commercial-scale launch — and 2026 is the year that gap started to close.

---

## Space launch — the reusability breakthrough

- **Cadence.** China is targeting **100+ orbital launches in 2026** for the first time, driven overwhelmingly by megaconstellation deployment — the two big constellations alone consumed ~45 launches in 2025 and are projected to need 70+ in 2026, launching batches every two to three weeks.
- **Reusability — the milestone.** On **August 19, 2026, LandSpace's methane-fueled Zhuque-3 became the first Chinese commercial rocket to land an orbital-class booster** (Gansu), with a reflight targeted within six months; CASC's reusable **Long March 12A** flew successfully in March 2026. Both are explicitly designed to compete with Falcon 9 on cost-per-kilogram, with **Space Pioneer (Tianlong-3), Deep Blue (Nebula-2), CAS Space, Cosmoleap, and Orienspace** close behind. This is the capability that turns high cadence into cheap cadence — and the clearest sign the launch gap to SpaceX is narrowing.

## Megaconstellations — the 2026 story

The single biggest driver of Chinese space activity, and an explicit bid for a Starlink-equivalent:
- **Guowang (China SatNet)** — the state constellation, ~13,000 satellites planned; ~190 launched by mid-2026, targeting 310 by year-end, then 900 in 2027 and 3,600/year from 2028.
- **Qianfan / SpaceSail (SSST, Shanghai + CAS)** — ~15,000+ planned; ~218 up by July 2026, having **cut per-satellite cost by ~96%**, and already signing service deals in **Brazil, Kazakhstan, and 30+ countries** in talks.
- Together **~28,000+ planned satellites** — dual-use broadband that PLA writing frames as a strategic-communications and resilience asset after watching Starlink in Ukraine. The race is also against the ITU spectrum/deployment deadlines. Not yet a Starlink peer, but a serious challenger by 2030.

## Counterspace — quietly world-class

The PLA Aerospace Force fields a broad offensive counterspace portfolio:
- **Co-orbital ASAT / RPO.** The **Shijian-21** demonstrator docked with a defunct Beidou-2 satellite and towed it 3,000 km above the GEO belt — a "debris-mitigation" mission that is functionally a co-orbital anti-satellite capability. US Space Force tracks Chinese "fast movers" conducting close inspections that could equally be interference.
- The full suite: **direct-ascent ASAT** (SC-19/DN-series), rendezvous-and-proximity operations, ground-based **laser dazzle / DEW**, and satellite jamming and cyber. Counterspace is a domain where China is a genuine peer competitor.

## Missiles — theater and strategic

- **Theater / conventional (the anti-access arsenal).** The Rocket Force's conventional missiles are the heart of the Taiwan-scenario problem: the **DF-26** (~4,000 km, "Guam killer"), the **DF-21D** anti-ship ballistic missile, land-attack cruise missiles (CJ-100/DF-100), and the massed force that US planners increasingly regard as the pacing conventional threat. China is [openly modeling how many missiles it needs to win a Taiwan war](https://asiatimes.com/2026/01/china-counting-how-many-missiles-it-needs-to-win-a-taiwan-war/), and building to that number.
- **Strategic.** The ICBM family — road-mobile **DF-41** (~15,000 km), the new **DF-61**, silo-based **DF-5C** and **DF-31** variants — is treated in the [Nuclear Forces essay](./nuclear-forces.md); the industrial base is CASC's CALT (1st Academy) and CASIC's 4th Academy.

## Hypersonics — arguably ahead

The category where China may lead the US outright:
- **Operational.** The **DF-17** medium-range missile with the **DF-ZF** hypersonic glide vehicle (Mach 5–10) is fielded; the longer-range **DF-27** (~5,000–8,000 km, assessed) extends it.
- **The 2021 "Sputnik moment."** China tested an HGV on a **fractional orbital bombardment system (FOBS)** — a glide vehicle carried partway to orbit before de-orbiting onto its target — a test that genuinely alarmed US officials.
- **The test base.** The **JF-22 hypervelocity wind tunnel** (CAS Institute of Mechanics, Beijing) can simulate Mach-30 flows — world-leading ground-test infrastructure — alongside scramjet hypersonic-cruise work and the **CAAA (CASC 11th Academy)** aerodynamics base. In hypersonics, the industrial and test base is not catching up; it is at or beyond the frontier.

## The production base

Missile and rocket output rides on the same scale advantage as the rest of the atlas: CASC and CASIC have expanded **solid- and liquid-rocket-motor production** for both military and space demand, feeding on the energetics base (ammonium perchlorate, HTPB — see the [ground-systems essay](./ground-systems.md)). The DoD's "5–6× faster" munitions-tempo assessment applies to missiles as much as to shells — magazine depth for a protracted regional fight.

## Where it's competitive, not dominant

- **Reusable heavy lift at scale.** Zhuque-3 and LM-12A are milestones, but SpaceX's launch cadence, reuse maturity, and Starship-class heavy lift remain ahead. This is the real remaining launch gap.
- **Space-grade electronics.** Radiation-hardened chips and some high-end space components still lean on foreign supply (see the [semiconductor essay](./semiconductor-chokepoint.md)).
- **Some seekers and guidance** at the very high end.

## Assessment

Across space and missiles, China is a genuine peer — and in hypersonics, counterspace, and theater anti-access missiles, arguably ahead of the US. The 2026 reusability breakthrough (Zhuque-3) and the 28,000-satellite megaconstellation surge are closing the one category where it lagged: cheap, high-cadence, commercial-scale spaceflight. Rated Competitive, but trending toward parity faster than almost any other domain in the atlas, with the megaconstellations the development to watch.
