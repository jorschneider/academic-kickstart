# The Chokepoint & Leverage Ledger

*The atlas's bottom line, as a strategic balance sheet. Every domain nets out to two columns — what China must still import, and what the world must import from China — and the analytically useful facts are the same six for each: who holds it, how binding it is, how long substitution takes, which way the trend runs, and what the lever is. This is the "so what."*

*Working draft — August 30, 2026. The cross-cutting synthesis of the [China Defense-Industrial Atlas](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/ecc69e75-bcc3-46cf-a303-3b95f8ac9b4c); companion interactive: the Chokepoint Ledger map.*

---

## The strategic read (the punchline first)

Strip the atlas down to its dependencies and a clear asymmetry appears:

- **China's dependencies are few, deep, and — with one exception — narrowing.** It must still import a short list of genuinely hard things: EUV lithography, the most advanced EDA software, high-bandwidth memory at scale, a handful of robot and machine-tool components. Most are closing on a 2–5-year horizon. The exception is **EUV lithography**, where the gap is wide and may not close this decade — the single highest-quality chokepoint the US and its allies hold.
- **China's leverage is broad, and mostly durable to ~2030.** The world depends on China for a *long* list of things — rare-earth magnets, gallium and germanium, small-drone components, mature-node chips, batteries, lidar, and the shipbuilding base — and substituting the biggest of them (magnets) is a ~6.7× scale-up that runs past 2030 at a 30–50% cost penalty.
- **The equilibrium is mutual, but the shapes differ.** The US holds *fewer but higher-quality* chokepoints (a few things China genuinely cannot make). China holds *more but more-fungible* chokepoints (many things others could eventually make, but not soon or cheaply) — and it has shown it will **trade them tactically**, dialing rare-earth and gallium controls up and down as negotiating chips (the December 2024 gallium ban, suspended in the November 2025 truce; the May 2025 EDA cutoff, met by rare-earth licensing, then both partly reversed). Call it mutually assured disruption: each side can hurt the other, neither can win cleanly, and the whole system now runs on managed, weaponized interdependence.

The practical upshot for policy: **US leverage is a wasting asset if China closes the EUV/EDA/HBM gaps, and Chinese leverage is a wasting asset if allied magnet and drone-component diversification matures — and both clocks are running.** The rest of this ledger is the line items.

## Column A — China's dependencies (what it must still import)

*Rated: severity (how binding), substitution horizon (how long to close), trend.*

| Chokepoint | Who holds it | Severity | Substitution horizon | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EUV lithography** | ASML (NL) | Critical | Very long (5–10 yr+, uncertain) | Stable — gap wide |
| **Advanced digital EDA** (≤5 nm synthesis/place-and-route) | Synopsys, Cadence, Siemens (US/DE) | High | Medium–long | Narrowing |
| **HBM at scale** (HBM3/3E) | SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron + tools | High | Short–medium (CXMT ~end-2026) | Narrowing fast |
| **Leading-edge fab tools** (metrology, ion implant, advanced dep) | US, Japan, NL | High | Medium | Narrowing |
| **Aero-engine hot section + civil certification** | US/UK/FR (CJ-1000A cert) | Moderate | Medium (~2027–30) | Narrowing |
| **Rad-hard / space-grade FPGAs** | US vendors | Moderate | Medium–long | Slow |
| **Top-tier RV reducers + planetary roller screws** | Nabtesco (JP); Rollvis/GSA/Ewellix (CH/EU) | Moderate | Medium | Narrowing (harmonic done) |
| **High-end force/tactile sensors** | ATI, Kistler (US/EU) | Moderate | Medium | Narrowing |
| **High-end 5-axis machine tools** | Japan, Germany, Switzerland | Moderate | Long | Slow |
| **Submarine quieting / high-end marine** | (Western know-how gap) | Moderate | Long | Slow |

**The read on Column A:** it is short, and the bottom two-thirds is narrowing. Everything from HBM down is a "when," not an "if" — China is closing these on money and time. The top of the column is where the leverage actually lives: **EUV is the master lock**, and **advanced digital EDA** and **leading-edge tools** are the two secondary locks that keep SMIC a generation behind at volume. If those three hold, the US retains a real edge in frontier compute; if they slip, most of the rest of this column is already gone.

## Column B — China's leverage (what the world must import from China)

*Rated: how much of the world's supply China controls, how hard it is to substitute, and how China has wielded it.*

| Chokepoint | China's share | Severity to others | Substitution horizon | How wielded |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rare-earth permanent magnets** (NdFeB) | ~85–90% output, ~90% processing | Critical — every motor, EV, missile, drone, robot | Long (~6.7× scale-up, past 2030, +30–50% cost) | April 2025 licensing regime, buyer-by-buyer |
| **Gallium / germanium / antimony** | Dominant processing | High — semiconductors, optics, IR, night vision | Medium (US pivoted to JP/DE/CA; partial) | Dec 2024 ban → Nov 2025 truce suspension; military-end-user ban persists |
| **Small-drone components** (motors, ESCs, batteries, cameras) | ~80–90% of FPV motors | High — everyone's drones, incl. Ukraine and the US | Medium (Ukraine ~27% motors; cameras hardest) | Component + magnet export scrutiny |
| **Mature-node ("legacy") chips** | ~1/3 of global, rising | Moderate–high and growing — cars, appliances, weapons, grid | Medium–long | Big Fund III capacity build; overcapacity/price-war risk |
| **Batteries** (CATL, BYD, EVE) | Dominant cells | High — EVs, grid, drones, some military | Medium–long | Latent; NDAA bars DoD procurement from Oct 2027 |
| **Lidar** (Hesai, RoboSense) | Leads robotaxi & robot lidar | Moderate — autonomy, robotics | Medium | 1260H-listed; commercial dominance |
| **Shipbuilding capacity** | ~230× US | Strategic — naval and commercial | Very long (structural) | Latent; the deepest structural asymmetry |
| **Processed critical minerals** (graphite, tungsten, etc.) | Dominant | Moderate — defense-adjacent | Medium–long | Added to the 2023–25 control regimes |

**The read on Column B:** it is long, and the top of it is *more* durable than anything in Column A except EUV. **Rare-earth magnets are China's crown jewel** — the one input under every motor and actuator on every page of this atlas, and the one others are furthest from replacing. Gallium and drone components are the next tier: real leverage, but partially substitutable and already being worked around under wartime and policy pressure. The bottom of the column (mature chips, shipbuilding) is leverage that is *growing*, not eroding — the places where the dependency is moving further in China's favor.

## The dynamics — how the two columns interact

The two columns are not static inventories; they are actively traded against each other, and 2025–26 was the year that became explicit:

- **May 2025:** Washington ordered the EDA majors (Column A, US-held) to halt China sales. Beijing answered by tightening **rare-earth magnet licensing** (Column B, China-held). Within weeks, as part of a broader truce, the EDA cutoff was reversed and magnet flows partially resumed. Neither side could absorb the escalation.
- **December 2024 → November 2025:** China banned **gallium/germanium/antimony** to the US (Column B); the US pivoted suppliers and built stockpiles; China suspended the ban in the November 2025 truce — while keeping the military-end-user prohibition in force. Leverage held in reserve, not spent.
- The pattern is **calibrated, reversible signaling** rather than all-out decoupling, because each side's chokepoints are load-bearing for the other's economy. This is the "weaponized interdependence" equilibrium, and it is why export-control policy now reads like arms control — thresholds, signaling, and the constant question of what each move provokes in the other column.

## What to watch — the crossover points

The ledger is analytically useful mainly for its **clocks**. The moments that would shift the balance:

1. **HBM at scale (China closing, ~2026–27).** If CXMT hits volume HBM3, the US loses one of its three compute locks and China's AI-accelerator ceiling rises sharply. The nearest-term crossover.
2. **Allied magnet diversification (~2028–30).** The G7's sub-60%-single-supplier-by-2030 target; if Lynas/MP Materials and allied magnet-making scale past ~40% of supply, China's crown-jewel leverage starts to erode. The furthest-out but highest-stakes crossover.
3. **The mature-node price war (2026–27).** If Big Fund III capacity triggers a legacy-chip glut, China's leverage there *grows* (by hollowing foreign fabs) even as its frontier dependency persists — leverage moving the "wrong" way for the West.
4. **Drone-component reshoring (rolling).** Ukraine and the US are localizing motors and controllers under combat pressure; cameras and magnets are the sticky last mile. Watch whether the war-driven learning curve outpaces China's component-export leverage.
5. **EUV (the long clock, low probability this decade).** Any credible sign that SSMB or LDP is nearing a *commercial* scanner — not just EUV light — would be the single most destabilizing development in the entire ledger. Treat every such report with heavy skepticism.

## Why this is the analytically useful view

The domain maps answer "who builds what." This ledger answers the questions a policymaker actually asks: **Where can we apply pressure, how long will it work, and what will it cost us in return?** The atlas's scorecard says China is "Dominant" in shipbuilding and "Dependent" in semiconductors; the ledger says *why that matters* — that the US holds three high-quality compute locks with running-down clocks, that China holds one nearly-unbreakable magnet lock and a dozen softer ones, and that both sides are now managing the interdependence rather than trying to sever it. That is the difference between a catalog and an analysis.
