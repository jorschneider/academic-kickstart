# China's Nuclear Forces & the Warhead Complex

*The fastest nuclear expansion in the world — from a minimal retaliatory deterrent to a modernizing triad heading toward peer status. What's driving it, who builds it, and why the atlas still rates it "catching up."*

*Working draft — August 30, 2026. ## The breakout

For fifty years China kept a deliberately minimal nuclear force — a few hundred warheads, de-alerted, built around a single promise of assured retaliation. That era is over. China's stockpile reached an estimated **~620 warheads at the start of 2026** (up from ~500 just two years earlier), and both the Pentagon and independent analysts project it passing **1,000 by 2030** and heading toward **~1,500 by 2035**. This is the fastest peacetime nuclear buildup by any state since the early Cold War.

Yet "fastest-growing" is not "largest." China's ~620 warheads remain a fraction of the ~1,550 deployed strategic warheads each held by the US and Russia, and getting much past 1,000 runs into a fissile-material ceiling. That combination — explosive growth, genuine triad modernization, but still well behind the two superpowers and constrained by material production — is why the atlas rates this domain **Catching up (fast)** rather than peer.

## The numbers and the silos

- **The desert silo fields.** Roughly **350 new missile silos** have been built across three fields — Yumen, Hami, and Ordos/Yulin — the most visible sign of the buildup. They shift China toward a US/Russia-style silo-based ICBM posture on top of its road-mobile force.
- **Higher readiness.** The Pentagon assesses that some warheads are now **mated to missiles at higher alert**, a break with decades of storing warheads separately — the physical correlate of the doctrine shift below.
- **The fissile ceiling.** China held ~14 tonnes of HEU and ~2.9 tonnes of separated plutonium as of 2023 — enough to roughly double the stockpile, but **producing well beyond ~1,000 warheads requires new fissile-material production** (see the complex below). This is the real quantitative constraint.

## The triad — revealed in 2025 (built by CASC & CASIC)

The September 2025 parade was the **first public display of a full Chinese nuclear triad**, and a catalog of a diversifying force:
- **Land (the largest leg).** China now fields ~nine ICBM variants — the road-mobile **DF-41**, the surprise new road-mobile **DF-61** (16-wheel TEL), the liquid-fueled multi-megaton **DF-5C**, a new silo-based **DF-31BJ**, and the DF-31 family — a deliberate mix of mobile and silo, solid and liquid, for survivability and flexibility.
- **Sea.** The MIRV-capable **JL-3 SLBM** now has intercontinental range, letting Type 094 SSBNs target the continental US from protected waters; the next-generation, quieter **Type 096** (up to 24 JL-3, entering service before ~2030) is the real survivability leap for the sea leg (see the [naval map](./naval-undersea-suppliers.md)).
- **Air.** The **JL-1 air-launched ballistic missile** on the H-6N completes and publicly confirms the third leg.

All of this delivery hardware comes from **CASC (1st Academy/CALT)** and **CASIC (4th Academy)** — the same missile-industrial base mapped in the Supplier Atlas.

## The warhead complex — the supplier base

Behind the delivery systems sits the weapons-production enterprise, the part that actually gates the stockpile:
- **CAEP — China Academy of Engineering Physics (Mianyang, "Science City").** China's Los Alamos: the warhead design authority, a dozen-plus institutes, and — per a [2026 CNN investigation](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/01/china/investigates-china-secretly-expanding-nuclear-weapons-infrastructure-intl-invs) and VERTIC imagery analysis — visibly expanding infrastructure. Entity-Listed since 1997 (the list's charter member).
- **CNNC — the fuel cycle and the plutonium restart.** The binding enabler of a larger arsenal. CNNC is bringing online two **CFR-600 sodium-cooled fast-breeder reactors** at Xiapu (unit 1 ~2023, unit 2 expected 2026) plus a **200 t/yr reprocessing plant** at Jinta, Gansu — infrastructure the Pentagon assesses is intended to **re-establish weapons-grade plutonium production at scale**. This is how China would break the fissile ceiling.
- **The Russian footnote.** In an irony worth flagging, Russia's **TVEL (Rosatom)** supplied the HEU fuel for CFR-600 unit 1 under a 2019 contract — a reminder that even the fissile-production restart leaned, at the margin, on foreign supply.

## The doctrine shift

China still officially maintains **No First Use** — but it is layering on an **"early-warning counterstrike" (预警反击)** posture, which the West calls **launch-on-warning**, and the Pentagon expects it to be able to implement by the **end of the 2020s**. The higher-readiness warheads, the silo fields, and a new space-based early-warning architecture are all consistent with this. It does not abandon NFU on paper, but it is a materially different, more responsive, more dangerous posture than the de-alerted force of the past.

## Where it's still "catching up"

- **Absolute numbers.** ~620 vs. ~1,550 deployed each for the US and Russia — a genuine gap, closing but wide.
- **Fissile-material scale-up.** Getting past ~1,000 warheads depends on the CFR-600s and Jinta reprocessing actually delivering weapons-grade plutonium at volume — an in-progress, not-yet-proven capability.
- **Sea-leg survivability.** The current Type 094s are relatively noisy; a truly survivable sea deterrent waits on the Type 096, which waits on the submarine-quieting the naval map flags as the PLAN's soft spot.

## Assessment

China is running the fastest nuclear expansion in the world, has revealed a genuine and diversifying triad, and is building the fuel-cycle infrastructure to sustain a much larger force while shifting to a launch-on-warning posture. But it remains well behind the two nuclear superpowers in absolute terms and constrained by fissile-material production — a domain defined by extraordinary momentum from a low base. Catching up, fast.
