# China's Naval & Undersea Suppliers

*A deep dive on who builds the PLA Navy — the yards, the propulsion and sonar institutes nobody names, the submarine enterprise, and the unmanned-undersea and seabed-infrastructure layer. Companion to the [PLA Supplier Atlas](./chinese-military-supplier-map.md).*

*Working draft — August 30, 2026.*

---

## Why naval is the sharpest case

If you want to see China's defense-industrial advantage at its most extreme, look at the sea. CSIS estimates Chinese shipbuilding capacity at [roughly 230× that of the United States](https://www.csis.org/analysis/china-outpacing-us-defense-industrial-base); a single CSSC yard, Jiangnan, has more capacity than every US shipyard combined. The PLAN is already the world's largest navy by hull count and is on track for ~435 ships by 2030. Unlike aviation (engines) or compute (lithography), there is no obvious binding constraint here — the constraints that remain are specific and undersea: quieting, marine propulsion at the high end, and the submarine enterprise, which is exactly where this dossier spends most of its time.

The organizing fact to hold onto: since the 2019 re-merger of CSSC and CSIC into a single **China State Shipbuilding Corporation**, essentially the entire enterprise — commercial and naval, north and south — sits under one roof. The numbered research institutes still carry their old CSIC/CSSC designations, and those numbers are the real map. Learn the institutes and you understand the industry; watch only the ships and you miss where the capability actually lives.

The whole complex became a trade-war front in October 2025, when USTR began levying [port fees on Chinese-built and Chinese-operated ships](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/ustr-port-fee-implementation) ($50/net ton, rising toward $140 by 2028) to try to reanimate US shipbuilding; Beijing retaliated within hours with mirror-image fees on US-linked ships, and the two sides [suspended the measures in November 2025](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/ustr-port-fee-suspension-what-you-need-to-know) as part of a broader truce. The episode underlined the thing that makes CSSC so hard to sanction: the commercial order book that subsidizes the warships is also woven into global trade.

## Layer 1 — The building yards

China builds warships at a scale and tempo no other country matches, across a handful of enormous yards that all serve both commercial and naval customers. The dual-use character is not incidental — it is the cross-subsidy that makes the naval output affordable.

- **Jiangnan Shipyard (Changxing Island, Shanghai)** — the crown jewel. Built the catapult carrier **Fujian** (commissioned November 5, 2025) alongside Type 055 cruisers and Type 052D destroyers, while running one of the world's busiest commercial berths. More capacity than the entire US shipbuilding base in one location.
- **Dalian Shipbuilding (DSIC)** — built the carrier **Shandong** and now the **Type 004** — a ~286m hull, larger than Fujian at the equivalent stage, with two shielded compartments that analysts read as [reactor containment](https://www.twz.com/sea/chinas-next-aircraft-carrier-is-taking-shape): China's first nuclear-powered carrier, likely drawing on the land-based "Dragon Might" prototype reactor near Leshan. Shares Type 055/052D serial production with Jiangnan.
- **Hudong-Zhonghua (Shanghai)** — big-deck amphibs (Type 075 LHD) and the world-first **Type 076 Sichuan** drone carrier (below), plus Type 054B frigates — in the same yard as a world-leading LNG-carrier line. The cleanest single illustration of the dual-use shipyard model.
- **Guangzhou Shipbuilding International (GSI, Longxue)** — the southern frigate/amphib workhorse: Type 054A/B frigates and Type 071 LPDs, again alongside heavy commercial output.
- **Bohai Shipbuilding (Huludao, Liaoning)** and **Wuchang (Wuhan)** — the submarine yards, treated in Layer 3.

## Layer 2 — The unheralded institutes: design, propulsion, sonar

This is the layer that decides whether the hulls are any good, and the one outside coverage almost never names. Each of these is a de facto national laboratory.

**Design bureaus**

- **701 Institute — China Ship Development & Design Center (Wuhan)** — the surface-combatant brain: designed the Type 055, the 052D line, and the carrier hull forms. If a PLAN surface ship exists, 701 drew it.
- **719 Institute — Wuhan Second Ship Design Institute** — the **nuclear-submarine** design bureau and the single most sensitive naval institute in China. Every SSN and SSBN traces here; its late director Huang Xuhua was China's "father of nuclear submarines."
- **708 Institute — Marine Design & Research Institute of China / MARIC (Shanghai)** — carriers, amphibious ships, and much of the auxiliary and merchant fleet. The Type 076's basic design lineage runs through 708.

**Propulsion — the real high-end constraint**

- **703 Institute (Harbin)** — marine **gas turbines**: the QC-280 that powers the Type 055 and 052D. China's blue-water surface fleet rode on this institute solving what Ukraine's Zorya-Mashproekt once supplied. No QC-280, no Type 055 fleet.
- **711 Institute — Shanghai Marine Diesel Engine Research Institute** — marine **diesels**, turbochargers, and governors; the design authority behind the **CHD620** engine that replaced blocked German MTUs on export submarines (below). The reliability of Chinese and Chinese-supplied conventional subs is a 711 question.
- **712 Institute (Wuhan)** — **integrated electric propulsion (IEP)** and motors. Together with the PLA's **Naval Engineering University** in Wuhan — home of Academician **Ma Weiming**, the driving figure behind China's [medium-voltage-DC IEP and electromagnetic catapult](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/china/plan-propulsion-electric.htm) work — 712 is central to the electric-drive future: the EMALS on Fujian and Sichuan, and the all-electric powertrain expected on the Type 004 and next-generation combatants. This IEP-plus-EMALS cluster is arguably the most strategically important naval-tech node in China.
- **704 Institute (Shanghai)** — auxiliary machinery and shipboard systems; the unglamorous but indispensable balance-of-plant supplier.

**Sensors and underwater weapons**

- **726 Institute — Shanghai Marine Electronic Equipment Research Institute** — **sonar** and surface-ship combat systems. Chinese ASW and submarine acoustic sensing lives here; quieting and detection are the PLAN's acknowledged weak spot, which makes 726 a key watch-item.
- **705 Institute (Xi'an, under CASC's academy system historically; naval torpedo work)** and the broader underwater-weapons complex — the **Yu-6** heavyweight and **Yu-10**-class torpedoes, plus naval mines. Torpedo and mine sourcing is one of the murkier corners of the enterprise and deserves more open-source work.

## Layer 3 — The submarine enterprise

Submarines are where the PLAN is genuinely still catching up, and therefore where the supplier story is most consequential. Two yards, one design bureau, and a propulsion problem.

**Nuclear boats — Huludao, and now Wuhan.** **Bohai Shipbuilding (Huludao)** has historically been the sole builder of Chinese nuclear submarines. Commercial satellite imagery from [February 2026 showed the first Type 095 SSN](https://www.janes.com/defence-intelligence-insights/defence-news/sea/china-begins-outfitting-first-type-095-nuclear-attack-submarine) (~110m × 12m) fitting out there, alongside continuing work on the **Type 096 SSBN** — a ~20,000-ton boat built around the **JL-3** SLBM that will give China a genuinely survivable sea-based deterrent. A [next-generation "Type 09V" SSN](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2026/02/chinese-type-09v-next-generation-ssn-appears-at-bohai/) has also appeared at Bohai.

The big structural news is that nuclear-submarine construction appears to be **expanding to Wuchang (Wuhan)** — a yard previously believed to build only conventional boats. That inference rests on an embarrassing episode: in spring 2024, the first **Zhou-class** SSN (distinctive X-stern) [sank at its pier at Wuchang](https://www.twz.com/sea/chinas-new-nuclear-submarine-sank-during-mysterious-incident-in-wuhan-report) during fitting-out, caught by satellite when salvage cranes suddenly appeared. Beijing never acknowledged it. The setback is real, but the location is the more important signal: a second nuclear-submarine line is exactly the capacity expansion US planners feared.

**Conventional boats — Wuchang and the AIP question.** The **Yuan class (Type 039A/B/C)** is the PLAN's diesel-electric mainstay and the first with indigenous **air-independent propulsion**, using a domestically built **Stirling engine** for weeks of quiet submerged endurance. China has [publicly debated but not fielded lithium-ion submarine batteries](https://cimsec.org/neither-fish-nor-fowl-chinas-development-of-a-nuclear-battery-aip-submarine/) (Japan leads there), citing thermal-runaway risk — a rare case of Chinese caution, and a useful tell about where they assess their own maturity.

**The export tell: CHD620.** The clearest window into China's remaining marine-propulsion gap is the **Hangor class** built for Pakistan (S26 export derivative of the Yuan). It was designed around German **MTU** diesels; after Germany [blocked MTU exports in 2021](https://www.defensenews.com/naval/2024/04/30/china-launches-pakistans-hangor-submarine-despite-engine-row-with-germany/) upon discovering they were reaching Chinese warships, the program had to re-engineer around the indigenous **CHD620** — which delayed both Pakistan's and Thailand's submarine programs and still lacks a long operational track record. The first Hangor was commissioned in May 2026. When a single blocked German engine can stall two countries' submarine fleets, you've found a real chokepoint.

## Layer 4 — Unmanned undersea and the seabed

The fastest-moving naval frontier, and the one where China has quietly built a lead in sheer numbers.

- **Extra-large UUVs.** The September 2025 Beijing parade rolled out two operational XLUUV classes: the **HSU100** (~15m, retractable ISR sensors) and the sleeker **AJX002** (~18–20m, assessed as a minelayer). Naval analyst H.I. Sutton's [observation is the headline](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2025/08/what-the-world-is-about-to-learn-about-chinas-extra-large-underwater-drones/): the eight vehicles paraded were drawn from an operational inventory, and "other navies don't have eight XLUUVs at all." The manufacturing base spans state institutes (719, 705) and commercial robotics firms feeding a program the US has struggled to match with its own Orca.
- **Unmanned surface vessels.** **Yunzhou Tech (Zhuhai)** pioneered the commercial-to-military USV swarm; the PLAN's uncrewed surface fleet and the drone-carrier logic of the Type 076 are downstream of that ecosystem.
- **Seabed infrastructure — the gray-zone layer.** **HMN Technologies (formerly Huawei Marine)** is one of only four firms worldwide that build and lay transoceanic **subsea cables**, and the only Chinese one — [Entity-Listed in 2020](https://thediplomat.com/2025/07/us-planned-ban-on-chinese-technology-in-undersea-cables-implications-for-southeast-asia/) and steadily excluded from Western consortium projects (SEA-ME-WE 6, East Micronesia) on espionage and cable-cutting concerns. Alongside it sits China's growing **seabed sensing** ambition — the so-called "Underwater Great Wall" of fixed hydrophone arrays marketed by CSSC subsidiaries for export. Cables and seabed sensors are where naval-industrial policy blurs into critical-infrastructure security.

## The platforms these suppliers feed (2026 snapshot)

Kept brief, because the point of this map is the suppliers, not the ships — but the programs are what make the supplier stakes concrete:

- **Fujian (Type 003)** — catapult trials of the **J-35**, **J-15T**, **J-15DT**, and **KJ-600** completed; [full operational capability expected during 2026](https://www.airdatanews.com/chinas-fujian-carrier-could-reach-full-operational-capability-by-end-of-2026/). China's first true blue-water strike carrier.
- **Type 076 Sichuan** — the world's first amphibious assault ship with EMALS, [in sea trials since November 2025](https://www.twz.com/sea/chinas-type-076-supersized-amphibious-assault-ship-heads-to-sea-on-its-maiden-voyage), designed to catapult **GJ-11** stealth UCAVs; commissioning expected 2026.
- **Type 004** — nuclear-powered supercarrier taking shape at Dalian.
- **Type 095 / 096 / Zhou-class** — the next-generation SSN and SSBN wave, out of Huludao and now Wuchang.
- **Type 055** — the 12,000-ton cruiser that anchors carrier escort, in serial production.

## The unheralded naval ten

The names worth an analyst's attention below the yard level:

1. **703 Institute (Harbin)** — marine gas turbines; the QC-280 gates the entire surface fleet.
2. **712 Institute + Naval Engineering University (Wuhan)** — integrated electric propulsion and EMALS; the electric-drive future runs through here.
3. **719 Institute (Wuhan)** — the nuclear-submarine design bureau; the ceiling on the undersea force.
4. **726 Institute (Shanghai)** — sonar and combat systems; the PLAN's acknowledged soft spot.
5. **711 Institute (Shanghai)** — marine diesels and the CHD620; the reliability of conventional and export boats.
6. **Bohai Shipbuilding (Huludao)** — every Chinese nuclear submarine, now with a possible second line at Wuchang.
7. **HMN Technologies** — the only non-Western transoceanic subsea-cable builder; a seabed-infrastructure chokepoint in reverse.
8. **The XLUUV base (719 / 705 / robotics firms)** — an operational unmanned-undersea inventory no other navy fields.
9. **708 / MARIC (Shanghai)** — carrier and amphib design, including the Type 076 drone carrier.
10. **CHD620 / marine-diesel supply** — the one imported-engine dependency that still visibly bites.
