# The Taiwan-Contingency Lens

*The atlas re-cut by the only question that ultimately matters for it: what is decisive when the shooting starts? The economic chokepoints of the [ledger](./chokepoint-leverage-ledger.md) run on a timescale of years; a cross-strait fight runs on hours, weeks, and reload rates — and that reframes which parts of the industrial base actually count.*

*Working draft — August 30, 2026. An operational lens over the [China Defense-Industrial Atlas](./defense-industrial-atlas.md). This is an analytical exercise grounded in published wargames, not a prediction.*

---

## The core insight: wartime chokepoints ≠ peacetime chokepoints

The single most useful thing this lens does is separate two things the debate usually conflates. **The chokepoints that dominate peacetime competition are mostly not the ones that decide a war, and vice versa.**

- **EUV lithography, advanced EDA, leading-edge AI compute** — the crown jewels of the export-control fight — bite on a **timescale of years**. In a conflict measured in weeks, China fights with the compute and chips it already has; denying it *future* frontier chips does little to the *current* fight. These are competition levers, not war-winning ones.
- **Magazine depth, munitions-production tempo, shipbuilding and battle-damage repair, drone mass, and the energetics and magnets that sustain them** — bite in **hours to months**, and they are decisive. This is exactly the layer where China's "wartime footing" is built, and where the US "peacetime footing" is most exposed.

That inversion is the analytical payoff of the whole atlas: the US holds the higher-quality *peacetime* chokepoints; China holds the structural *wartime* advantages. A Taiwan contingency is fought on China's preferred axis.

---

## Phase 1 — The standing posture (what's already fielded)

Before a shot, the balance is a function of inventory and surge capacity, not future production:
- **China's missile magazine** (PLA Rocket Force, built by CASC/CASIC) is the largest conventional ballistic and cruise arsenal on earth — the DF-26/DF-21D/DF-17 layer designed precisely to hold US carriers and regional bases at risk. **China is openly modeling how many missiles it needs to win.**
- **The drone arsenal** — from Wing Loong/CH strike drones to the FPV mass drawn from the world's dominant component base — is deep and cheap.
- **Shipbuilding** at ~230× US capacity means China starts with hulls *and* the yards to replace losses.
- **Munitions stockpile + 150–250% surge capacity** and a base acquiring materiel [5–6× faster than the US](https://asiatimes.com/2026/07/us-wartime-buildup-races-against-chinas-industrial-clock/).

The US enters with different assets: **forward posture and alliances, ISR/C2 quality, the undersea advantage (SSNs), and pockets of deep, exquisite munitions** — but a defense-industrial base on a peacetime tempo.

## Phase 2 — The opening salvo (hours to days)

The first exchange is decided by **first-salvo magazine depth and the ability to blind the other side's kill chain**:
- **Missiles saturate.** China's opening problem for the US is volume — enough precision munitions arriving together to overwhelm defenses (the [FK-3000/HQ counter-swarm layer](./counter-uas-ecosystem.md) exists because both sides expect this).
- **Counterspace and EW blind the sensors.** China's [co-orbital ASAT, direct-ascent ASAT, and GNSS/SATCOM jamming](./space-missiles-hypersonics.md) target the space-based kill chain both sides depend on; the CETC EW suite contests the spectrum.
- **The uncomfortable US data point:** CSIS wargaming has the US firing its **entire inventory of LRASM anti-ship missiles within the first days "in all scenarios,"** and running out of some long-range precision munitions in [**under a week**](https://www.csis.org/analysis/empty-bins-wartime-environment-challenge-us-defense-industrial-base). Both sides go "winchester" on key munitions fast — but only one has the magazine and the tempo to reload.

## Phase 3 — The reload fight (weeks)

This is where the atlas's central asymmetry becomes operational, and where China's advantage is starkest:
- **US "empty bins."** With LRASM expended and JASSM-ER [less effective against naval targets](https://www.csis.org/analysis/empty-bins-wartime-environment-challenge-us-defense-industrial-base), the US faces a ~two-year production lag, rocket-motor bottlenecks, and reliance on imported energetics — the Heritage "[Triple Bind](./ground-systems.md)" that could produce supply failure within **25–120 days**.
- **China's reload machine.** Automated "smart factories," 150–250% surge, and MCF conversion of commercial production feed the magazine back up at 5–6× the US rate.
- **The drone irony.** Attritional drone warfare consumes FPVs by the million — and the components to build them (motors, batteries, magnets) come ~80–90% from China. In a protracted fight the US and its partners would be trying to mass-produce drones from a supply chain China controls (see the [ledger](./chokepoint-leverage-ledger.md)); China would not.

## Phase 4 — Protracted war & blockade (months) — where it cuts both ways

Only at this timescale do the peacetime chokepoints and China's *own* vulnerabilities finally matter — and the picture becomes genuinely two-sided:
- **China's industrial edges compound.** Shipbuilding (~230×) means battle-damage repair and hull replacement; energetics and rare-earth magnets sustain munitions and motors; the mobilized DIB grinds.
- **But China's own dependencies open up.** A protracted conflict likely means a **blockade — in both directions.** China imports the majority of its **oil, LNG, food, and — critically — advanced chips and chipmaking tools**; a war that interdicts those hits the very [semiconductor and compute dependencies](./semiconductor-chokepoint.md) the ledger flags, now on a wartime clock. The economic chokepoints the US holds *do* bite over months — just not in the opening fight.
- **The US quality and undersea edges persist.** The one domain where China clearly lags — **submarine quieting and ASW** — is exactly the domain most decisive in a blockade fight, and US/allied SSNs are the sharpest counter to a Chinese amphibious operation. Alliances, basing, and C2 quality are force multipliers a supplier map understates.

## The chokepoints that bite — and when

The lens's one-table summary — the same chokepoints as the ledger, sorted by *when they matter in a war*:

| Chokepoint / capability | Bites in… | Decisive for a Taiwan fight? | Advantaged |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Missile magazine depth** | Hours–days | Decisive | 🟥 China |
| **Counterspace / EW (kill-chain blinding)** | Hours–days | Decisive | 🟥 China (contested) |
| **US long-range munitions (LRASM/JASSM)** | Days (run out) | Decisive weakness | 🟦 US (shallow) |
| **Munitions-production tempo** | Weeks | Decisive | 🟥 China |
| **Drone mass + component base** | Days–weeks | Decisive | 🟥 China |
| **Shipbuilding & battle-damage repair** | Weeks–months | Decisive | 🟥 China |
| **Energetics & rare-earth magnets (reload)** | Weeks–months | Decisive | 🟥 China |
| **Undersea / ASW (SSNs, quieting)** | Weeks–months | Decisive | 🟦 US/allied |
| **Alliance, basing, ISR/C2 quality** | All phases | Decisive | 🟦 US/allied |
| **China's energy / food / chip imports (blockade)** | Months | Decisive (China's vuln.) | 🟦 US/allied |
| **EUV / EDA / frontier AI compute** | Years | Marginal in a short war | 🟦 US/allied |

The pattern reads top-to-bottom: **the fast-biting, war-decisive rows favor China; the slow-biting economic chokepoints — the ones the US holds — mostly don't bite in time.** The exceptions that keep it two-sided are undersea warfare, alliances, and the blockade's double edge on China's own imports.

---

## The honest bottom line

China's defense-industrial base is optimized for the fight it is most likely to choose: a short, sharp, high-intensity regional war decided by magazine depth, production tempo, and mass. On that axis, the "wartime footing vs. peacetime footing" gap the atlas keeps flagging is not rhetoric — it is the operational crux, and it favors China. **But "optimized for the opening fight" is not "assured of winning the war."** The US and allied edges — undersea dominance, alliances and basing, C2 and ISR quality, and the blockade's ability to turn China's own import dependencies against it over months — are real and, in a protracted conflict, potentially decisive. The uncomfortable synthesis for a US planner is that *the longer the war, the more American advantages matter — and the whole point of China's industrial design is to make sure the war is short.*
