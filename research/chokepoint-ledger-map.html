Chokepoint Ledger

The atlas's bottom line. Every domain nets out to two columns: what China must still import, and what the world must import from China. The asymmetry is the point — the US holds fewer but higher-quality chokepoints (a short list of things China genuinely can't make: EUV lithography above all), while China holds more but softer ones (a long list others could eventually replace, but not soon or cheaply — rare-earth magnets above all) and trades them tactically. Both clocks are running. The chart plots every chokepoint by how binding it is and how long it takes to escape; the decisive ones sit top-right.

The crossover clocks

The ledger is useful mainly for its timing. The moments that would shift the balance:

~2026–27HBM at scale. If CXMT hits volume HBM3, the US loses one of its three compute locks and China's AI-accelerator ceiling rises. The nearest crossover.
2026–27Mature-node price war. If Big Fund III capacity gluts legacy chips, China's leverage grows by hollowing foreign fabs — leverage moving the wrong way for the West.
rollingDrone-component reshoring. Ukraine and the US localize motors and controllers under fire; cameras and magnets are the sticky last mile. A race between the war's learning curve and China's export leverage.
~2028–30Allied magnet diversification. The G7's sub-60%-single-supplier-by-2030 target; if allied magnet-making passes ~40% of supply, China's crown jewel starts to erode. Furthest out, highest stakes.
this decade?EUV. Any credible sign SSMB or LDP nears a commercial scanner would be the most destabilizing event in the ledger — and the master lock the US holds. Treat every such report with heavy skepticism.
