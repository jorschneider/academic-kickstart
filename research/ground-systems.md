# China's Ground Systems & the Munitions Machine

*The domain where China's scale advantage is most operationally decisive — not because the tanks are better, but because the factories are. NORINCO, the energetics base, and the munitions arithmetic that worries US planners most.*

## The scale story is the story

Ground systems is rated **Competitive** in the atlas on hardware terms — China's tanks and artillery are good, not clearly better than the best Western kit. But that rating undersells the thing that actually matters here: **production capacity for a protracted, high-intensity regional war**, where China is not merely competitive but arguably ahead. This is the sharpest expression of the atlas's "two footings" throughline — a Chinese base on a wartime footing against an American one on a peacetime footing.

The [DoD estimate that Chinese state enterprises acquire munitions and materiel roughly **five to six times faster** than the US](https://asiatimes.com/2026/07/us-wartime-buildup-races-against-chinas-industrial-clock/) lands hardest in this domain. China's ordnance conglomerates run automated "smart factories" with a reported **150–250% wartime surge capacity**, and military-civil fusion lets Beijing convert commercial manufacturing and logistics into munitions production quickly. The [Heritage Foundation's January 2026 "TIDALWAVE" assessment](https://www.heritage.org/tidalwave/chapters/chapter-4-assessing-the-prc-military-munitions-system) frames the contrast bluntly: the US Indo-Pacific munitions system faces a "Triple Bind" — a roughly two-year production lag, rocket-motor bottlenecks, and reliance on imported Polish TNT — that could produce supply failure within **25 to 120 days** of a major conflict, while the Chinese system is a vertically integrated enterprise built precisely for that fight.

In a Taiwan scenario, magazine depth and reload rate may matter more than platform sophistication. That is exactly where China's ground-industrial base is strongest — and it is why the [Taiwan-contingency lens](./taiwan-contingency-lens.md) treats munitions tempo, not platform quality, as the variable that decides a long war.

## The arithmetic, as far as it can be counted

Abstract claims about "industrial capacity" become concrete fast when you look at the numbers that exist.

**On the American side, the numbers are audited and they are not reassuring.** CSIS's 2023 Taiwan wargame ran 24 iterations; in *every single one*, the US expended its entire inventory of Long-Range Anti-Ship Missiles [within the first week](https://features.csis.org/preparing-the-US-industrial-base-to-deter-conflict-with-China/), with JASSM stocks gone on average by day nine. Replacement was not a meaningful option inside the scenario: some of these precision weapons take roughly **two years from contract signature to first delivery**. Long-range munitions were essential in the opening phase of every simulation precisely because Chinese anti-access systems keep US aircraft outside the First Island Chain — so the weapons that run out first are the ones the campaign depends on most.

The conventional-ammunition picture is the same story at lower altitude. The US Army set a target of **100,000 155mm rounds per month by October 2025**; as of March 2026 the [DoD Inspector General found actual output at about **36,000 per month**](https://www.militarytimes.com/news/your-military/2026/07/14/manufacturing-woes-hamper-us-155-mm-ammo-production/), with the goal slipped to later in 2026 and projectile metal parts named as the limiting factor. And the constraint underneath the constraint is chemical: hitting 100,000 complete rounds a month requires roughly **66,000 tons of explosives**, a figure that has historically forced heavy import reliance — the Polish-TNT dependency Heritage flags.

**On the Chinese side, there is no comparable number — and that absence is the finding.** No public, quantified estimate of Chinese 155mm or rocket-artillery output exists. Nobody outside the system audits Chinese ordnance factories; there is no PRC equivalent of a DoD Inspector General report on shell production. What we have instead are inferences from industrial base, plant footprints, and procurement growth.

This produces an epistemically lopsided comparison that analysts should name rather than smooth over: **the US side is measured and the Chinese side is inferred.** The widely-cited 5–6× ratio and the 150–250% surge figure are US-government and think-tank estimates — directional judgments, not audited counts. They are almost certainly right about the *direction*; they carry real error bars on the *magnitude*. That is why the [methodology page](./methodology-and-confidence.md) grades this domain B rather than A, and why "energetics and magazine depth becoming visible" ranks in the top five of the atlas's [tripwires](./indicators-and-warnings.md) — it is the single most decision-relevant number that open source currently cannot see.

## The prime — NORINCO, now sole

**NORINCO (China North Industries Group)** became, with the July 2025 dissolution of China South Industries and the folding of its defense assets inward, the **sole central ordnance prime** — the first time since 1999 that Chinese ground-systems production has sat under a single conglomerate. It spans tanks, artillery, rocket artillery, precision munitions, ATGMs, small arms, and most of the PLA's ammunition base, alongside a major arms-export business and, awkwardly for sanctions enforcers, one of China's largest civilian commodity-trading operations. It is 1260H-listed and OFAC-sanctioned.

The consolidation matters analytically. A single prime means a single set of production priorities, a single surge plan, and — for the analyst — a single corporate perimeter to watch. It also means the export business and the domestic war reserve are managed by the same organization, which is why NORINCO's overseas sales are a legitimate window into its domestic industrial state.

### The institutes underneath

The conglomerate's name is on the products; the design authority sits in the numbered institutes, and this is where the unheralded work happens:

- **203 Institute (Xi'an Modern Control Technology)** — NORINCO's rocket-artillery and guided-projectile design brain. The family of 300–370mm rounds the PHL-191 fires, and the guidance work that converts saturation fires into precision fires, originates here.
- **204 Institute (Xi'an Modern Chemistry)** — China's principal research base for **energetic materials**: the explosives and the gun and rocket propellants that every shell, missile and warhead runs on. If you want to know whether the surge claim is real, this is the institute at the bottom of the question.
- **North Electro-Optics (Xi'an)** — the stabilized sights, thermal imagers and laser rangefinders that let armor shoot on the move and gunners see at night. High-end optics and fire control are the domain's named dependency, and this is where China is closing it.
- **North Navigation (listed)** — the laser and satnav guidance kits and gyros that turn the PHL-191's rockets into precision rounds. Massed precision fires are a components story before they are a launcher story.
- **Inner Mongolia First Machinery (Baotou)** — the listed tank plant: Type 99A, Type 100, and the VT-4 export line.

## The hardware, refreshed at the 2025 parade

- **Armor.** The mainstay **Type 99A** MBT, joined by the new **Type 100** — a lighter, hybrid-drivetrain vehicle debuted 3 September 2025 with a lower infrared and acoustic signature and a range extender. The design direction is telling: not a heavier tank, but a networked, survivable, lower-signature one. China is reading the same Ukraine lessons everyone else is.
- **Tube artillery.** The truck-mounted **PCL-181** 155mm/L52 howitzer (in service since 2019) — mobile, rapidly relocatable fires, built for shoot-and-scoot survival against counter-battery radar.
- **Rocket artillery — the standout.** The modular **PHL-191 / PCL-191** MLRS, a HIMARS-analogue on an 8×8 chassis firing guided rockets and tactical ballistic missiles out past **350 km** depending on payload; two variants appeared at the 2025 parade. Massed, long-range, precision rocket fires are a signature PLA ground capability and the one most directly relevant to a strait scenario, where the "ground" force shoots at ships and airfields.
- **Precision & guidance.** North Navigation's kits, above — the reason the rockets land where they are aimed.

### What Ukraine changed

The war reset everyone's assumptions about consumption rates, and the PLA has been an attentive student. Three lessons show up in Chinese procurement and design: artillery ammunition is consumed at rates that peacetime stockpiles never anticipated; cheap drones and counter-battery radar make static, high-signature platforms rapidly lethal to their own crews (hence the Type 100's signature reduction and the truck-mounted howitzers); and the industrial base, not the order of battle, sets the ceiling on how long a force can fight. The last of those is the lesson China was already best positioned to act on.

## The foundation — energetics and ammunition

The least glamorous and most decisive layer is chemical. You cannot surge shells you cannot fill.

**Hexi Chemical** and the wider energetics base design solid-rocket motors on **ammonium-perchlorate/HTPB** propellant (the FG series), and China's broad **ammonium-perchlorate** production underpins both its munitions and its space-launch base — a genuine dual-use synergy, since every commercial solid-motor launch validates the same chemistry that fills military rocket motors. Chinese advances in energetic materials have been repeatedly flagged as a real and underappreciated edge, and this chemical-industrial depth is what makes the surge claim credible in principle even though it cannot be verified in detail.

Note the contrast with the American constraint above: the US bottleneck for 155mm is *explosives supply and metal parts*, and it has leaned on imported TNT. China's ordnance complex sits inside the world's largest chemical industry. That structural difference is more durable than any particular production-line investment on either side.

**A proliferation footnote.** The same base is an export concern: [suspected shipments of ammonium-perchlorate precursors from China to Iran](https://gcaptain.com/suspected-chemical-shipments-from-china-to-iran-raise-concerns-over-potential-missile-expansion/) in 2025 raised alarms about feeding Iranian missile production. Energetics is a proliferation vector, not just a domestic strength — and, usefully for analysts, export flows are one of the few places where the otherwise-invisible energetics base leaves a customs trail.

## The export business as a window

NORINCO's arms-export line — the **VT-4** tank, the **SH-15** howitzer, guided rockets, and a broad small-arms and ammunition trade — serves Pakistan, Thailand, Nigeria, Bangladesh and a long list of others. Two analytic uses follow. First, exported systems are the only Chinese ground kit that generates third-party performance data, since Chinese equipment is otherwise untested in high-intensity combat; an export customer fighting a real war would tell us more than a decade of parades. That is precisely why "combat performance data" is a top-ranked [tripwire](./indicators-and-warnings.md). Second, sustained export volume alongside domestic buildup is itself evidence about spare capacity — a base running flat-out for the PLA has less to sell.

## Where it's "competitive," not "dominant"

- **Top-tier optics, fire control and networked C2.** Western armies retain edges in the highest-end sensors, battle-management integration, and combined-arms sophistication. North Electro-Optics is closing the sensor half; the integration half is harder to assess from outside and harder to build.
- **Combat-proven-ness.** Chinese ground systems are largely untested in high-intensity combat, unlike Western, Russian and Ukrainian kit. "Fielded" is not "proven under fire," and the atlas does not treat it as such.
- **Joint integration.** The ground force's relevance in a strait fight depends on how well its long-range fires plug into a joint targeting architecture run largely by other services — a coordination problem that parades do not test.

But on the metric that decides a long regional war — how fast you can build and reload — the ground domain is where China's industrial scale converts most directly into military advantage.

---

## Assessment

China's ground systems are competitive on hardware and sit atop a munitions-production machine that US planners increasingly regard as the more decisive asymmetry. The tanks and howitzers matter; the smart factories, the energetics base and the production tempo matter more.

The honest version of the finding has two halves. The first: on every measurable indicator, the American munitions base is running behind its own stated targets while facing a competitor with a structurally deeper chemical and manufacturing foundation. The second: **we cannot actually count the Chinese side**, and the ratios everyone quotes are inferences. Both halves are true at once, and an analyst who drops either one is telling half a story — the first half alone is alarmism, the second alone is complacency.

Rated Competitive on platforms. But this is the one domain where "wartime footing versus peacetime footing" is not a metaphor, and the one where the thing we most need to know is the thing we can least see.
